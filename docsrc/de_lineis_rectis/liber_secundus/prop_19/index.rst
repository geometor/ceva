**Proposition XIX · Problem III** (pp. 54–57, `Tab. 8, Figs. 63–66`)

*Let two triangles be so fitted together that reciprocally the vertex of one ends on the base of the other, and thus their sides intersect: in such a figure I distinguish six ratios of lines, from which, any four being given, let it be proposed to find the remaining two.*

There are fifteen cases of this problem; for so many are the binary combinations of six.

**I.** Let the four ratios :g:`GE` to :g:`EF`, :g:`AB` to :g:`BC`, :g:`AH` to :g:`HE`, and :g:`BD` to :g:`DF` be known; and let the two remaining :g:`GH` to :g:`HB` and :g:`FD` to :g:`DB` be sought. 
Let the two straight lines :g:`HD`, :g:`BE` be joined, cutting one another at :g:`I`. Since in the Third Element, whose center is :g:`I` and weights :g:`A`; :g:`H`–:g:`A`; :g:`D`–:g:`C`; :g:`C`, the three ratios :g:`AH` to :g:`HE`, :g:`ED` to :g:`DC`, and :g:`AB` to :g:`BC` are given, 
I shall also give the remaining two ratios (from Problem IX of Book I) :g:`HI` to :g:`ID` and :g:`BI` to :g:`IE`; wherefore since in a figure of the same Element, whose center is :g:`I` and weights :g:`G`; :g:`H`–:g:`G`; :g:`D`–:g:`F`; :g:`F`, 
the three ratios :g:`GE` to :g:`EF`, :g:`HI` to :g:`ID`, and :g:`EI` to :g:`IB` are given, I shall show (from the same problem) the remaining two ratios :g:`GH` to :g:`HB` and :g:`FD` to :g:`DB`.

**II.** Let the two ratios :g:`AH` to :g:`HE` and :g:`ED` to :g:`DC` be sought, the rest being known; this case will be similar to the former.

**III.** Let the ratios we must discover be the two :g:`AB` to :g:`BC` and :g:`GE` to :g:`EF`, having the remaining four 
(take care however in this case that :g:`AC`, :g:`GF` are not parallel). Let :g:`AC`, :g:`GF` be produced so as to meet at :g:`I`. Since then :g:`AB` to :g:`BC` is compounded from the ratios :g:`AB` to :g:`BI` and :g:`BI` to :g:`BC`; 
likewise :g:`GE` to :g:`EF` is compounded from the ratios :g:`GE` to :g:`EI` to :g:`EF`; and in the First Element, whose weights are :g:`A`, :g:`I`, :g:`G` and center is :g:`H`, the two ratios :g:`GH` to :g:`HB` and :g:`EH` to :g:`HA` are given; 
and likewise in another figure of the same Element, whose weights are :g:`B`, :g:`I`, :g:`E` and their center is :g:`D`, the two ratios :g:`ED` to :g:`DC` and :g:`FD` to :g:`DB` are given; we shall disclose in the first figure the remaining two :g:`AB` to :g:`BI` and :g:`GE` to :g:`EI`; 
and in the second figure the remaining two :g:`BI` to :g:`BC` and :g:`EI` to :g:`EF`; therefore the ratios :g:`AB` to :g:`BI` to :g:`BC`, that is :g:`AB` to :g:`BC`, are given; and likewise the ratios :g:`GE` to :g:`EI` to :g:`EF`, that is :g:`GE` to :g:`EF`, Q.E.F.

**IV.** Suppose we must now show the two ratios :g:`ED` to :g:`DC` and :g:`FD` to :g:`DB`, the remaining four being supposed. If the two :g:`AC`, :g:`GF` are parallel, what is proposed is already clear; if they are not, let us produce them as before so that they meet at :g:`I`; 
and since in the First Element, whose weights are :g:`A`, :g:`I`, :g:`G` and center is :g:`H`, the two ratios :g:`AH` to :g:`HE` and :g:`GH` to :g:`HB` are given, we shall show (from *Problem VI, Part I*) the remaining two :g:`AB` to :g:`BI` and :g:`GE` to :g:`EI`; 
but the ratios :g:`AB` to :g:`BC` and :g:`GE` to :g:`EF` are given; therefore, having taken away the known ratios :g:`AB` to :g:`BI` from :g:`AB` to :g:`BC`, and :g:`GE` to :g:`EI` from the aforesaid :g:`GE` to :g:`EF`, the remaining ratios :g:`BI` to :g:`BC` and :g:`IE` to :g:`EF` will be known: 
wherefore, these two ratios being given in the First Element, whose weights are :g:`B`, :g:`I`, :g:`E` and center is :g:`D`, the remaining two :g:`BD` to :g:`DF` and :g:`DE` to :g:`DC` will become clear, Q.E.F.

**V.** But if the ratios to be investigated are :g:`AH` to :g:`HE` and :g:`BH` to :g:`HG`, 
we shall solve the problem by nearly the same reasoning; for given the two :g:`BD` to :g:`DF` and :g:`ED` to :g:`DC` in the First Element whose weights are :g:`B`, :g:`I`, :g:`E` and center is :g:`D`, the remaining two :g:`BC` to :g:`BI` and :g:`EF` to :g:`EI` become known; 
and since :g:`CB` to :g:`BA` and :g:`FE` to :g:`EG` are also supposed given, the remaining two :g:`IB` to :g:`BA` and :g:`IE` to :g:`EG` will also be given. 
Thus since in the First Element whose weights are :g:`A`, :g:`I`, :g:`C` [:g:`G`] and center is :g:`H`, the two ratios :g:`IB` to :g:`BA` and :g:`IE` to :g:`EG` are known, we shall likewise show the remaining two :g:`AH` to :g:`HE` and :g:`GH` to :g:`HB`.

**VI.** Let the two ratios :g:`AH` to :g:`HE` and :g:`GE` to :g:`EF` be sought, the rest being given. If :g:`AC`, :g:`GF` are parallel, it is evident that :g:`AH` to :g:`HE` is in the same ratio as :g:`BH` to :g:`HG`, and therefore given. 
Next, since :g:`GE` to :g:`EF` is compounded from the ratios :g:`GE` to :g:`AB`, :g:`AB` to :g:`BC`, and :g:`BC` to :g:`EF`; while as :g:`GE` is to :g:`AB`, so is the given :g:`GH` to :g:`HB`; 
:g:`AB` to :g:`BC` is given, and as :g:`BC` is to :g:`EF`, so is the given :g:`CD` to :g:`DE`; therefore :g:`EF` to :g:`EG`, which is compounded from given ratios, will also be given.

But if :g:`AC`, :g:`GF` are not parallel, let them meet produced at :g:`I`; and since in the First Element, whose weights are :g:`B`, :g:`I`, :g:`E` and center is :g:`D`, the two ratios :g:`BD` to :g:`DF` and :g:`ED` to :g:`DC` are given, 
we shall also render the remaining :g:`CB` to :g:`BI` and :g:`EI` to :g:`FE` known; and therefore since the known ratio :g:`CB` to :g:`BA` is compounded from the ratios :g:`CB` to :g:`BI`, which is known, and :g:`IB` to :g:`BA`, this too will be given; 
and therefore since in the First Element, whose weights are :g:`A`, :g:`I`, :g:`G` and center is :g:`H`, the two ratios :g:`IB` to :g:`BA` and :g:`BH` to :g:`HG` are given, we shall also display the remaining :g:`AH` to :g:`HE` and :g:`GE` to :g:`EI` as known: 
wherefore since :g:`GE` to :g:`EF` is compounded from the given ratios :g:`GE` to :g:`EI` to :g:`EF`, this too will be clear.

**VII.** We shall show the two ratios :g:`GH` to :g:`HB` and :g:`AB` to :g:`BC` in the same way; for the case is similar to the preceding.

**VIII.** Let the ratios :g:`GE` to :g:`EF` and :g:`GH` to :g:`HB` be sought, the remaining four being given. From the two given ratios :g:`ED` to :g:`DC` and :g:`FD` to :g:`DB`, :g:`BI` to :g:`BC` and :g:`EI` to :g:`EF` come to light; 
but the ratio compounded from :g:`AB` to :g:`BI` to :g:`BC` is :g:`AB` to :g:`BC`, which is given; therefore the remaining ratio :g:`AB` to :g:`BI` will be given, and thus in the First Element, whose weights are :g:`A`, :g:`I`, :g:`G` and center is :g:`H`, 
since the two ratios :g:`AH` to :g:`HE` and :g:`AB` to :g:`BI` are given, we shall also give the remaining two :g:`GH` to :g:`HB` and :g:`GE` to :g:`EI`; now the ratio :g:`GE` to :g:`EF` is compounded from the ratios :g:`GE` to :g:`EI` and :g:`EI` to :g:`IF` [:g:`EF`], 
which are given, so that too will become clear. And if :g:`AC`, :g:`GF` are parallel, we shall use the same reasoning as we used in the sixth case.

**IX.** But if the ratios to be investigated are :g:`AH` to :g:`HE` and :g:`AB` to :g:`BC`, this case will be similar to the preceding.

**X.** Let the two ratios to be found be :g:`GE` to :g:`EF` and :g:`ED` to :g:`DC`, the rest being previously known. If :g:`AC`, :g:`GF` are parallel, we shall show what is proposed as in the sixth case; 
but if :g:`GE`, :g:`EF` [:g:`AC`, :g:`GF`] are not parallel, produced they will meet at :g:`I`; wherefore since the two ratios :g:`GH` to :g:`HB` and :g:`AH` to :g:`HE` are given, we shall also know the remaining two, namely :g:`AB` to :g:`BI` and :g:`GE` to :g:`EI`; 
now the given ratio :g:`AB` to :g:`BC` is compounded from the given ratio :g:`AB` to :g:`BI` and :g:`BI` to :g:`BC`; therefore this remaining ratio will also come to light; wherefore, given the two ratios :g:`BI` to :g:`BC` and :g:`BD` to :g:`DF` in the figure of the First Element, 
whose weights are :g:`B`, :g:`I`, :g:`E` and center is :g:`D`, we shall also show the remaining two :g:`ED` to :g:`DC` and :g:`EI` to :g:`EF`; wherefore the two ratios :g:`GE` to :g:`EI` and :g:`EI` to :g:`EF` are given; 
but from these is compounded the ratio :g:`GE` to :g:`EF`; therefore it too will not lie hidden.

**XI.** But if the ratios we must show are :g:`AB` to :g:`BC` and :g:`BD` to :g:`DF`, we shall demonstrate this part of the problem in the manner stated above.

**XII.** Let it now be required to find the two ratios :g:`AB` to :g:`BC` and :g:`CD` to :g:`DE`; and if :g:`AC`, :g:`GF` are parallel we shall use the same reasoning as we used in the sixth case; if not, let :g:`CA`, :g:`FG` produced meet at :g:`I`. 
Given the two ratios :g:`GH` to :g:`HB` and :g:`AH` to :g:`HE`, we shall give the remaining two :g:`AB` to :g:`BI` and :g:`GE` to :g:`EI`; and given the two :g:`GE` to :g:`EF` and :g:`GE` to :g:`EI`, we shall make known the remaining :g:`EI` to :g:`EF`; 
wherefore, having the two :g:`EI` to :g:`EF` and :g:`BD` to :g:`DF`, we shall likewise know

the two :g:`CD` to :g:`DE` and :g:`BI` to :g:`BC`; and having the two :g:`AB` to :g:`BI` to :g:`BC`, from which is made the ratio :g:`AB` to :g:`BC`, this will likewise be given.

**XIII.** But if the two ratios to be made known are :g:`GE` to :g:`EF` and :g:`FD` to :g:`DB`, this case will be similar to the preceding.

**XIV.** If we wish to find the two ratios :g:`AH` to :g:`HE` and :g:`BD` to :g:`DF` when the rest are previously known, 
let :g:`GC` be joined cutting the two :g:`HE`, :g:`BD` at :g:`I`, :g:`K`. Thus since in the figure of the First Element whose weights are :g:`A`, :g:`C`, :g:`G` and center is :g:`H`, the two ratios :g:`AB` to :g:`BC` and :g:`G` [:g:`GH`] to :g:`HB` are clear, 
the remaining two :g:`AH` to :g:`AI` and :g:`IC` to :g:`CG` will be known; and likewise since in another figure of the First Element whose weights are :g:`C`, :g:`G`, :g:`F` and their center is :g:`D`, the two ratios :g:`ED` to :g:`DC` and :g:`GE` to :g:`EF` are given, 
the remaining two :g:`DF` to :g:`DK` and :g:`GC` to :g:`CK` will also become conspicuous; wherefore :g:`IC` to :g:`KC` (compounded namely from the two given :g:`IC` to :g:`CG` to :g:`CK`) will be known; and therefore in the Third Element, 
whose weights are :g:`A`; :g:`B`–:g:`A`; :g:`D`–:g:`E`; :g:`E` and center is :g:`K`, given the three ratios :g:`AB` to :g:`BC`, :g:`IC` to :g:`KC`, and :g:`ED` to :g:`DC`, the remaining two :g:`AI` to :g:`AE` and :g:`DK` to :g:`DB` will also be given, provided however that :g:`AE` and :g:`BD` are not parallel; 
therefore the ratios :g:`AH` to :g:`AI` to :g:`AE` and :g:`DF` to :g:`DK` to :g:`DB` are given, that is, the two ratios :g:`AH` to :g:`AE` and :g:`DF` to :g:`DB`: but if :g:`BD` and :g:`AE` are parallel, while :g:`AC` and :g:`GF` are by no means so, the problem will be impossible; 
to make this clear, let us draw from point :g:`E` the line :g:`EL` cutting :g:`DF` at :g:`L`, and produced through :g:`E` let it be cut by line :g:`EK` so that :g:`LE` to :g:`EK` is as :g:`FE` to :g:`EG`, and let :g:`BK` be joined cutting :g:`AE` at :g:`I`. 
Therefore because of the parallels :g:`BF`, :g:`AE`, as :g:`BH` is to :g:`HG`, so :g:`BI` will be to :g:`IK`; and as :g:`FE` is to :g:`EG`, so :g:`LE` to :g:`EK`; while the ratios :g:`AB` to :g:`BC` and :g:`CD` to :g:`DE` are the supposed ones themselves; 
therefore if the case were possible, the ratio :g:`AH` to :g:`HE` would have to be the same as :g:`AI` to :g:`IE`; and likewise as :g:`BD` is to :g:`DF`, so would :g:`BD` have to be to :g:`DL`, which since it is not, nothing certain can be determined.

**XV. & lastly.** If the ratios to be shown are :g:`BH` to :g:`HG` and :g:`CD` to :g:`DE`, care must be taken as above that :g:`BG`, :g:`CE` are not parallel when :g:`AC`, :g:`GF` are not parallel; this case is similar to the above, and so the whole proposition holds.
