**Proposition XVI · Theorem XIV** (pp. 51–52, `Tab. 7, Fig. 57`)

*If from the angles of a triangle three straight lines bisecting the angles be drawn to the opposite sides, they will divide one another at the same point; and the figure consisting of these lines will be that of the Second Element.*

Let :g:`AIE` be a triangle, and from the bisected angles :g:`I`, :g:`A`, :g:`E` let the lines :g:`IC`, :g:`AG`, :g:`EN` be drawn meeting the opposite sides at :g:`C`, :g:`G`, :g:`N`: I say that these lines are cut at the same point :g:`B`, 
that is, :g:`EC` to :g:`CA` is compounded from the ratios :g:`IN` to :g:`NA` and :g:`EG` to :g:`GI`; and therefore the figure composed of these lines belongs to the Second Element. 
For the ratio :g:`EC` to :g:`CA`, that is :g:`EI` to :g:`IA` (since angle :g:`I` is bisected), is compounded from the ratios :g:`IE` to :g:`EA` to :g:`AI`;

and as :g:`IF` [:g:`IE`] is to :g:`EA`, so :g:`IN` is to :g:`NA`; and as :g:`EA` is to :g:`AI`, so :g:`EG` is to :g:`GI`; therefore :g:`EC` to :g:`CA` is compounded from the ratios :g:`IN` to :g:`NA` and :g:`EG` to :g:`GI`, Q.E.D.

**Lemma VI** (p. 52, `Tab. 7, Figs. 58, 60, 61`)

*Given two circles, to draw a line touching both.*

Let there be two circles whose centers are :g:`K`, :g:`I`; it is required to draw a line touching both. Suppose the problem already done, and let the tangent line be :g:`AF`; let us join :g:`AK`, :g:`KI`, :g:`IF`: 
and since in the first case, when the two circles are equal, :g:`KA` and :g:`IF` are also equal and parallel to one another, since each of them is perpendicular to the same :g:`AF`, the space :g:`KAFI` will be a rectangular parallelogram, 
and therefore the tangent :g:`AF` will be parallel to line :g:`KI`; since then the two points :g:`K`, :g:`I` are given, line :g:`KI` will also be given in position, and likewise :g:`KA` perpendicular to it: 
wherefore point :g:`A` is given, from which if a line be drawn parallel to :g:`KI` it will fall upon line :g:`AF`, and the tangent :g:`AF` will be given, Q.E.D.

Next let the circles be unequal, as in the two remaining cases, 
and let :g:`KI` produced in the third figure meet at :g:`L` with the tangent :g:`AF` likewise produced toward the side of the smaller circle, just as in the second figure the tangent :g:`AF` meets the same :g:`KI` at the same point :g:`L`. 
Since each of :g:`KA` and :g:`IF` is perpendicular to :g:`AF`, they will be parallel to one another, and therefore as :g:`KA` is to :g:`IF`, so :g:`KL` is to :g:`LI`; and by composition in the second figure, and by division in the third, 
:g:`KI` will be to :g:`IL` as the sum of the two :g:`KA`, :g:`FI` in the second figure, and as their difference in the third, to the same :g:`HI` [:g:`FI`]. 
Therefore, since both the ratio of the sum and that of the difference of the two aforesaid radii to the smaller radius are given, and the antecedent :g:`KI` is also given in length and position, the consequent :g:`IL` and point :g:`L` will also be given; 
wherefore since from the same point :g:`L` we can draw only one line touching :g:`IF` and only one other touching circle :g:`KA`, these must fall upon the tangents :g:`FL`, :g:`LA`, or :g:`AFL`, which from the beginning we supposed tangent to the two circles. 
The synthesis of the problem is manifest.
