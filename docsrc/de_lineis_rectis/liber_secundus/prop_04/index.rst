Proposition IV: Proportionally Divided Quadrilateral
====================================================

.. folio:: 0057
   :page: p. 40

.. picture:: 0111.g40.png
   :alt: 1678 copperplate Tabula V Fig. 40: Quadrilateral with proportionally divided opposite pairs

**Proposition IV · Theorem IV** (p. 40, `Tab. 5, Fig. 40`)

*If in a quadrilateral two lines cutting one another be drawn, each of which divides two opposite sides in the same ratio, the resulting figure will be the fifth element.*

Let there be quadrilateral :g:`ACEG`, and let :g:`CB` be to :g:`BA` as :g:`EF` to :g:`FG`, 
and likewise :g:`ED` to :g:`DC` as :g:`GH` to :g:`HA`, and let lines :g:`BF`, :g:`HD` be joined, cutting one another at :g:`I`. I say that this figure is that which we considered in the fifth element. 
For if this be not true, the ratio :g:`ED` to :g:`DC` will not be that which is compounded of the ratios :g:`AB` to :g:`BC`, :g:`GH` to :g:`HA`, and :g:`EF` to :g:`FG`. Let there be therefore another ratio :g:`EK` to :g:`KC`. 
And so, since :g:`EK` to :g:`KC` is compounded of the aforesaid ratios, or rather of the same :g:`AB` to :g:`BC`, :g:`EF` to :g:`FG`, and :g:`GH` to :g:`HA` taken in perturbed order; nay even of the ratios :g:`AB` to :g:`BC`, 
:g:`CB` to :g:`BA`, and :g:`GH` to :g:`HA`; or finally of the ratios :g:`AB` to :g:`BA`, and :g:`GH` to :g:`HA`; or of :g:`GH` to :g:`HG` to :g:`HA`; :g:`EK` will be to :g:`KC` as :g:`GH` to :g:`HA`: 
but :g:`ED` to :g:`DC` is also in the same ratio; therefore as :g:`EK` to :g:`KC`, so :g:`ED` to :g:`DC`, and by composition, :g:`EC` to :g:`CK` will be as the same :g:`EC` to :g:`CD`; 
therefore :g:`CK` is equal to :g:`CD`, the whole to a part, which is absurd, which &c.
