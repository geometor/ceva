**Book II · Lemma I** (`Tab. 4, Figs. 32–34`)

*If any rectilinear figure be circumscribed about a circle or an ellipse so that each of its sides touches the aforesaid circle or ellipse, then the sides themselves are so divided by the points of contact*
 *that the ratio of the parts of one side is compounded of the ratios of the similarly taken parts of the remaining sides in succession.*

Let triangle :g:`ECA`, or quadrilateral :g:`ACEG`, or any other polygon be circumscribed about the circle or ellipse :g:`BDF`, and let the points of contact be
 :g:`B`, :g:`D`, :g:`F`, :g:`✠`. We must show that :g:`CD` to :g:`DE` is compounded in the triangle of the ratios :g:`AF` to :g:`FE` and :g:`CB` to :g:`BA`; and in the quadrilateral of the three ratios :g:`GF` to :g:`FE`, :g:`A✠` to :g:`✠G`, and :g:`CB` to :g:`BA`.


In a circle, tangent :g:`DC` equals :g:`CB`, :g:`BA` equals :g:`AF`, and :g:`FE` equals :g:`ED` (when the circumscribed figure is a triangle); hence, since :g:`CD` to :g:`DE` is compounded of the ratios :g:`DC` to :g:`CB`, :g:`CB` to :g:`BA`, :g:`BA` to :g:`AF`, :g:`AF` to :g:`FE`, and :g:`FE` to :g:`ED`,
if the ratios of equality :g:`DC` to :g:`CB`, :g:`BA` to :g:`AF`, and :g:`FE` to :g:`ED` be removed, there will remain the ratio compounded only of the two ratios :g:`CB` to :g:`BA` and :g:`AF` to :g:`FE`,
or of the same taken perturbedly, namely :g:`AF` to :g:`FE` and :g:`CB` to :g:`BA`. By the same reasoning we shall prove in a quadrilateral circumscribed about a circle that the ratio :g:`CD` to :g:`DE` is compounded of the ratios :g:`GF` to :g:`FE`, :g:`A✠` to :g:`✠G`, and :g:`CB` to :g:`BA`:
therefore what we proposed is manifest whenever a triangle, or a quadrilateral, or any other polygon is circumscribed about a circle.

But if it be about an ellipse, we can find some cylinder of which this proposed ellipse is a section (and this we shall prove below);
let the base of this cylinder be circle

   :page: p. 36

:g:`GLI` (`Tab. 4, Fig. 35`), and let the said ellipse be the section of the cylinder denoted by the letters :g:`FDB`, about which let triangle :g:`EAC` be circumscribed; from the points of contact :g:`FDB` of the circumscribed figure,
let lines :g:`FG`, :g:`DL`, :g:`BI` fall to points :g:`G`, :g:`I`, :g:`L` of the circumference; next let us draw planes through :g:`EA` and :g:`FG`, :g:`AC` and :g:`BI`, and :g:`EC` and :g:`DL`: these will touch the surface of the cylinder along the lines :g:`FG`, :g:`DL`, :g:`BI`.
Finally, extending the plane of the base, let all the common intersections of these planes be :g:`ME`, :g:`AH`, :g:`CK`, as well as :g:`MGH`, :g:`HIK`, :g:`KLM`. And since the former planes were drawn through the lines :g:`FG`, :g:`BI`, :g:`DL`, which are parallel to one another,
the common intersections of those planes, such as :g:`AH`, :g:`CK`, :g:`EM`, will also be parallel to one another and to the aforesaid sides :g:`FG`, :g:`BI`, :g:`DL`; therefore :g:`AF` will be to :g:`FE` as :g:`HG` to :g:`GM`, :g:`CB` to :g:`BA` as :g:`KI` to :g:`IH`,
and :g:`CD` to :g:`DE` as :g:`KL` to :g:`LM`; in circle :g:`GLI` the ratio :g:`KL` to :g:`LM` is compounded of the ratios :g:`HG` to :g:`GM` and :g:`KI` to :g:`IH`; therefore :g:`CD` to :g:`DE` will also be compounded of :g:`AF` to :g:`FE` and :g:`CB` to :g:`BA`.
Similarly we shall show in quadrilateral :g:`CBA✠GFED` that the ratio :g:`CD` to :g:`DE` is compounded of the ratios :g:`EF` to :g:`FG`, :g:`A✠` to :g:`✠G`, and :g:`CB` to :g:`BA`. We shall use the same process if any other figure be circumscribed, Q.E.D.

**Lemma II** (`Tab. 4, Fig. 36`)


*It remains for us to show what we assumed in the preceding lemma: namely, given any ellipse, say* :g:`FDB✠`, *to find a certain cylinder and so cut it by a plane that the resulting section is similar and equal to the given ellipse.*


Let the axis of the given ellipse be line :g:`✠D` and the second diameter :g:`FB`, to which let :g:`AC` be taken equal, and about it let circle :g:`AC` be described; now any right cylinder (not scalene) erected upon this circle as base will be the one sought.
Let us take it to be one whose axial parallelogram is :g:`AMGC`, with diagonal :g:`GA` greater than axis :g:`✠D` and side :g:`GM` less than the axis: therefore if with center :g:`G` and radius :g:`GE` equal to :g:`✠D` a circle be described, its circumference will divide line :g:`AM`;
let :g:`E` therefore be the point of intersection, and let :g:`GE` joined be produced toward the base so as to cut :g:`AC` produced at :g:`O`, from which point let us draw line :g:`ON` in the same plane of the circle perpendicular

   :page: p. 37

to :g:`OC`;
and passing a plane through lines :g:`ON` and :g:`GEO`, let section :g:`GIEH` be made in the cylinder; since this is an ellipse, we shall show that it is also similar and equal to the given :g:`F✠BD`. For let us bisect straight line :g:`EG` at :g:`P`,
through which as center of the ellipse let straight line :g:`IPH` pass parallel to :g:`NO`, produced both ways to the surface of the cylinder; this will therefore be an ordinate at right angles to axis :g:`EG`, and thus line :g:`IPH` will be called the second diameter,
which indeed will be equal to :g:`AC` or :g:`FB`. Now if we conceive ellipse :g:`EIGH` superposed upon ellipse :g:`DF✠B` so that line :g:`✠D` coincides with line :g:`GE`, line :g:`FB` will also coincide with :g:`IH`, and thus (as we shall also show) ellipse will fit upon ellipse, Q.E.D.


**Lemma III** (`Tab. 4, Fig. 37`)

*If, when one ellipse is conceived as placed upon another, two conjugate diameters of the one coincide with two of the other, the aforesaid ellipses will be similar and equal to one another.*

Let ellipse :g:`FABCE` be placed upon ellipse :g:`BACED`, whose conjugate diameters :g:`AE`, :g:`BC` are common.
I say that these sections coincide with one another; for if it be possible, let some point such as :g:`F` be assigned which lies on one ellipse only. Let line :g:`FDG` be drawn ordinatewise to diameter :g:`EA`, cutting the other ellipse at :g:`D` and the said diameter at :g:`G`:
thence from point :g:`E` let us erect at right angles to :g:`AE` straight line :g:`EH`, a third proportional to the two :g:`EA` and :g:`BC`; therefore :g:`EH` will be the latus rectum and :g:`EA` the transverse diameter;
yet since both ordinate :g:`FG` and :g:`DG` are equal in square to the same rectangle :g:`GEH` deficient by a figure similar and similarly placed to that contained by lines :g:`AE`, :g:`EH`, the said two ordinates will also be equal in length, namely the whole to a part,
which is absurd; wherefore no point can be assigned which does not exist in both ellipses, and thus the aforesaid ellipses will be similar and equal to one another, Q.E.D.
