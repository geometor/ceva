============================================================================

**Proposition XXI · Theorem XVIII** (p. 59, `Tab. 8, Fig. 67`): *In the same parallel configuration (`Fig. 67`), the ratio :g:`DI` to :g:`IK` along the oblique sight-line :g:`DK` is likewise compounded directly from the local graduated ratios along :g:`DE` and :g:`BC`, with Corollary.*

**SCHOLIUM — A New Instrument for Measuring Distances (*Novum instrumentum pro distantiis metiendis*)** (pp. 59–60, `Tab. 10, Fig. 85`): Ceva unveils a practical **single-station optical rangefinder** built straight from Propositions XX–XXI! Mounting two graduated parallel sighting bars :g:`DC` and :g:`EL` perpendicular to a fixed basebar :g:`DE`, the observer places the eye at :g:`D` and pivots a sighting rule :g:`DM` with pinnules until aligning with a distant target :g:`K`, noting the exact intersection :g:`I` on the diagonal bar :g:`BC`. From the two numerical readings $\overline{BD}:\overline{DE}$ and $\overline{IC}:\overline{BI}$, Propositions XX–XXI immediately yield both the transverse distance :g:`EK` and the direct line-of-sight range :g:`DK` without moving stations or using trigonometric tables!

**Epilogue & FINIS CONSTRUCTIONIS STATICÆ** (p. 60, folio ``0077``): Ceva marvels at the vast scope of his method: in *Elementum II* (`Tab. 1, Fig. 5`), once the inner chords $DB, BK, KD$ are drawn, out of **51 segment ratios**, giving **any 2** determines all remaining **49**! Beneath the floral-basket woodcut (`0077.g1.png`), Ceva adds a brief **Preface to the Appendix Geometrica**: *"It seemed fitting to append certain theorems solved by me partly by ancient geometric laws and partly by Cavalieri's method... For while squaring the circle in vain, all these were discovered not in vain!"*
