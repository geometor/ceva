**Proposition XXI · Theorem XVIII** (p. 59, `Tab. 8, Fig. 67`)

*The same things being supposed and constructed, let* :g:`NO` *to* :g:`OP` *moreover be made compounded of the ratios* :g:`BE` *to* :g:`ED`, *and* :g:`CI` *to* :g:`IB`. *I say that* :g:`DI` *to* :g:`IK` *is compounded of the ratios* :g:`DB` *to* :g:`BE`, *and* :g:`NO` *to* :g:`NP`.

:g:`AE` to :g:`EK` is compounded of the ratios :g:`AE` to :g:`DC` to :g:`EK`; and as :g:`AE` is to :g:`DC`, so is :g:`EB` to :g:`BD`; and :g:`DC` to :g:`EK` is compounded (from the preceding) of the ratios :g:`BD` to :g:`DE`, and :g:`CI` to :g:`IB`;
therefore :g:`AE` to :g:`EK` is compounded of the ratios :g:`EB` to :g:`BD` to :g:`DE`, and :g:`CI` to :g:`IB`; yea of the ratios :g:`EB` to :g:`DE`, and :g:`CI` to :g:`IB`; that is, :g:`AE` to :g:`EK` is as :g:`NO` to :g:`OP`;
and by composition, then by conversion of ratio, and by inversion, :g:`EA` will be to :g:`AK` as :g:`ON` to :g:`NP`. But in the first element, whose weights are :g:`AKD` and center :g:`B`, straight line :g:`DI` to :g:`IK`, that is weight :g:`K` to :g:`D`,
is compounded of the ratios of weights :g:`K` to :g:`E` to :g:`D`, namely of straight lines :g:`EA` to :g:`AK` and :g:`DB` to :g:`BE`; or of :g:`NO` to :g:`NP` and :g:`DB` to :g:`BE`; therefore the proposition holds.

**COROLLARY.**

*It is evident that given the two ratios* :g:`DB` *to* :g:`BE`, *and* :g:`CI` *to* :g:`IB`, *we shall also manifest the ratio* :g:`DI` *to* :g:`IK`.

**SCHOLIUM** (`Tab. 10, Fig. 85`, margin: *A new instrument for measuring distances.*)

Hence anyone may construct an instrument by whose aid he may measure intervals of visual rays, without any previously known distance or repeated stations, as is customary.

About :g:`DE` let there be two parallel rules, :g:`EL` immovable about :g:`E`, and :g:`BM` [:g:`DM`] movable about :g:`D` as center,
and from point :g:`C` let another immovable line be drawn which cuts line :g:`DE` anywhere, provided the point of section lies between the endpoints :g:`DE`, of which sort is line :g:`CB`. Let therefore the proposed object be :g:`K`.

With the eye placed at :g:`E`, let the instrument be raised or lowered until the visual line be in a straight line with rule :g:`EL`, which will happen when through the sights of :g:`EL` we see object :g:`K`; then let the instrument be fixed and secured in that position,
lest it be able to be moved in

any direction; and let the eye be placed at :g:`D`, and let rule :g:`DM` be moved about center :g:`D` until through the sights of :g:`DM` we look at the same point :g:`K` along visual line :g:`DK`;
having observed which, let point :g:`I`, namely the intersection of lines :g:`BC`, :g:`DM`, be diligently noted.

These things laid down, since the two ratios :g:`BD` to :g:`DE`, and :g:`IC` to :g:`BI`, can be had at least very nearly according to numbers,
the ratio compounded of the same, namely :g:`DC` to :g:`EK` (by Proposition 20 of Book II), will also be given in the same way; and thus we shall know how many times :g:`DC` is contained in :g:`EK`, which indeed is the interval of visual line :g:`EK` from the eye to the object;
but by the same reasoning (by Proposition 21 of Book II) we shall know how many times :g:`DI` measures :g:`DK` itself, the other visual line from the eye to the object; therefore, etc.

These things touched upon by me in few words, how widely they extend you see, gentle reader.
For there are no windings of straight lines meeting one another so entangled that they are not resolved into our elements; provided the lines cut one another by this law, that upon changing any section, all the others must necessarily vary.
Hence, two or three ratios being given, it will often happen that countless others are laid open; which anyone can try in the second element, in which, with :g:`DB`, :g:`BK`, :g:`KD` joined (`Tab. 1, Fig. 5`), out of 51 ratios, any two being given, he will find the other 49.

**END OF THE STATIC CONSTRUCTION.**

It seemed good to append to these problems certain theorems solved by me partly by ancient laws of geometry, partly by Cavalieri's method, although they depend not at all on the things stated above.
For since in squaring the circle in vain all these things were found not in vain, it was fitting that in the same volume they should enjoy the public light, although they might more opportunely have lain hidden in their own shadows.
