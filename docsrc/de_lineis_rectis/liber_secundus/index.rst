Book II: Conic Sections, Converse of Ceva & Optical Mensuration
===============================================================

.. folio:: 0052
   :page: p. 35

*Liber Secundus* (printed pp. 35–60, folios ``0052``–``0077``, `Tabulae IV–VIII & X, Figs. 32–67 & 85`) extends static construction from linear polygons to **conic sections, triangle geometry, and practical instrumentation** across six Lemmas and **21 Propositions**. Opening with oblique cylindrical projection (*Lemmas I–III*, citing :person:`Borelli <borelli>`, :person:`Serenus <serenus>`, and :person:`Apollonius <apollonius>`), Ceva proves inscribed-conic concurrency (generalized Gergonne point :math:`X(7)`) and deduces the explicit **Converse of Ceva's Theorem** (*Prop. I Corollary*, p. 38). He goes on to construct ellipses inscribed in triangles (*Prop. IX*), prove Brianchon-type concurrency and a cubic area law for three tangents to a parabola (*Props. X–XII*), establish the concurrency of triangle angle bisectors at the Incenter (*Prop. XVI*, `Tab. 7, Fig. 57`), solve 15 cases of reciprocally inscribed triangles (*Prop. XIX*), and conclude *FINIS CONSTRUCTIONIS STATICAE* with his **single-station optical rangefinder** (*Prop. XXI Scholium*, `Tab. 10, Fig. 85`).
