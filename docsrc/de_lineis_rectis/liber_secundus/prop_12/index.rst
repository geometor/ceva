Proposition XII: Cubic Area Ratio on Three Parabola Tangents
============================================================

.. folio:: 0065
   :page: p. 48

**Proposition XII · Theorem XI** (pp. 48–49, `Tab. 6, Fig. 50`)

*If a line touching a parabola cuts two other tangents, and these points of intersection and the points of contact of those same tangents are joined by two mutually intersecting lines;*
*the cubes on the portions of the first tangent intercepted between the parabola and the two remaining tangents will stand to one another as the triangles taken homologously, whose bases are the remaining tangents, and whose vertex is the same point in which the said joined lines intersected one another.*

Let the straight line :g:`FD` touch the parabola :g:`AEC` at :g:`E`, and let it cut the two tangents :g:`FA` and :g:`DC` at :g:`F` and :g:`D`; then let :g:`AD` and :g:`FC` be joined, intersecting one another at :g:`H`. I say that the cube on :g:`FE` to the cube on :g:`ED` is in the same ratio
as triangle :g:`FAH`, whose base is tangent :g:`AF`, is to triangle :g:`DHC`, whose base is the other tangent :g:`DC`. Let :g:`AC` and :g:`EH` be joined, and let the tangents :g:`AF` and :g:`CD` produced meet at :g:`G`; let :g:`EH` produced both ways cut :g:`AC` at :g:`B`, which on the other side will pass through :g:`G`.
Since in the Fourth Element, in which :g:`H` is the center of the weights :g:`EB`, namely of the weights :g:`F`, :g:`A`, :g:`C`, :g:`D`, the line :g:`AB` to :g:`BC`—namely weight :g:`C` to :g:`A`—is compounded from the ratios of the weights :g:`C` to :g:`F`, :g:`F` to :g:`D`, and :g:`D` to :g:`A`, namely of the lines :g:`FH` to :g:`HC`, :g:`ED` to :g:`EF`, and :g:`AH` to :g:`HD`,
or from the same taken in perturbed order, that is, from the ratios :g:`FH` to :g:`HC`, :g:`AH` to :g:`HD`, and :g:`DE` to :g:`EF`; yet the same :g:`AB` to :g:`BC` is compounded from the duplicate ratio of :g:`FE` to :g:`ED`, or from the triplicate of the same ratio :g:`FE` to :g:`ED` together with the inverse ratio of :g:`DE` to :g:`EF`.
Therefore, if the ratio :g:`DE` to :g:`EF` be taken away on both sides, there will remain the triplicate of :g:`FE` to :g:`ED`, that is, the cube on :g:`FE` to the cube on :g:`ED`, compounded from the ratios :g:`FH` to :g:`HC` and :g:`AH` to :g:`HD`;
since triangle :g:`FHA` to :g:`DHC` is likewise compounded from these same ratios, it is evident that the cube on :g:`FE` to the cube on :g:`ED` is as triangle :g:`FHA` to triangle :g:`DHC`, Q.E.D.

$$\triangle FHA : \triangle DHC = \overline{FE}^3 : \overline{ED}^3.$$
