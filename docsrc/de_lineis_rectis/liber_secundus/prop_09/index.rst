**Proposition IX · Problem I** (pp. 44–46, `Tab. 5, Fig. 44`)

*To a proposed triangle, to inscribe an ellipse, or when it is possible, a circle, so that out of the three points of contact any two be given.*

.. folio:: 0062
   :page: p. 45

Let there be triangle :g:`ABC`, 
and in it the given points :g:`F`, :g:`E`, through which the ellipse to be drawn must touch lines :g:`AB`, :g:`CB`. Let the problem be supposed as done, and let the inscribed ellipse be :g:`FED`, touching the remaining side :g:`AC` at :g:`D`. 
Since therefore :g:`CD` to :g:`DA` is compounded of the geometrically given ratios :g:`FB` to :g:`FA`, and :g:`CE` to :g:`EB`, point :g:`D` will be given; wherefore if lines :g:`DF`, :g:`FE`, :g:`ED` be joined, they too will be given; 
and so also the joined :g:`AH`, :g:`BI`, :g:`CK`, which will be diameters of the same ellipse. And since the center of the said ellipse exists on each of them, and they meet one another at one and the same point :g:`G`, this intersection will be given, 
and therefore the aforesaid center :g:`G` will be given. Lines :g:`AG`, :g:`BG`, :g:`CG` will therefore be those which are said to be from the center of the section; wherefore if one of them be produced, namely :g:`FG` from point :g:`G`, 
and on the production :g:`GL` be marked equal to :g:`FG`, point :g:`L` will be given, together with diameter :g:`FL`. And since the given point :g:`E` is placed on the section, and tangent :g:`BF` is given in position, that which is drawn from the same point parallel to :g:`BF`, 
meeting diameter :g:`FL` at :g:`M`, will be determined in position and length, and will be applied ordinate-wise to the same diameter :g:`FL`. Now let it be made as the given :g:`FM` to :g:`ME`, so :g:`ME` to another line :g:`Y`, 
to which let :g:`MN` be cut equal, erected perpendicularly from the given point :g:`M` to :g:`FL` given in position; and with line :g:`FO` drawn indefinitely from the given point :g:`F` parallel to :g:`MN`, let :g:`LN` be joined, 
and produced until it meets :g:`FO` at :g:`O`. This being placed, point :g:`O` will be given; and rectangle :g:`FMN` will be equal to the square of the ordinate :g:`ME`, which rectangle is applied to line :g:`FO`, 
and has for its breadth :g:`FM` intercepted between ordinate :g:`EM` and contact :g:`F`, and falls short by a figure similar and similarly placed to that which is contained by diameter :g:`FL` and line :g:`FO`; 
wherefore diameter :g:`FL` will be the transverse side of the figure, and :g:`FO` the upright side. If therefore, given two terminated straight lines :g:`FO`, :g:`FL` at right angles to one another, we find an ellipse about diameter :g:`FL`, 
so that the vertex is point :g:`F` at a right angle, and the ordinates in angle :g:`BFL` are equal in square, as :g:`ME`, to the rectangles applied to :g:`FO` 
having as breadth the line intercepted between the vertex :g:`F` of the section and the ordinates themselves, and falling short by a figure similar and similarly placed to that which is contained by lines :g:`FO` and :g:`FL`, this ellipse will touch line :g:`BA` at :g:`F` and will pass through point :g:`E`. I say moreover that such an ellipse will so pass through :g:`E` that it does not

.. folio:: 0063
   :page: p. 46

cut, but touches :g:`BC` and :g:`AC`, and therefore that the problem has been satisfied.

These things, however, will become clear by two lemmas, retaining the same letters and hypothesis.

**Lemma IV.** (*Tab. 6, Fig. 47*, p. 46)

If the ellipse :g:`FED` passing through point :g:`E` does not touch line :g:`BC`, let it touch if possible another line :g:`BK`, and let :g:`FK`, :g:`KE` be joined. Since therefore :g:`FB` touches the section at :g:`F`, the diameter :g:`BG` will bisect at :g:`L` the line :g:`FK` which joins the contacts :g:`F`, :g:`K`, and :g:`FL` will be equal to :g:`LK`; but by hypothesis :g:`FI` is also equal to :g:`IE`; therefore line :g:`EK` will be parallel to diameter :g:`BG`, and hence they will not meet. But because :g:`DC` to :g:`DA` is compounded of the ratios :g:`BF` to :g:`FA` and :g:`CE` to :g:`EB`, and :g:`FD`, :g:`FE`, :g:`ED` are bisected at :g:`N`, :g:`I`, :g:`K`, the joined lines :g:`AN`, :g:`BI`, :g:`CK` will cut one another at the same point within triangle :g:`FED`; and on that account point :g:`G` will lie below line :g:`FE`. Yet it also lies below :g:`FK`, because the two tangents :g:`BF`, :g:`BK` meet; therefore line :g:`KE` will not be parallel to diameter :g:`BG`, which cannot happen. Therefore the ellipse :g:`DFE` will touch line :g:`BEC` at point :g:`E`.

**Lemma V.** (*Tab. 6, Figs. 48, 49*, p. 46)

If an ellipse whose center is :g:`G`, touching two straight lines :g:`BFA` and :g:`BEC` at :g:`E` and :g:`F`, does not also touch :g:`ADC` at :g:`D`, let :g:`ARO` be the line touching the section. And because :g:`OR` to :g:`RA` is compounded of the ratios :g:`BF` to :g:`FA` and :g:`OE` to :g:`EB`, it is established that the point of contact is at :g:`R`. Therefore, since :g:`AF` and :g:`AR` touch the ellipse, the diameter :g:`AG` will bisect :g:`FR`, which joins the contacts; yet :g:`FD` was also bisected by hypothesis at :g:`N`. Therefore :g:`RD` will be parallel to diameter :g:`AG`, wherefore :g:`FL` too will be bisected by that same diameter at center :g:`G` (for by Euclid VI.2, as :g:`FN` to :g:`ND`, so :g:`FG` to :g:`GL`); and hence line :g:`GF`, that is :g:`GP`, will be equal to :g:`GL`, the whole to its part, which is absurd, since point :g:`L` lies within the ellipse and therefore within line :g:`GP`.
