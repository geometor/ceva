Unit IV · Appendix Geometrica (Textus Latinus, 1678, pp. 73–104)
================================================================

*Folio-verified Neo-Latin primary text from Giovanni Ceva's De lineis rectis se invicem secantibus statica constructio* (:cite:`Mediolani: Ex Typographia Ludovici Montiae, 1678 <ceva:1678:dlr>`, *Appendix Geometrica*, pp. 73–104; `PDF pp. 90–121`, `Tab. 8–10, Figs. 62–85`).

.. rst-class:: latin-source

1. De Rationibus Triangulorum Absque Principiis Staticis (pp. 73–76)
--------------------------------------------------------------------

.. rst-class:: latin-source

**Appendix Geometrica, in qua Theorematum nonnullorum demonstratio purè geometrica exhibetur, unà cum variis circuli, lunularum, ungularum ac solidorum contemplationibus.**

Ut iis etiam morem geramus, qui in Geometricis nil nisi purè geometricum admittunt, ostendemus primo loco rationes segmentorum in triangulo à rectis concurrentibus abscissorum ex sola triangulorum planorum comparatione (:person:`Euclides <euclid>`, :just:`VI.1` & :just:`V.19`), nullo prorsus adhibito ponderum aut centrorum gravitatis subsidio, demonstrari posse: nempe in triangulo $ABC$, concurrentibus $AD, BE, CF$ in puncto $P$, est triangulum $PAB$ ad triangulum $PAC$ ut basis $BD$ ad basim $DC$; pariterque triangulum $PBC$ ad $PAB$ ut $CE$ ad $EA$, ac $PAC$ ad $PBC$ ut $AF$ ad $FB$—quarum trium rationum :term:`ratio composita <ratio-composita>` manifestè est ratio æqualitatis.

2. Propositio II (Lunula Hippocratis) & Propositio VII (Ungula Cylindrica)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------

.. rst-class:: latin-source

* **Propositio II. Theorema** (`Tab. 8`, ex :person:`Hippocrate Chio <hippocrates>`): Super latere quadrati seu subtensa trianguli rectanguli isoscelis :g:`triABC` descripto semicirculo :g:`arcHyp`, atque ex angulo recto descripto quadrante :g:`arcQuad`, :g:`lunula <lune>` curvilinea inter utrumque arcum comprehensa spatio rectilineo ipsius trianguli :g:`triABC` exactè æquatur ($\frac{1}{2} R^2$).
* **Propositio VII. Theorema** (`Tab. 8–9`): Si cylindrus rectus plano per diametrum basis oblique transeunte secetur, resecabitur solidum quod :term:`ungulam cylindricam <ungula-cylindrica>` :g:`ungula` vocant; cuius soliditas—sectis per plana axi æquidistantia triangulis rectangulis similibus :g:`sliceTri`—exactè dupla est tertiæ partis (:eq:`duas tertias partes <ungula>`) totius :g:`prismatis triangularis <prism>` eidem ungulæ circumscripti ($\frac{2}{3} R^2 H$).

3. Propositiones XIII–XIV — De Centris Gravitatis Hemisphærii & Conoidis
------------------------------------------------------------------------

.. rst-class:: latin-source

* **Propositio XIII. Theorema** (`Tab. 9`): In omni hemisphærio solido :g:`hemi`, :term:`centrum gravitatis <centrum-gravitatis>` :g:`cgH` dividit axem :g:`axisVO` à vertice $V$ ad centrum basis $O$ in ratione **quinque ad tria** ($5 : 3$), quemadmodum olim divino ingenio demonstravit :person:`Lucas Valerius <valerio>` (:cite:`1604 <valerio:1604:centro>`), **subtilissimus nostri æui Archimedes**.
* **Propositio XIV & Scholium**: In conoide autem parabolico ab acutissimo :person:`Evangelista Torricellio <torricelli>` (:cite:`1644 <torricelli:1644:opera>`) ac :person:`Commandino <commandino>` celebrato, centrum gravitatis secat axem à vertice ad basim in ratione **dupla** ($2 : 1$); mirabileque insuper occurrit solidum hyperbolicum acutum infinitæ longitudinis, finitæ tamen dimensionis capax.

4. Cautio in Methodo Indivisibilium Circa Superficies Curvas (pp. 100–104)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------

.. rst-class:: latin-source

**Scholium & Cautio Coronidis Loco** (:term:`Indivisibilia Cavallerii <indivisibilia-cautio>`): Quamvis methodus indivisibilium clarissimi :person:`Bonaventuræ Cavallerii <cavalieri>` (:cite:`1635 <cavalieri:1635:indivis>`) in planis spatiis ac solidis corporibus dimetiendis mirificam præstet facilitatem, summopere tamen cavendum est tironibus, ne eandem incautè ad **superficies curvas** transferant! Si quis enim ex meris peripheriis circulorum æquidistantium (:g:`chordSlice`) superficiem sphæræ :g:`hemi` aut coni colligere attentet, neglecta obliquitate lateris seu frusti conici (:g:`slantArc`) inscripti, in apertissimum paralogismum prolabetur, falsamque omnino superficiei sphæricæ mensuram contra :person:`Archimedis <archimedes>` invictas demonstrationes concludet.
