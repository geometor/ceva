Proposition VII: Cylindrical Hoof as Two-Thirds of Circumscribed Prism
======================================================================

.. folio:: 0084
   :page: p. 67

   :alt: Calibrated interactive SVG of Ceva Appendix Prop. VII Cylindrical Hoof / Ungula (Tab. IX Fig. 74)

.. picture:: 0119.g74.png

**Proposition VII · Theorem VII** (pp. 67–69, `Tab. 9, Fig. 74`)

*A portion of a cylinder contained by two semi-ellipses or semicircles, whose common section is the second diameter, together with the convex surface of the cylinder intercepted between the same conic semi-sections, is two-thirds of a certain triangular prism circumscribed about it.*

Let a parallelepiped be conceived circumscribed about a certain semicylinder whose opposite bases are semicircles (margin: *Tab. 9, Fig. 74*); and let this together with the semicylinder inscribed in it be cut transversely by some plane, so that section :g:`BKQA` is made in the parallelepiped, 
and :g:`BCA` in the semicylinder, and let line :g:`BA` be the common section of the cutting plane and the axial parallelogram on which the semicylinder stands; then let another plane pass through :g:`BA`, cutting off both solids, and let it make section :g:`BLPA` in 
the parallelepiped, and :g:`BDA` in the semicylinder. I say that the portion of the cylinder contained by the two semicylinder sections or semi-ellipses :g:`BCA`, :g:`BDA`, and by the curved surface of the cylinder intercepted between those same conic semi-sections, is two-thirds of the 
solid circumscribed about it (namely a triangular prism, as I shall show) touching the inscribed portion at points :g:`B`, :g:`A`, and along line :g:`CD`; and its opposite bases are triangles :g:`AQP`, :g:`KBL`. Let :g:`BA` be bisected at :g:`E`, and let :g:`CE`, :g:`ED` be joined. 
Since plane :g:`KLPQ` touches the cylindrical portion along line :g:`CD`, and :g:`QK` is the common section of planes :g:`KLPQ`, :g:`BKQA`, :g:`KQ` will touch section :g:`BCA` at :g:`C`; but because we cut two parallel planes of the parallelepiped, namely :g:`KLPQ` and the opposite one 
in which line :g:`BA` lies, by plane :g:`AK`, the common section :g:`QK` touching section :g:`BCA` at :g:`C` will be parallel to the common section or second diameter :g:`BA`; and therefore joined :g:`EC` will be the conjugate semidiameter of :g:`BA`. Similarly I shall show :g:`LP`, touching section :g:`BDA` at :g:`D`, to be parallel to the same :g:`BA`, and hence :g:`ED`

   :page: p. 68

to be the conjugate semidiameter of the same :g:`BA`. Next, because :g:`KB`, :g:`QA` are the common sections of the cutting plane :g:`KA` and the two parallel triangles :g:`KBL`, :g:`QAP` (for they are parts of two parallel planes of the parallelepiped) and the triangles themselves touch 
the cylinder portion at points :g:`B`, :g:`A`; the two lines :g:`BK`, :g:`AQ` will not only be parallel to each other, but also tangent to section :g:`BCA` at points :g:`B`, :g:`A`; whence it follows that the said :g:`BK`, :g:`AQ` are also parallel to the same semidiameter :g:`CE`. 
And equally using the same reasoning I shall show that lines :g:`BL`, :g:`AP` touch section :g:`BDA` at those same two points :g:`B`, :g:`A`, and are parallel to each other and to the same semidiameter :g:`ED`, wherefore spaces :g:`KA`, :g:`BP` will be parallelograms, and therefore 
:g:`QK`, :g:`PL` will be parallel and equal to each other, inasmuch as they are equal to the one straight line :g:`BA`, from which it follows that space :g:`KP` is a parallelogram; thus the circumscribed solid :g:`QBP` will be a prism whose opposite bases are triangles :g:`KBL`, :g:`QAP`. 
Moreover let us extend through line :g:`KL` and point :g:`E` a plane cutting off the prism and the cylinder portion inscribed in it, and let the section of the prism be triangle :g:`KEL`; then finally through any taken point :g:`F` on line :g:`BA` let us draw another 
plane parallel to :g:`CDE` cutting pyramid :g:`KBLE` and again both said solids, and let the sections of the drawn planes be :g:`GNF`, :g:`IMF`, :g:`HFO`; and since the same plane :g:`KA` cuts the mutually parallel planes of triangles :g:`QPA`, :g:`CDE`, :g:`IMF`, :g:`KLB`, all the common 
sections :g:`QA`, :g:`CE`, :g:`IF`, :g:`KB` will be mutually parallel, and likewise all :g:`AP`, :g:`ED`, :g:`FM`, :g:`BL`, and equally :g:`QP`, :g:`CD`, :g:`IM`, :g:`KL`; wherefore, as :g:`BE` is to :g:`EF`, so :g:`KE` is to :g:`EG`, and :g:`LE` to :g:`EN`. Since these last two ratios are similar, it is necessary 
that line :g:`GN` be parallel to :g:`KL`. Moreover, because (margin: *Conics* I.21) the square on :g:`CE` is to the square on :g:`HF` as rectangle :g:`AEB` is to rectangle :g:`AFB`; and in the same ratio is also the square on :g:`ED` to the square on :g:`FO`; 
line :g:`CE` will be to :g:`HF` as :g:`ED` is to :g:`FO`; and angle :g:`HFO` is equal to angle :g:`CED` (because :g:`CE` is parallel to :g:`HF` and :g:`ED` to :g:`FO`), therefore triangles :g:`CED`, :g:`HFO` will be similar, and hence side :g:`HO` will also be parallel to :g:`CD`, 
which is a side of the cylinder, from which it follows that the same :g:`HO` is a straight line, inasmuch as it is a side of the cylinder; therefore since :g:`HO` is parallel to line :g:`CD`, it will likewise be parallel to lines :g:`IM`, 
:g:`KL`, and :g:`GN`; and in this way triangles :g:`CED`, :g:`IFM`, :g:`KBL`, :g:`GNF`, and :g:`HOF` are similar. These things having been shown, since square :g:`CE`,

that is :g:`IF` itself, is to square :g:`FH` as rectangle :g:`AEB` is to rectangle :g:`AFB` (margin: *ibid.*), by conversion of ratio rectangle :g:`AEB`, that is square :g:`EB`, will be to square :g:`EF` as square :g:`IF` is to its own excess over square :g:`HF`; 
but as square :g:`BE` is to square :g:`EF`, so square :g:`KB`, or :g:`IF`, is to square :g:`GF`; wherefore as square :g:`IF` is to the said excess, so the same square :g:`IF` is to :g:`GF` itself; and therefore square :g:`GF` will be equal to the excess of square :g:`IF` over 
square :g:`HF`; that is, square :g:`HF` together with square :g:`GF` will be equal to square :g:`IF`; indeed triangle :g:`IFM` of prism :g:`CLE` will be equal to triangle :g:`GFN` of pyramid :g:`KBLE` together with triangle :g:`HFO` of the semicylindrical portion; and equally triangle :g:`CED` of the same prism 
will be equal to the triangle of the said semiportion :g:`CEDB`, namely to itself; and since triangle :g:`KBL` of the prism is also equal to the triangle of the pyramid, that is to itself, all the triangles of half-prism :g:`CLE` will be equal to all the triangles of the said 
semiportion of the cylinder together with all the triangles of pyramid :g:`KBLE`: that is, half-prism :g:`CLE` will be equal to semiportion :g:`CEDB` of the cylinder together with pyramid :g:`KBLE`, and so the whole prism will be equal to the whole portion :g:`CADB` together with twice the said 
pyramid; but twice the same pyramid is a third part of the whole circumscribed prism, therefore the two remaining third parts of the same prism will be equal to the set-out portion of the cylinder, and therefore this same portion will be two-thirds of the aforesaid circumscribed prism :g:`QBP`, Q.E.D.

**Corollary I.**

*By force of this demonstration it is evident that, if that described portion of the cylinder be cut by any plane* :g:`HFO` *parallel to* :g:`CED`, *each segment of it, such as* :g:`HFOB`, *is equal to its circumscribed prism* :g:`IFL`, *minus* 
*the frustum of the pyramid lying hidden in the same prism: and equally the other remaining segment is equal to its circumscribed prism* :g:`QAM`, *with pyramids* :g:`KBLE`, :g:`GNFE` *removed.*

**Corollary II.**

*Likewise it is evident that the triangle composed of the conjugate semidiameters of the said two semi-ellipses or semicircles, together with that part of the side of the cylinder intercepted between the same semidiameters, that is triangle* :g:`CED`, *is equal to the base of the circumscribed prism.*
