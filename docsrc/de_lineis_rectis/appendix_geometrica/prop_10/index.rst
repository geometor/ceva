Proposition X: Hyperbolic Annulus as Two-Thirds of Asymptotic Cylinder
======================================================================

.. folio:: 0091
   :page: p. 74

.. picture:: 0119.g77.png
   :alt: 1678 copperplate Tabula IX Fig. 77: Hyperbolic ring/annulus around axis MN

**Proposition X · Theorem X**

*A hyperbolic annulus whose axial section consists of opposite sections is subsesquialter (two-thirds) of that cylinder whose altitude is the same as that of the annulus, and whose base is the circle generated by the revolution of the asymptotes, whose circumference and the rim of the annulus lie in the same plane and are concentric.*

Around the same axis :g:`MN` let these solids of revolution be understood (*Tab. 9, Fig. 77*): namely, the cylinders whose axial rectangles are :g:`CD`, :g:`LH`, and :g:`TO`; the two cones whose axial triangles are :g:`LGK` and :g:`SGH`; and finally the hyperbolic annulus whose axial section consists of the opposite sections :g:`CBA` and :g:`EFD`, whose asymptotes are :g:`LGH` and :g:`KGS`, 
and let the straight lines :g:`TBV` and :g:`RFO` touch them at the points :g:`B` and :g:`F`. I say that this annulus is subsesquialter (two-thirds) of the cylinder :g:`LH`. From any assumed point :g:`✠` let a plane be drawn parallel to the circle whose diameter is the straight line :g:`LK`, cutting the cylinder :g:`TO`, the cone :g:`LGK`, and the hyperbolic drum :g:`CBADFE`; the sections will be circles whose diameters lie in the same plane of the parallelogram :g:`CD`, namely :g:`℣Y`, :g:`℞Z`, and :g:`QP`. 
Since therefore the rectangle :g:`CKE` is equal to the square on :g:`GF`, that is, on :g:`MR` (*Conics* II.11), while the rectangle :g:`CKE` together with the square on :g:`MK` is equal to the square on :g:`ME`, the square on :g:`MR` together with the square on :g:`MK` will be equal to the square on :g:`ME`, and likewise their doubles; wherefore the circle whose diameter is :g:`TR`, together with that whose diameter is :g:`LK`, will be equal to the circle whose diameter is :g:`CE`; and by the exact same reasoning the circle whose diameter is :g:`VO`, together with the circle whose diameter is :g:`SH`, will be equal to the circle whose diameter is :g:`AD`. 
Again, the rectangle :g:`QZP`, that is, the square on :g:`GF`—nay, on :g:`✠Y` itself—together with the square on :g:`✠Z`, is equal to the square on :g:`✠P`; therefore the circle whose diameter is :g:`℣Y`, together with that whose diameter is :g:`℞Z`, will be equal to the circle whose diameter is :g:`QP`. Moreover, the circle :g:`BF` is common to both solids, namely the cylinder :g:`TO` and the aforesaid drum; therefore, since it is established that all the circles of the cylinder :g:`TO`, together with all those of the two cones :g:`LGK` and :g:`SGH`, are equal to all the circles of the hyperbolic drum :g:`CBADFE`, that drum will be equal to the cylinder :g:`TO` together with the two cones :g:`LGK` and :g:`SGH`; 
but the cylinder :g:`TO` is equal to the difference

of the cylinders :g:`LH` and :g:`CD`, and the two cones :g:`SGH` and :g:`LGK` taken together are one third of the cylinder :g:`LH`. Therefore the aforesaid drum will be equal to one third of the cylinder :g:`LH` together with the difference of the cylinders :g:`CD` and :g:`LH`; and hence the excess by which the cylinder :g:`CD` exceeds the sum of one third of the cylinder :g:`LH` and the difference of the cylinders :g:`CD` and :g:`LH`—which excess is :math:`\frac{2}{3}` of the cylinder :g:`LH`—will be equal to the hyperbolic annulus :g:`CBA`, :g:`DFE`, which was to be proved.
