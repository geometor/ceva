**Proposition III · Problem III · Element III**

*In triangle* :g:`EAC` *let two straight lines intersect each other at* :g:`G`, *of which* :g:`EB`, *drawn from vertex* :g:`E`, *cuts the base at* :g:`B`, *and the other,* :g:`FD`, *meets the sides* :g:`AE <EA>`, :g:`CE <EC>` *at* :g:`F` *and* :g:`D`. *Let it be proposed to us to suspend heavy bodies from the angles of the same triangle:*
:g:`I` *at point* :g:`C`; *two,* :g:`L`, :g:`K`, *at* :g:`E`; *and a single* :g:`H` *at* :g:`A`, *so that* :g:`K` *to* :g:`I` *is as straight line* :math:`CD` *to* :math:`DE`; :g:`I` *to* :g:`H` *as* :math:`AB` *to* :math:`BC`; :g:`H` *to* :g:`L` *as* :math:`EF` *to* :math:`FA`;
:g:`L` :g:`K` *to* :g:`H` :g:`I` *as* :math:`BG` *to* :math:`GE`, *and the two* :g:`H`, :g:`L` *to the two* :g:`K`, :g:`I` *as* :math:`DG` *to* :math:`GF`.

(`Tab. 1, Fig. 6`) Let :g:`L` be any weight whatever, and let another :g:`H` be found to which the first weight :g:`L` has that ratio which :math:`AF` has to :math:`FE`.
Next let :g:`I` be placed, to which weight :g:`H` has that ratio in which :math:`CB` stands to :math:`BA`; likewise let weight :g:`K` be found, to which weight :g:`I` has the ratio which :math:`ED` has to :math:`DC`; I say that the problem has been completed.

For since :g:`FD` is a certain balance-beam, at whose extremity :g:`F` is the center—and therefore the weight—of the heavy bodies :g:`H`, :g:`L`, while at the other extremity :g:`D` is the weight of the heavy bodies :g:`K`, :g:`I` (since their center of gravity is at point :g:`D`),
the center of gravity of the aforesaid weights :g:`H`, :g:`L`, :g:`K`, :g:`I` will lie on the same balance-beam :g:`FD`. Similarly, since :g:`EB` is a balance-beam at whose extremity :g:`E` are the heavy bodies :g:`L`, :g:`K`, while at the other extremity :g:`B` is the weight of the heavy bodies :g:`H`, :g:`I`,
the center of gravity of the same weights :g:`L`, :g:`K`, :g:`H`, :g:`I`, existing in the same former position, will lie on the same balance-beam :g:`EB`. Since therefore the center of gravity of weights :g:`L`, :g:`K`, :g:`H`, :g:`I` lies both on balance-beam :g:`FD` and on :g:`BE <EB>`, and that center is unique,
it will lie at the common intersection :g:`G`, and therefore :g:`L`, :g:`K` to :g:`H`, :g:`I` will be as straight line :math:`BG` to :math:`GE`, and as :g:`K`, :g:`I` to :g:`L`, :g:`H`, so :math:`FG` to :math:`GD`, which was &c.

**Scholium.**

*The aggregate of weights* :g:`L`, :g:`K` *we shall signify by the letter* :g:`E`; *weight* :g:`H` *by the letter* :g:`A`, *and weight* :g:`I` *the letter* :g:`C` *will denote;* :g:`D` *will express the two weights* :g:`K`, :g:`I`, *the letter* :g:`F` *the two* :g:`L`, :g:`H`,
*and the letter* :g:`B` *will indicate the two* :g:`H`, :g:`I`; *weight* :g:`L` *in the analytic or algebraic manner we shall write* ``F - A``, *that is* :g:`F` *minus* :g:`A`; *and by the same reason weight* :g:`K` *we shall write* ``D - C``;
*and all the weights* :g:`L`, :g:`H`, :g:`I`, :g:`K` *the letter* :g:`G` *will signify.*
