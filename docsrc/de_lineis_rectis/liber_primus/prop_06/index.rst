**Lemma III.**

(`Tab. 1, Fig. 9`) Let $B$ be the center of gravity of weights $A, C$. I say that weight $B$—namely the aggregate of the heavy bodies $A, C$ weighing at $B$—is to weight $C$ as $AC$ to $BA$.
Since $B$ is the center of gravity of weights $A, C$, $A$ will be to $C$ as $CB$ to $BA$; therefore by compounding, weight $B$ will be to $C$ as $CA$ to $BA$, which &c.

**Proposition VI · Problem VI**

*Having set forth the figure* :g:`A` :g:`E` :g:`C` :g:`F` *of the First Element,*
*let weights* :g:`A`, :g:`E`, :g:`C` *be understood as disposed at* :g:`A`, :g:`E`, :g:`C` *in that manner in which the weights* $G, I, H$ *were set forth there, so that* :g:`D` *is the center of gravity of weights* :g:`A`, :g:`E`, :g:`B` *of weights* :g:`A`, :g:`C`, *and* :g:`F` *of all;*
*therefore there will be four ratios,* $AB$ *to* $BC$, $AD$ *to* $DE$, $EF$ *to* $FB$, *and* $CF$ *to* $FD$, *of which if any two be given in numbers, we shall investigate the remaining two.*

Of this problem there are six cases; for (as from the combinatorial art)
multiplying four (that is, how many ratios are given) by three, namely the number deficient by unity, there arises $12$, half of which, $6$, furnishes us the number of pairs into which the aforesaid number $4$ can be distributed.

.. folio:: 0025
   :page: p. 8

(`Tab. 1, Fig. 4`) Let there be given first two ratios $EF$ to $FB$ and $CF$ to $FD$, let the former be as $2$ to $1$, and the latter as $3$ to $2$; we must now make manifest the remaining two ratios $CB$ to $BA$ and $ED$ to $DA$.

Since weight :g:`B` (namely the aggregate of :g:`A`, :g:`C`) is to :g:`C` (by the premised Third Lemma) as straight line $AC$ to $AB$, while the ratio of weight :g:`B` to :g:`C` is compounded of the ratios of weights :g:`B` to :g:`F` and :g:`F` to :g:`C`,
and as :g:`B` to :g:`F`, so $EF$ to $EB$, and as :g:`F` to :g:`C`, so $DC$ to $DF$, the ratio $AC$ to $AB$ will be compounded of the ratios $EF$ to $EB$ and $DC$ to $DF$; and since $EF$ to $FB$ is as $2$ to $1$, by compounding, then by conversion of ratio,
and by converting, $EF$ to $EB$ is as $2$ to $3$; and $CF$ to $FD$ was also as $3$ to $2$, and by compounding $CD$ to $FD$ as $5$ to $2$; therefore straight line $AC$ to $AB$ will be compounded of the ratios $2$ to $3$ and $5$ to $2$,
or of these, $2$ to $3$ and $3$ to $1\tfrac{1}{5}$; that is, the same $AC$ to $AB$ as $2$ to $1\tfrac{1}{5}$, or as $10$ to $6$; wherefore by dividing, $CB$ to $BA$ will be as $4$ to $6$, or as $2$ to $3$.
By the same reason, since weight :g:`D` to :g:`E` is compounded of the ratios of weights :g:`D` to :g:`F` and :g:`F` to :g:`E`, namely of straight lines $CF$ to $CD$ and $EB$ to $BF$, the same weight :g:`D` to :g:`E`, that is, $AE$ to $DA$, will be compounded
of the ratios $3$ to $5$ and $3$ to $1$: and of these ratios is compounded that $3$ to $1\tfrac{2}{3}$, that is, $9$ to $5$; therefore $AE$ to $DA$ is as $9$ to $5$,
but by dividing, $ED$ to $DA$ will be as $4$ to $5$; therefore $CB$ to $BA$ is as $2$ to $3$, and $ED$ to $DA$ as $4$ to $5$, which was to be done.

II. Let two ratios be given, $EF$ to $FB$ as $4$ to $5$,
and $ED$ to $DA$ as $7$ to $9$; we must trace out the remaining ratios $CF$ to $FD$ and $CB$ to $BA$.

The ratio of straight line $AC$ to $CB$—namely of weight :g:`B` to :g:`A`—is compounded of the ratios of weights :g:`B` to :g:`E` and :g:`E` to :g:`A`,
namely of straight lines $EF$ to $FB$ and $AD$ to $DE$, that is, of the ratios $4$ to $5$ and $9$ to $7$; wherefore $AC$ to $CB$ is compounded of the ratios $4$ to $5$ and $9$ to $7$: now this compounded ratio is that which $4$ has to $3\tfrac{8}{9}$,
or $36$ to $35$; therefore $AC$ to $CB$ is as $36$ to $35$, and by dividing, $AB$ to $BC$ as $1$ to $35$.

Similarly, since $CD$ to $CF$—that is, weight :g:`F` to :g:`D`—is compounded of the ratios of weights :g:`F` to :g:`E` and :g:`E` to :g:`D`,
namely of straight lines $EB$ to $BF$ and $DA$ to $AE$, that is, of the ratios $9$ to $5$ and $9$ to $16$; now this ratio is the same as that which $9$ has to $8\tfrac{8}{9}$, or as $81$ to $80$;
by dividing, $DF$ to $FC$ will be as $1$ to $80$.

III. But if two ratios be given, $CF$ to $FD$ as $4$ to $5$, and $CB$ to $BA$ as $7$ to $9$, we shall likewise give, in the same way as above, $AD$

   :page: p. 9

to $DE$ as $1$ to $35$; and $BF$ to $FE$ as $1$ to $80$.

IV. Let the ratio $ED$ to $DA$ be as $20$ to $21$, and $CB$ to $BA$ as $99$ to $25$, and let it be required to make known the two ratios $EF$ to $FB$ and $CF$ to $FD$.
The ratio of straight line $CF$ to $FD$—namely of weight :g:`D` to :g:`C`—is compounded of the ratios of weights :g:`D` to :g:`A` and :g:`A` to :g:`C`, that is, of straight lines $EA$ to $ED$ and $CB$ to $BA$, namely of the ratios $41$ to $20$ and $99$ to $25$:
but $41$ to $5\tfrac{5}{99}$ is compounded of the same ratios; therefore $CF$ to $DF$ is as $41$ to $5\tfrac{5}{99}$, that is, as $4059$ to $500$.

By the same reason, since $EF$ to $FB$—that is, weight :g:`B` to :g:`E`—is compounded of the ratios of weights :g:`B` to :g:`A`
and :g:`A` to :g:`E`, namely of the lines $CA$ to $CB$ and $ED$ to $DA$, that is, of the ratios $124$ to $99$ and $20$ to $21$; and since of the same ratios is compounded the ratio which $124$ has to $103\tfrac{19}{20}$, that is, as $2480$ to $2079$,
$EF$ to $FB$ will be in the same ratio.

V. Let two proportions be given, $ED$ to $DA$ as $2$ to $1$, and $CF$ to $FD$ of equality; we must investigate the remaining two, namely $CB$ to $BA$ and $EF$ to $FB$.
Compounded is $CB$ to $BA$—doubtless weight :g:`A` to :g:`C`—of the ratios of weights :g:`A` to :g:`D` and :g:`D` to :g:`C`, namely of straight lines $ED$ to $EA$ and $CF$ to $FD$;
that is, of the ratios $2$ to $3$ and $3$ to $3$; therefore $CB$ to $BA$ is as $2$ to $3$.

Similarly $EB$ to $FB$—that is, the ratio of weight :g:`F` to :g:`E`—is compounded of the ratios of weights :g:`F` to :g:`D`, :g:`D` to :g:`E`, namely of straight lines
$CD$ to $CF$ and $AE$ to $AD$, that is, of the ratios $2$ to $1$ and $3$ to $1$; but of the same ratios is compounded $2$ to $\tfrac{1}{3}$, or $6$ to $1$; therefore $EB$ to $BF$ is as $6$ to $1$,
and by dividing, $EF$ to $FB$ as $5$ to $1$.

VI. And lastly. If the given proportions be $CB$ to $BA$ as $2$ to $1$, and $EF$ to $FB$ of equality, we shall show (as was done above) $ED$ to $DA$ as $2$ to $3$,
and $CF$ to $FD$ as $5$ to $1$.

**Scholium.**

*As often as a problem is impossible, it will be discerned from the operation itself; for if the ratio* $DF$ *to* $FC$ *be given as a ratio of equality, and likewise* $BF$ *to* $FE$ *of equality, I say that the question is impossible.*
*For since weight* :g:`C` *to* :g:`B`, *that is, straight line* $AB$ *to* $AC$, *is compounded of the ratios of weights* :g:`C` *to* :g:`F` *and* :g:`F` *to* :g:`B`, *namely of straight lines* $DF$ *to* $DC$ *and* $EB$ *to* $EF$, *that is, of the ratios* $1$ *to* $2$ *and* $2$ *to* $1$,
*the part* $AB$ *to the whole* $AC$ *will be as* $1$ *to* $1$, *the part equal to the whole, which is absurd. This, or a not dissimilar absurdity, always*

.. folio:: 0027
   :page: p. 10

*follows whenever the proposed question is impossible.*

*The first part of this proposition, or the first case, whose title I had transmitted to my friend without adding or indicating any solution, was demonstrated geometrically by a noble youth, conspicuous for his multitude of languages, arts, and sciences,*
*Pietro Paolo Caravaggio, son of my teacher Pietro Paolo. I set down his demonstration, which will place this static method in a geometric light.*

*Let* $EF$ *to* $FB$ *be as* $2$ *to* $1$, *and* $CF$ *to* $FD$ *as* $3$ *to* $2$; *I say that* $ED$ *to* $DA$ *is as* $4$ *to* $5$, *and* $CB$ *to* $BA$ *as* $2$ *to* $3$.*

(`Tab. 1, Fig. 9`, emend. `Fig. 10`) Let :g:`GH` be drawn parallel to :g:`EC`.
Since :g:`EC` to $GF$ is as $CD$ to $DF$, namely as $5$ to $2$, and :g:`EC` to $FH$ as $15$ to $5$, :g:`EC` to :g:`GH` will be as $15$ to $11$; and equally :g:`AE` to $AG$ and :g:`AC` to $AH$ as $15$ to $11$;
therefore of those parts of which :g:`AE` is $15$, $GE$ will be $4$, and likewise of those parts of which :g:`AC` is $15$, $HC$ will be $4$; since therefore $GE$ to $ED$ is as $CF$ to $CD$, namely as $3$ to $5$, if as $3$ to $5$,
so $4$ be made to another number, the number $\tfrac{20}{3}$ will come forth for $ED$, whose remainder from $EA$ ($15$) will be $\tfrac{25}{3}$; therefore $ED$ to $DA$ will be as $4$ to $5$.

Similarly since, of those parts of which :g:`AC` is $15$, $CH$ is $4$, and $CH$ to $CB$ is as $2$ to $3$, $CB$ will be $6$, and $BA$ $9$: wherefore $CB$ to $BA$ will be as $2$ to $3$.
