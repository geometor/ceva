Proposition IX: Fifteen Combinatorial Cases of Element II
=========================================================

.. folio:: 0030
   :page: p. 13

**Proposition IX · Problem VII** (`Tab. 1, Fig. 5`)

*The figure of the Second Element being set out, and the weights understood as suspended in the same way, given any two of the six ratios* :g:`ED` *to* :g:`DA`, :g:`AB` *to* :g:`BC`, :g:`CK` *to* :g:`KE`, :g:`KF` *to* :g:`FA`, 
:g:`DF` *to* :g:`FC`, *and* :g:`EF` *to* :g:`FB`, *it is required to investigate the remaining four.*

There are fifteen cases of this problem; for multiplying 6 by 5 makes the product 30, whose half is 15, namely the number of pairs derived from six.

**I.** First, let two ratios be given: :g:`ED` to :g:`DA` as 6 to 7, and :g:`AB` to :g:`BC` as 8 to 9; and let those we must exhibit be :g:`EF` to :g:`FB`, :g:`DF` to :g:`FC`, :g:`KF` to :g:`FA`, and :g:`EK` to :g:`KC`.

Now in figure :g:`EACF`, since two ratios :g:`ED` to :g:`DA` and :g:`AB` to :g:`BC` are given, the remaining two will be known, namely :g:`DF` to :g:`FC` as 16 to 39, and :g:`EF` to :g:`FB` as 34 to 21; 
next, because in figure :g:`ACEF` of the First Element two ratios :g:`EF` to :g:`FB` as 34 to 21 and :g:`AB` to :g:`BC` as 8 to 9 are given, 
the remaining two ratios :g:`AF` to :g:`FK` as 37 to 18 and :g:`EK` to :g:`KC` as 16 to 21 will also be given.

**II.** But if the given ratios had been :g:`AB` to :g:`BC` as 6 to 7 and :g:`CK` to :g:`KE` as 8 to 9, 
the four ratios would likewise be given: :g:`BF` to :g:`FE` as 16 to 39, :g:`AF` to :g:`FK` as 34 to 21, :g:`CF` to :g:`FD` as 37 to 18, and :g:`AD` to :g:`DE` as 16 to 21.

**III.** And if the two given ratios were 
:g:`CK` to :g:`KE` as 6 to 7 and :g:`ED` to :g:`DA` as 8 to 9, in the same manner we shall make known the four ratios :g:`KF` to :g:`FA` as 16 to 39, :g:`CF` to :g:`FD` as 34 to 21, :g:`EF` to :g:`FB` as 37 to 18, 
and :g:`CB` to :g:`BA` as 16 to 21.

**IV.** Let the two given ratios be :g:`AF` to :g:`FK` as 21 to 13 and :g:`EK` to :g:`KC` as 10 to 11; we must find the remaining four, namely :g:`ED` to :g:`DA`, :g:`AB` to :g:`BC`, :g:`EF` to :g:`FB`, 
and :g:`CF` to :g:`FD`. Since in the First Element whose vertex is :g:`C` and center :g:`F` the two ratios :g:`AF` to :g:`FK` as 21 to 13 and :g:`EK` to :g:`KC` as 10 to 11 are given, the remaining two

   :page: p. 14

:g:`EF` to :g:`FB` as 23 to 11 and :g:`AB` to :g:`BC` as 10 to 13 will be given; and because again in the First Element whose vertex is :g:`E` and the same center :g:`F` the two ratios :g:`AF` to :g:`FK` as 21 to 13 
and :g:`EK` to :g:`KC` as 10 to 11 are given, we shall likewise make known the remaining two: :g:`ED` to :g:`DA` will be as 13 to 11, and :g:`CF` to :g:`FD` as 24 to 10.

**V.** But if the given ratios are :g:`CF` to :g:`FD` as 21 to 13 
and :g:`AD` to :g:`DE` as 10 to 11, the four ratios will likewise be known: :g:`AF` to :g:`FK` as 23 to 11, :g:`CK` to :g:`KE` as 10 to 13, :g:`AB` to :g:`BC` as 13 to 11, and :g:`EF` to :g:`FB` as 24 to 10.

**VI.** And if the two given ratios had been :g:`EF` to :g:`FB` as 21 to 13 and :g:`CB` to :g:`BA` as 10 to 11, the remaining four ratios would be had in the exact same way, namely :g:`CK` to :g:`KE` as 13 to 11, 
:g:`AF` to :g:`FK` as 24 to 10, :g:`CF` to :g:`FD` as 23 to 11, and :g:`AB` to :g:`BC` as 10 to 13.

**VII.** Having the two ratios :g:`EF` to :g:`FB` of equality and :g:`CF` to :g:`FD` as 52 to 18, we shall also find the remaining four. 
For in the First Element whose vertex is :g:`A` and center :g:`F`, both ratios :g:`EF` to :g:`FB` and :g:`CF` to :g:`FD` are given, wherefore we shall not be ignorant of the two remaining, namely :g:`CB` to :g:`BA` as 17 to 18, 
and :g:`AD` to :g:`DE` as 35 to 17. Therefore the two ratios :g:`EF` to :g:`FB` of equality and :g:`AB` to :g:`BC` as 18 to 17 are given; hence in the First Element whose vertex is :g:`C` and center :g:`F`, the two remaining will also be given: 
:g:`EK` to :g:`KC` as 18 to 35, and :g:`KF` to :g:`FA` as 17 to 53.

**VIII.** And if the given ratios had been :g:`EF` to :g:`FB` of equality and :g:`AF` to :g:`FK` as 52 to 18, we know by the same reasoning 
:g:`EK` to :g:`KC` as 17 to 18, :g:`CB` to :g:`BA` as 35 to 17, :g:`AD` to :g:`DE` as 18 to 35, and :g:`DF` to :g:`FC` as 17 to 53.

**IX.** But if the two known ratios are :g:`CF` to :g:`FD` as 1 to 1, 
and :g:`AF` to :g:`FK` as 52 to 18, the remaining four will not lie hidden: :g:`AD` to :g:`DE` will be as 17 to 18, :g:`EK` to :g:`KC` as 35 to 17, :g:`CB` to :g:`BA` as 18 to 35, and :g:`BF` to :g:`FE` as 17 to 53.

**X.** Let the two given ratios be :g:`ED` to :g:`DA` as 39 to 105 and :g:`BF` to :g:`FE` as 105 to 69; we shall know the remaining four as well: for in the First Element whose vertex is :g:`A` and center :g:`F`, the two exposed ratios are given, 
therefore the two remaining ratios will also become plain, namely :g:`CF` to :g:`FD` as 144 to 30, and :g:`CB` to :g:`BA` as 39 to 30. Yet since again in another First Element, whose vertex is :g:`C` and whose center is the same :g:`F`, two ratios are known—:g:`BF` to :g:`FE` as 105 to 69, and :g:`CB` to :g:`BA` as 39 to 30—

the remaining ratios will likewise be given, :g:`AF` to :g:`FK` as 135 to 39, and :g:`EK` to :g:`KC` as 30 to 105.

The remaining five cases are wholly similar to this tenth case above, although their position appears different, and therefore they are solved in the same manner, which was to be shown.
