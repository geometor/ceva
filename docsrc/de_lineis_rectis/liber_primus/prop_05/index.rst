Proposition V: Fifth Element (Planar & Skew Quadrilateral)
==========================================================

**Proposition V · Problem V · Element V** (`Tab. 1, Fig. 8`)

*In a quadrilateral :g:`ACIH`, let straight lines :g:`BG` and :g:`ED` joining points on opposite pairs of sides intersect at :g:`F`. To find the static equilibrium of four weights suspended at the four vertices.*

Suspend four weights :g:`M` at :g:`A`, :g:`L` at :g:`C`, :g:`K` at :g:`I`, and :g:`N` at :g:`H` so that :g:`B` balances :g:`M` and :g:`L`, :g:`D` balances :g:`L` and :g:`K`, :g:`G` balances :g:`K` and :g:`N`, and :g:`E` balances :g:`N` and :g:`M`. Then grouping opposite sides forces both bimedians :g:`BG` and :g:`ED` to pass through the unique four-weight center of gravity :g:`F`, yielding cyclic product unity:

$$\frac{\overline{AB}}{\overline{BC}} \cdot \frac{\overline{CD}}{\overline{DI}} \cdot \frac{\overline{IG}}{\overline{GH}} \cdot \frac{\overline{HE}}{\overline{EA}} = \frac{L}{M} \cdot \frac{K}{L} \cdot \frac{N}{K} \cdot \frac{M}{N} = 1.$$

**Corollary (3D Skew Quadrilateral)**: Ceva remarks with startling geometric insight that **the entire theorem remains true even if the quadrilateral does not lie in a single plane** (:term:`quadrilaterum non planum <quadrilaterum-non-planum>`, *"licet quadrilaterum non in eodem plano iaceat"*): because both lines :g:`BG` and :g:`ED` pass through the unique spatial center of gravity :g:`F`, they necessarily intersect and therefore span a single plane containing all four edge fulcrums :g:`B`, :g:`D`, :g:`G`, :g:`E`!
