**Proposition XX · Problem XI**

*The figure of the Fifth Element being set forth, and out of the six ratios of which we treated in the same Element, any three being given, to find the remaining two.*

This problem has twenty cases; for multiplying 6 by 5 makes 30, from half of which, 15, multiplied by 4 makes 60; the third part of this is 20, namely the number of all triples arising from the number 6.

I. (`Tab. 2, Fig. 22`) Let the three ratios be given: :g:`CD` to :g:`DE` as 12 to 8, :g:`EF` to :g:`FG` as 4 to 12, and :g:`GH` to :g:`HA` as 8 to 4; we must investigate the remaining three. 
As was done in the Fifth Element, let us suspend from the angles :g:`C`, :g:`E`, :g:`G`, :g:`A` the heavy bodies :g:`C`, :g:`E`, :g:`G`, :g:`A`, so that the weights are reciprocally among themselves as the lengths from which they hang; 
thus if :g:`C` weighs as 4, :g:`E` will weigh as 6, because :g:`ED` to :g:`DC` is as 8 to 12, or 4 to 6. By the same ratio, :g:`E` being as 6, :g:`G` will be as 2; for :g:`GF` to :g:`FE` is as 12 to 4, or 6 to 2; 
and finally if :g:`G` be 2, :g:`A` will be 4; for :g:`AH` to :g:`HG` is as 4 to 8, nay as 2 to 4; wherefore :g:`C` will be 4, :g:`E` 6, :g:`G` 2, and :g:`A` 4; but (inasmuch as it is the figure of the Fifth Element) 
:g:`AB` to :g:`BC` is as weight :g:`C` 4 to weight :g:`A` 4; therefore :g:`AB` to :g:`BC` will be as 4 to 4, namely of equality; next :g:`HI` to :g:`ID` as weight :g:`D` 10 to weight :g:`H` 6, that is as 10 to 6; 
and likewise :g:`BI` to :g:`IF` as heavy body :g:`F` 8 to :g:`B` 8, namely as 8 to 8, or of equality, and thus we have made known the three remaining ratios :g:`AB` to :g:`BC` of equality, just as :g:`BI` to :g:`IF`, 
and :g:`HI` to :g:`ID` as 10 to 6, or 5 to 3.

II. III. IV. But if the three given ratios be :g:`EF` to :g:`FG`; :g:`GH` to :g:`HA`; :g:`AB` to :g:`BC`: or :g:`GH` to :g:`HA`; :g:`AB` to :g:`BC`, and :g:`CD` to :g:`DE`: 
or :g:`AB` to :g:`BC`; :g:`CD` to :g:`DE`; :g:`EF` to :g:`FG`, we shall solve the problem in the same manner as above.

V. Let the three ratios be given: :g:`AB` to :g:`BC` as 5 to 6; :g:`BI` to :g:`IF` of equality; and :g:`GF` to :g:`FE` as 8 to 3; 
and let it be required to make known the remaining ones. Let us suspend from the angles the heavy bodies :g:`C`, :g:`E`, :g:`G`, :g:`A`, as the Fifth Element teaches; therefore as :g:`AB` is to :g:`BC`, that is as 5 to 6, so is heavy body :g:`C` to :g:`A`; 
if therefore :g:`C` weighs 5, :g:`A` will weigh 6. Next, because as :g:`FI` is to :g:`IB`, that is as 1 to 1, so is weight :g:`B` 11 to :g:`F`, :g:`F` will also be 11; 
now :g:`GF` is to :g:`FE` as 8 to 3; and by composition :g:`GE` to :g:`FE` as 11 to 3, in

   :page: p. 28

which proportion is also weight :g:`F` to :g:`G`; therefore since :g:`F` weighs 11, :g:`G` will weigh 3, and the remaining weight :g:`E` 8; 
and so the remaining ones are at once had, that is :g:`HI` to :g:`ID` as weight :g:`D` 13 to weight :g:`H` 9; and likewise :g:`AH` to :g:`HG` as heavy body :g:`G` 3 to heavy body :g:`A` 6, 
and finally :g:`CD` to :g:`DE` as heavy body :g:`E` 8 to heavy body :g:`C` 5.

VI. But if the three given ratios be the same ones which we have just made known, we shall give the remaining three in the same manner.

VII. Let three ratios be given: :g:`GF` to :g:`FE` as 6 to 5, :g:`HI` to :g:`ID` as 13 to 9, and :g:`FI` to :g:`IB` as 11 to 11, namely of equality; we must investigate the remaining ones as well. 
Since as :g:`GF` is to :g:`FE`, so is weight :g:`E` to :g:`G`, and :g:`GF` to :g:`FE` is as 6 to 5; if therefore weight :g:`E` be set as 6, :g:`G` will be 5; next, because as :g:`BI` is to :g:`IF`, nay as 11 to 11, 
so is weight :g:`F` to :g:`B`; and :g:`F` is 11, therefore :g:`B` will likewise be 11, wherefore weight :g:`I` will be 22; now :g:`HI` is to :g:`ID` as 13 to 9; therefore by composition :g:`HD` to :g:`DI`, that is weight :g:`I` to :g:`H`, will be as 22 to 9; 
and hence since weight :g:`I` is 22, the heavy body :g:`H` will be 9; but weight :g:`I` 22 is equal to the weights :g:`H` 9 and :g:`D`, therefore :g:`D` will be 13; similarly because :g:`H` 9 is equal to the two weights :g:`G` 5 and :g:`A`, and :g:`D` 13 
is equal to the two weights :g:`E` 6 and :g:`C`, :g:`A` will be 4 and :g:`C` 7; and therefore :g:`AB` to :g:`BC` will be as weight :g:`C` 7 to weight :g:`A` 4, :g:`GH` to :g:`HA` as weight :g:`A` 4 to weight :g:`G` 5, 
and finally :g:`ED` to :g:`DC` as weight :g:`C` 7 to :g:`E` 6.

VIII. IX. X. But if the three given ratios be :g:`AB` to :g:`BC`, :g:`HI` to :g:`ID`, :g:`BI` to :g:`IF`; or the three :g:`CD` to :g:`DE`, :g:`BI` to :g:`IF`, and :g:`DI` to :g:`IH`; 
or the three :g:`GH` to :g:`HA`, :g:`FI` to :g:`IB`, and :g:`HI` to :g:`ID`; by the same reasoning we shall satisfy the problem.

XI. Further, let three other ratios be given: :g:`GH` to :g:`HA` as 5 to 17, :g:`FI` to :g:`IB` as 7 to 16, 
and :g:`GF` to :g:`FE` as 15 to 17; it is required to search out the remaining three.

Because as :g:`GH` to :g:`HA`, that is as 5 to 17, so is weight :g:`A` to :g:`G`, if heavy body :g:`A` be 5, heavy body :g:`G` will be 17. 
Likewise since :g:`EF` to :g:`FG`, that is 17 to 15, is as weight :g:`G` to :g:`E`, and weight :g:`G` is 17, weight :g:`E` will be 15; and finally, because :g:`BI` to :g:`IF`, that is 16 to 7, or 32 to 14, is as weight :g:`F` to :g:`B`, and :g:`F` is 32, 
:g:`B` will be 14; but :g:`B` 14 is the weight of the heavy bodies :g:`A` 5 and :g:`C`; therefore :g:`C` will be 9, and so :g:`CD` to :g:`DE` will be as weight :g:`E` 15 to weight :g:`C` 9; similarly :g:`AB` to :g:`BC` will be as :g:`C` 9 to :g:`A` 5; 
and finally :g:`HI` to :g:`ID` will be as :g:`D` 24 to :g:`H` 22.

XII. XIII. XIV. XV. XVI. XVII. XVIII. Seven other cases similar to the aforesaid are solved in the same manner.

XIX. (`Tab. 2, Fig. 23`) Let the ratios be given: :g:`GF` to :g:`FE` as 8 to 9, :g:`HI` to :g:`ID` as 15 to 19, and :g:`AB` to :g:`BC` as 7 to 10.
Care must be taken, however, if the three :g:`GE`, :g:`HD`, :g:`AC` be parallel, 
that the other three :g:`GA`, :g:`FB`, :g:`EC` be not equidistant.

We shall solve this case algebraically by second roots in this manner.

Since as :g:`GF` to :g:`FE`, so weight :g:`E` to :g:`G`, if :g:`E` be put as ℞ 1, :g:`G` will be ℞ 1 1/8. 
Likewise because :g:`AB` to :g:`BC` is as weight :g:`C` to :g:`A`, if we will that :g:`C` weigh A 1, :g:`A` will weigh A 1 3/7, and because :g:`HI` to :g:`ID` is as weight :g:`D` to :g:`H`, and :g:`D` is set as 15, :g:`H` will be as 19; 
but since 19, namely weight :g:`H`, is equal to the two weights :g:`G`, :g:`A`, that is ℞ 1 1/8 ✠ A 1 3/7; and :g:`D` is equal to the two :g:`C`, :g:`E`, that is 15, namely ℞ 1 ✠ 1 A; 
it will be as 19 to 15, so ℞ 1 1/8 ✠ A 1 3/7 to ℞ 1 ✠ 1 A; wherefore the product of the means will be equal to that of the extremes, namely ℞ 19 ✠ 19 A will be equal to ℞ 16 7/8 ✠ A 21 3/7; 
and subtracting ℞ 16 7/8 ✠ 19 A in common, there will remain an equation between ℞ 2 1/8 and A 2 3/7; and if it be made as A 2 3/7 to ℞ 2 1/8, so A 1 to another number, there will come out ℞ 119/136, the value of one A, 
and therefore ℞ 1 119/136, that is ℞ 255/136, will be equal to the absolute number 15, namely the aggregate of the two heavy bodies :g:`C`, :g:`E`, that is ℞ 1 ✠ A 1. Dividing therefore 15 by the fraction 255/136, there comes out the absolute number 2040/255, 
that is 8, the value of one root, and therefore heavy body :g:`E`, which was put as ℞ 1, will be 8; and :g:`G` will be 9. Next, because (as we said above) weight :g:`D` equals the two :g:`E`, :g:`C`, that is 15, :g:`E` will be 8 and :g:`C` 7, 
wherefore A 1 is 7; therefore :g:`A`, namely A 1 3/7, will be 10: which being so, :g:`GH` to :g:`HA` will be as weight :g:`A` 10 to weight :g:`G` 9; :g:`CD` to :g:`DE` as weight :g:`E` 8 to weight :g:`C` 7; 
and finally :g:`FI` to :g:`IB` as :g:`B` 17 to weight :g:`F` 17, which &c.

XX. Lastly. If the given ratios be :g:`GH` to :g:`HA`, :g:`FI` to :g:`IB`, and :g:`ED` to :g:`DC`, 
the figure being supposed as has been said, this case will be similar to the preceding XIX., and so we shall disclose the remaining three by a like artifice.
