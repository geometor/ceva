Proposition XX: Twenty Combinatorial Cases of Element V
=======================================================

.. folio:: 0044
   :page: p. 27

.. picture:: 0105.g22.png
   :alt: 1678 copperplate Tabula II Fig. 22: Element V quadrilateral cases

.. picture:: 0105.g23.png
   :alt: 1678 copperplate Tabula II Fig. 23: Case XIX algebraic solution with cossic notation

**Proposition XX · Problem XI** (pp. 27–29, `Tab. 2, Figs. 22–23`)

*In the Fifth Element (quadrilateral with opposite sides joined by two intersecting lines :g:`BG`, :g:`ED` meeting at :g:`F`), given any three of the six independent segment ratios, to determine the remaining three.*

Ceva systematically resolves all **20 combinatorial cases** ($\binom{6}{3} = 20$) for the four-weight quadrilateral.

**Case XIX — Cossic Algebra (*per secundas radices*)** (p. 29, folio ``0046``, `Tab. 2, Fig. 23`): When three non-adjacent ratios produce a quadratic constraint on the unknown vertex weight, Ceva explicitly writes out a full Renaissance cossic equation using the radical/unknown symbol ``℞`` (*res* / *radix*) alongside his addition symbol ``✠`` and solves it explicitly by *"second roots"* (*per secundas radices*) — demonstrating a remarkable fusion of Italian cossic algebra with synthetic statics.
