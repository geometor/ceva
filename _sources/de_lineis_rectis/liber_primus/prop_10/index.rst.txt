**Proposition X · Problem VIII** (`Tab. 1, Fig. 5`)

*The same figure of the Second Element being set out, and the ratios* :g:`DA` *to* :g:`AB`, *and* :g:`DE` *to* :g:`BC` *being given, it is required to make known the ratio* :g:`CK` *to* :g:`KE`.*

Let the ratio :g:`DA` to :g:`AB` be as 2 to 3, and :g:`DE` to :g:`BC` as 5 to 4; 
because the proportion :g:`CK` to :g:`KE` is compounded of the ratios of weights :g:`E` to :g:`A` to :g:`C`, namely of the straight lines :g:`AD` to :g:`DE`, and :g:`CB` to :g:`BA`; from these ratios is also compounded the rectangle of :g:`AD` into :g:`CB` to the rectangle of :g:`DE` into :g:`BA`; 
and these rectangles are indeed compounded also of the two ratios :g:`DA` to :g:`BA`, and :g:`CB` to :g:`DE`, that is, of the two 2 to 3, and 4 to 5; therefore :g:`DA` to :g:`AB` will be as 8 to 15, 
namely as the product of :g:`DA` into :g:`CB` to the product of :g:`BA` into :g:`DE`, which was to be shown.

*The same geometrically from the aforesaid* :person:`Pietro Paolo Caravaggio Jr. <caravaggio>`.

*Of which parts* :g:`ED` *is 2, let* :g:`CB` *be 5; and of which* :g:`DA` *is 1, let* :g:`AB` *be 3.* 
*I say that* :g:`CK` *to* :g:`KE` *has the same ratio which the rectangle of* :g:`AD` *into* :g:`CB` *has to the rectangle of* :g:`ED` *into* :g:`EB`, *namely as 5 to 6.*

For since as :g:`AD` is to :g:`DE`, so is triangle :g:`ACD` to triangle :g:`DCE`, 
and as :g:`AD` is to :g:`DE`, so is triangle :g:`AFD` to triangle :g:`DFE`; therefore as :g:`AD` is to :g:`DE`, so will triangle :g:`ACF` be to triangle :g:`FCE`. Similarly, as :g:`CB` is to :g:`BA`, so will triangle :g:`EFC` be to triangle :g:`EFA`; 
therefore triangle :g:`AFC` to triangle :g:`AFE` has the ratio compounded of :g:`AD` to :g:`DE`, and :g:`CB` to :g:`BA`; but as triangle :g:`AFC` is to triangle :g:`AFE`, so is :g:`CK` to :g:`KE`; therefore :g:`CK` to :g:`KE` has the ratio compounded

of :g:`AD` to :g:`DE`, and :g:`CB` to :g:`BA`; but of the same ratios is compounded the ratio which the rectangle of :g:`AD` into :g:`BC` has to the rectangle of :g:`DE` into :g:`BA`; therefore as the rectangle of :g:`AD` into :g:`BC` is to the rectangle of :g:`DE` into :g:`BA`, 
so is :g:`CK` to :g:`KE`, which was to be shown.

*I myself accomplished the same geometrically when Caravaggio's solution had not yet been imparted to me, having premised the following lemma.*

**Lemma V** (`Tab. 2, Fig. 13`)

*Let there be a triangle* :g:`ABD`, *in which lines* :g:`DGI`, :g:`BGH`, :g:`AGC` *intersect one another at* :g:`G`; *and from point* :g:`A` *let* :g:`FE` *be drawn parallel to* :g:`BD` *meeting the lines* :g:`CIF` *and* :g:`CHE` *at points* :g:`F` *and* :g:`E`; *I say that the straight line* :g:`FA` *is equal to* :g:`AE`.*

Let :g:`BH` and :g:`DI` be produced until they meet the line :g:`LK` at :g:`K` and :g:`L`. Since :g:`DC` to :g:`LA` is as :g:`CG` to :g:`GA`, and as :g:`CG` is to :g:`GA`, so is :g:`BC` to :g:`AK`, it will be as :g:`DC` to :g:`LA`, so :g:`BC` to :g:`AK`, 
and alternating, :g:`DC` to :g:`CB` as :g:`LA` to :g:`AK`. Again, because :g:`CD` to :g:`AE` is as :g:`CH` to :g:`HE`, and as :g:`CH` is to :g:`HE`, so is :g:`BC` to :g:`EK`, :g:`CD` to :g:`AE` will be as :g:`BC` to :g:`EK`; and alternating, :g:`CD` to :g:`CB` will be as :g:`AE` to :g:`EK`: 
finally, since similarly :g:`CD` to :g:`CB` is as :g:`LF` to :g:`FA`, the three ratios :g:`LA` to :g:`AK`, :g:`AE` to :g:`EK`, and :g:`LF` to :g:`FA` will be similar to the same :g:`CD` to :g:`CB`, and therefore also to one another; thus since as :g:`LF` is to :g:`FA`, so is :g:`AE` to :g:`EK`, 
and by composition as :g:`LA` to :g:`AF`, so :g:`AK` to :g:`EK`, by alternation it will be as :g:`LA` to :g:`AK`, so :g:`AF` to :g:`EK`; 
but as :g:`LA` to :g:`AK`, so is also :g:`AE` to :g:`EK`; therefore as :g:`AF` is to :g:`EK`, so :g:`AE` to the same :g:`EK`; therefore :g:`FA` and :g:`AE` are equal, which was to be shown.

*Let triangle* :g:`AHE` *be given, and in it let lines* :g:`EK`, :g:`FA`, :g:`HD` *intersect one another at* :g:`I`; 
*and then, the ratios* :g:`AK` *to* :g:`AD` *and* :g:`KH` *to* :g:`DE` *being given in numbers, let it be required to investigate the ratio* :g:`EF` *to* :g:`FH`.* (`Tab. 2, Fig. 14`)

Let a straight line :g:`BC` be drawn from point :g:`A` parallel to :g:`HE`; 
and let the straight lines :g:`FB`, :g:`FC` drawn from point :g:`F` through :g:`K`, :g:`D` cut :g:`BC` at points :g:`B`, :g:`C`, and finally from :g:`A` let the straight line :g:`AG` be drawn parallel to :g:`CF` meeting :g:`EH` produced at :g:`G`.

Since :g:`GA` is parallel to :g:`FC`, and :g:`AC` is parallel to :g:`GF`, :g:`AF` will be a parallelogram, and :g:`GF` will be equal to :g:`AC`, that is, to :g:`AB`; since

therefore the ratio :g:`EF` to :g:`FH` is compounded of the two ratios :g:`EF` to :g:`FG`, and :g:`FG` to :g:`FH`, 
and as :g:`EF` is to :g:`FG`, so is :g:`ED` to :g:`DA`, and as :g:`FG`, that is :g:`AB`, is to :g:`FH`, so is :g:`AK` to :g:`KH`, the ratio :g:`EF` to :g:`FH` will be compounded of the two ratios :g:`ED` to :g:`DA`, and :g:`AK` to :g:`KH`; 
but of the same is compounded the ratio of the rectangle of :g:`AK` into :g:`DE` to the rectangle of :g:`KH` into :g:`AD`, or of these other two :g:`KA` to :g:`AD`, and :g:`DE` to :g:`HK`; 
therefore :g:`EF` to :g:`FH` is also compounded of the same ratios :g:`KA` to :g:`AD`, and :g:`DE` to :g:`KH`, which since they are given, the ratio :g:`EF` to :g:`FH` will be manifest, which was to be shown.
