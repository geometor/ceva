Proposition VII & Lemma IV: Pyramid Ratios and Compound Proportions
===================================================================

.. folio:: 0027
   :page: p. 10

.. picture:: 0103.g11.png
   :alt: 1678 copperplate Tabula I Fig. 11: Triangle area comparison

.. picture:: 0103.g12.png
   :alt: 1678 copperplate Tabula I Fig. 12: Pyramid ratio theorem

**Proposition VII · Theorem I** (pp. 10–11, `Tab. 1, Fig. 12`): *In Element I (triangle :g:`AEC` with intersecting lines :g:`EB`, :g:`CD` meeting at :g:`F`), the ratio of segment :g:`EF` to :g:`FB` equals the ratio of the triangular pyramid (or solid) having base $\triangle AEC$ and height $CB$ to the solid having base $\triangle EFD$ and height $AB$ — equivalently, expressing the segment ratio via compounded triangle and rectangle ratios.*

Ceva cites :person:`Federico Commandino's <commandino>` commentary on :person:`Euclid <euclid>` (:just:`VI.23`) for compounding ratios of parallelograms and solids, and adds a **Corollary** translating the solid proportion into planar rectangles.

**Lemma IV** (pp. 11–12): *Compound ratio permutation* (:term:`ratio composita <ratio-composita>`): citing :person:`Christoph Clavius <clavius>` (:cite:`1589 <clavius:1589:euclides>`, *Elements* VIII.19), Ceva proves that if four magnitudes satisfy a compounded proportion, permuting factors preserves the compound ratio—the algebraic engine used throughout the rest of Book I to convert static weight ratios into pure Euclidean rectangles and triangles.
