Proposition VII & Lemma IV: Pyramid Ratios and Compound Proportions
===================================================================

.. folio:: 0027
   :page: p. 10

.. picture:: 0103.g11.png
   :alt: 1678 copperplate Tabula I Fig. 11: Triangle area comparison

.. picture:: 0103.g12.png
   :alt: 1678 copperplate Tabula I Fig. 12: Pyramid ratio theorem

**Proposition VII · Theorem I.**

*Let straight line* :g:`DB` *cut triangle* :g:`EAC` *in any way so that triangle* :g:`DAB` *is formed, and let* :g:`BE`, :g:`DC` *be joined cutting one another at* :g:`F`; 
*I say that* :g:`DF` *has to* :g:`FC` *the ratio which the pyramid whose base is triangle* :g:`ABD` *and altitude* :g:`EF` *has to the pyramid whose base is triangle* :g:`AEC` *and altitude* :g:`FB`.

(`Tab. 1, Fig. 12`) Since the ratio of weight :g:`C` to :g:`D`, namely of straight line :g:`DF` to :g:`FC`, is compounded of the ratios of weights :g:`C` to :g:`B`, :g:`B` to :g:`E`, and :g:`E` to :g:`D`, namely of straight lines :g:`AB` to :g:`AC`, :g:`EF` to :g:`FB`, and :g:`AD` to :g:`AE`: 
and from the same ratios is compounded the ratio of the parallelepiped contained by the rectangle :g:`AB` by :g:`EF` as base and altitude :g:`DA`, to the parallelepiped contained by the rectangle :g:`AC` by :g:`FB` as base and altitude :g:`AE`; 
therefore :g:`DF` to :g:`FC` is as the parallelepiped

.. folio:: 0028
   :page: p. 11

formed from rectangle :g:`AB` by :g:`EF` with altitude :g:`DA`, to the parallelepiped from rectangle :g:`AC` by :g:`FB` with altitude :g:`AE`, 
or as the parallelepiped contained by rectangle :g:`DAB` with altitude :g:`EF`, to the parallelepiped contained by rectangle :g:`CAE` and altitude :g:`FB`: now the ratio of these two parallelepipeds is compounded of the ratio which rectangle :g:`DAB`, the base of the one, has to rectangle :g:`CAE`, the base of the other, 
and of altitude :g:`EF` to altitude :g:`FB`; but rectangle :g:`DAB` to rectangle :g:`CAE` is compounded of the ratios of straight lines :g:`AB` to :g:`AC` and :g:`DA` to :g:`AE`, of which is also compounded triangle :g:`DAB` to triangle :g:`AEC`, since angle :g:`A` at the vertex is common; 
therefore :g:`DF` to :g:`FC` is compounded of the ratios of triangle :g:`DAB` to triangle :g:`EAC` and of straight line :g:`EF` to :g:`FB`; that is, :g:`DF` to :g:`FC` is in the ratio in which is the pyramid whose base is triangle :g:`DAB` and altitude :g:`EF`, to the pyramid 
whose base is triangle :g:`AEC` and altitude :g:`FB`, which was to be proved (:person:`Federico Commandino <commandino>` on Euclid :just:`VI.23`).

**Corollary.**

*Hence it is clear that if* :g:`EF` *be equal to* :g:`FB`, *triangle* :g:`DAB` *is to triangle* :g:`AEC` *as straight line* :g:`DF` *is to* :g:`FC`. 
For since straight lines :g:`EF`, :g:`FB` are equal altitudes of the aforesaid pyramids, they will therefore be to one another as their bases.

**Lemma IV.**

*If any proportion be compounded of several ratios in sequence, then by permuting the order of the antecedents, or of the consequents, or even of both,* 
*so that just as many other ratios are formed, those ratios will compound the same former ratio.*

.. code-block:: text

   A C E G ✠ K M   ::   O
   B D F H I L N   ::   P

   A ✠ K E C G M   ::   Q
   N D H L F I B   ::   R

Let there be set out as many ratios as you please, say seven: :g:`A` to :g:`B`, :g:`C` to :g:`D`, :g:`E` to :g:`F`, :g:`G` to :g:`H`, ``✠`` to :g:`I`, :g:`K` to :g:`L`, and :g:`M` to :g:`N`: and let the ratio compounded from them be :g:`O` to :g:`P`. 
Now let the order of the antecedents

.. folio:: 0029
   :page: p. 12

``A C E G ✠ K M`` be permuted among themselves in any way, so that it becomes ``A ✠ K E C G M``; 
or the order of the consequents ``B D F H I L N``, so that it becomes ``N D H L F I B``. I say that if from the ratios :g:`A` to :g:`N`, ``✠`` to :g:`D`, :g:`K` to :g:`H`, :g:`E` to :g:`L`, 
:g:`C` to :g:`F`, :g:`G` to :g:`I`, and :g:`M` to :g:`B` there be made the ratio :g:`Q` to :g:`R`, this is similar to the former :g:`O` to :g:`P` (:person:`Christoph Clavius <clavius>` on Euclid :just:`VIII.19`).

For from the multiplication of the sides ``A C E G ✠ K M``, 
or of ``A ✠ K E C G M`` themselves, the same product always arises; and equally the same product which arises from multiplying the sides ``B D F H I L N`` arises also from the multiplication of the sides ``N D H L F I B``; 
therefore the ratio of the product of the former antecedents to the product of the former consequents will be altogether the same as that of the product of the latter, or permuted, antecedents to the product of the latter consequents; 
but as :g:`O` to :g:`P`, so is the product from the antecedents of the first order to the product of the consequents of the same first order; 
and likewise as :g:`Q` to :g:`R`, so is the product from the antecedents of the second order to the product of the consequents of the same order; therefore if those two ratios of products (as I have shown) are similar to one another, 
it is necessary that the two ratios :g:`O` to :g:`P` and :g:`Q` to :g:`R` are also similar, which was to be proved.
