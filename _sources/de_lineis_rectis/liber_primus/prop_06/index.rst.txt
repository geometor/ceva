**Lemma III.**

(`Tab. 1, Fig. 9`) Let :math:`B` be the center of gravity of weights :math:`A, C`. I say that weight :math:`B`—namely the aggregate of the heavy bodies :math:`A, C` weighing at :math:`B`—is to weight :math:`C` as :math:`AC` to :math:`BA`.
Since :math:`B` is the center of gravity of weights :math:`A, C`, :math:`A` will be to :math:`C` as :math:`CB` to :math:`BA`; therefore by compounding, weight :math:`B` will be to :math:`C` as :math:`CA` to :math:`BA`, which &c.

**Proposition VI · Problem VI**

*Having set forth the figure* :g:`A` :g:`E` :g:`C` :g:`F` *of the First Element,*
*let weights* :g:`A`, :g:`E`, :g:`C` *be understood as disposed at* :g:`A`, :g:`E`, :g:`C` *in that manner in which the weights* :math:`G, I, H` *were set forth there, so that* :g:`D` *is the center of gravity of weights* :g:`A`, :g:`E`, :g:`B` *of weights* :g:`A`, :g:`C`, *and* :g:`F` *of all;*
*therefore there will be four ratios,* :math:`AB` *to* :math:`BC`, :math:`AD` *to* :math:`DE`, :math:`EF` *to* :math:`FB`, *and* :math:`CF` *to* :math:`FD`, *of which if any two be given in numbers, we shall investigate the remaining two.*

Of this problem there are six cases; for (as from the combinatorial art)
multiplying four (that is, how many ratios are given) by three, namely the number deficient by unity, there arises :math:`12`, half of which, :math:`6`, furnishes us the number of pairs into which the aforesaid number :math:`4` can be distributed.

.. folio:: 0025
   :page: p. 8

(`Tab. 1, Fig. 4`) Let there be given first two ratios :math:`EF` to :math:`FB` and :math:`CF` to :math:`FD`, let the former be as :math:`2` to :math:`1`, and the latter as :math:`3` to :math:`2`; we must now make manifest the remaining two ratios :math:`CB` to :math:`BA` and :math:`ED` to :math:`DA`.

Since weight :g:`B` (namely the aggregate of :g:`A`, :g:`C`) is to :g:`C` (by the premised Third Lemma) as straight line :math:`AC` to :math:`AB`, while the ratio of weight :g:`B` to :g:`C` is compounded of the ratios of weights :g:`B` to :g:`F` and :g:`F` to :g:`C`,
and as :g:`B` to :g:`F`, so :math:`EF` to :math:`EB`, and as :g:`F` to :g:`C`, so :math:`DC` to :math:`DF`, the ratio :math:`AC` to :math:`AB` will be compounded of the ratios :math:`EF` to :math:`EB` and :math:`DC` to :math:`DF`; and since :math:`EF` to :math:`FB` is as :math:`2` to :math:`1`, by compounding, then by conversion of ratio,
and by converting, :math:`EF` to :math:`EB` is as :math:`2` to :math:`3`; and :math:`CF` to :math:`FD` was also as :math:`3` to :math:`2`, and by compounding :math:`CD` to :math:`FD` as :math:`5` to :math:`2`; therefore straight line :math:`AC` to :math:`AB` will be compounded of the ratios :math:`2` to :math:`3` and :math:`5` to :math:`2`,
or of these, :math:`2` to :math:`3` and :math:`3` to :math:`1\tfrac{1}{5}`; that is, the same :math:`AC` to :math:`AB` as :math:`2` to :math:`1\tfrac{1}{5}`, or as :math:`10` to :math:`6`; wherefore by dividing, :math:`CB` to :math:`BA` will be as :math:`4` to :math:`6`, or as :math:`2` to :math:`3`.
By the same reason, since weight :g:`D` to :g:`E` is compounded of the ratios of weights :g:`D` to :g:`F` and :g:`F` to :g:`E`, namely of straight lines :math:`CF` to :math:`CD` and :math:`EB` to :math:`BF`, the same weight :g:`D` to :g:`E`, that is, :math:`AE` to :math:`DA`, will be compounded
of the ratios :math:`3` to :math:`5` and :math:`3` to :math:`1`: and of these ratios is compounded that :math:`3` to :math:`1\tfrac{2}{3}`, that is, :math:`9` to :math:`5`; therefore :math:`AE` to :math:`DA` is as :math:`9` to :math:`5`,
but by dividing, :math:`ED` to :math:`DA` will be as :math:`4` to :math:`5`; therefore :math:`CB` to :math:`BA` is as :math:`2` to :math:`3`, and :math:`ED` to :math:`DA` as :math:`4` to :math:`5`, which was to be done.

II. Let two ratios be given, :math:`EF` to :math:`FB` as :math:`4` to :math:`5`,
and :math:`ED` to :math:`DA` as :math:`7` to :math:`9`; we must trace out the remaining ratios :math:`CF` to :math:`FD` and :math:`CB` to :math:`BA`.

The ratio of straight line :math:`AC` to :math:`CB`—namely of weight :g:`B` to :g:`A`—is compounded of the ratios of weights :g:`B` to :g:`E` and :g:`E` to :g:`A`,
namely of straight lines :math:`EF` to :math:`FB` and :math:`AD` to :math:`DE`, that is, of the ratios :math:`4` to :math:`5` and :math:`9` to :math:`7`; wherefore :math:`AC` to :math:`CB` is compounded of the ratios :math:`4` to :math:`5` and :math:`9` to :math:`7`: now this compounded ratio is that which :math:`4` has to :math:`3\tfrac{8}{9}`,
or :math:`36` to :math:`35`; therefore :math:`AC` to :math:`CB` is as :math:`36` to :math:`35`, and by dividing, :math:`AB` to :math:`BC` as :math:`1` to :math:`35`.

Similarly, since :math:`CD` to :math:`CF`—that is, weight :g:`F` to :g:`D`—is compounded of the ratios of weights :g:`F` to :g:`E` and :g:`E` to :g:`D`,
namely of straight lines :math:`EB` to :math:`BF` and :math:`DA` to :math:`AE`, that is, of the ratios :math:`9` to :math:`5` and :math:`9` to :math:`16`; now this ratio is the same as that which :math:`9` has to :math:`8\tfrac{8}{9}`, or as :math:`81` to :math:`80`;
by dividing, :math:`DF` to :math:`FC` will be as :math:`1` to :math:`80`.

III. But if two ratios be given, :math:`CF` to :math:`FD` as :math:`4` to :math:`5`, and :math:`CB` to :math:`BA` as :math:`7` to :math:`9`, we shall likewise give, in the same way as above, :math:`AD`

.. folio:: 0026
   :page: p. 9

to :math:`DE` as :math:`1` to :math:`35`; and :math:`BF` to :math:`FE` as :math:`1` to :math:`80`.

IV. Let the ratio :math:`ED` to :math:`DA` be as :math:`20` to :math:`21`, and :math:`CB` to :math:`BA` as :math:`99` to :math:`25`, and let it be required to make known the two ratios :math:`EF` to :math:`FB` and :math:`CF` to :math:`FD`.
The ratio of straight line :math:`CF` to :math:`FD`—namely of weight :g:`D` to :g:`C`—is compounded of the ratios of weights :g:`D` to :g:`A` and :g:`A` to :g:`C`, that is, of straight lines :math:`EA` to :math:`ED` and :math:`CB` to :math:`BA`, namely of the ratios :math:`41` to :math:`20` and :math:`99` to :math:`25`:
but :math:`41` to :math:`5\tfrac{5}{99}` is compounded of the same ratios; therefore :math:`CF` to :math:`DF` is as :math:`41` to :math:`5\tfrac{5}{99}`, that is, as :math:`4059` to :math:`500`.

By the same reason, since :math:`EF` to :math:`FB`—that is, weight :g:`B` to :g:`E`—is compounded of the ratios of weights :g:`B` to :g:`A`
and :g:`A` to :g:`E`, namely of the lines :math:`CA` to :math:`CB` and :math:`ED` to :math:`DA`, that is, of the ratios :math:`124` to :math:`99` and :math:`20` to :math:`21`; and since of the same ratios is compounded the ratio which :math:`124` has to :math:`103\tfrac{19}{20}`, that is, as :math:`2480` to :math:`2079`,
:math:`EF` to :math:`FB` will be in the same ratio.

V. Let two proportions be given, :math:`ED` to :math:`DA` as :math:`2` to :math:`1`, and :math:`CF` to :math:`FD` of equality; we must investigate the remaining two, namely :math:`CB` to :math:`BA` and :math:`EF` to :math:`FB`.
Compounded is :math:`CB` to :math:`BA`—doubtless weight :g:`A` to :g:`C`—of the ratios of weights :g:`A` to :g:`D` and :g:`D` to :g:`C`, namely of straight lines :math:`ED` to :math:`EA` and :math:`CF` to :math:`FD`;
that is, of the ratios :math:`2` to :math:`3` and :math:`3` to :math:`3`; therefore :math:`CB` to :math:`BA` is as :math:`2` to :math:`3`.

Similarly :math:`EB` to :math:`FB`—that is, the ratio of weight :g:`F` to :g:`E`—is compounded of the ratios of weights :g:`F` to :g:`D`, :g:`D` to :g:`E`, namely of straight lines
:math:`CD` to :math:`CF` and :math:`AE` to :math:`AD`, that is, of the ratios :math:`2` to :math:`1` and :math:`3` to :math:`1`; but of the same ratios is compounded :math:`2` to :math:`\tfrac{1}{3}`, or :math:`6` to :math:`1`; therefore :math:`EB` to :math:`BF` is as :math:`6` to :math:`1`,
and by dividing, :math:`EF` to :math:`FB` as :math:`5` to :math:`1`.

VI. And lastly. If the given proportions be :math:`CB` to :math:`BA` as :math:`2` to :math:`1`, and :math:`EF` to :math:`FB` of equality, we shall show (as was done above) :math:`ED` to :math:`DA` as :math:`2` to :math:`3`,
and :math:`CF` to :math:`FD` as :math:`5` to :math:`1`.

**Scholium.**

*As often as a problem is impossible, it will be discerned from the operation itself; for if the ratio* :math:`DF` *to* :math:`FC` *be given as a ratio of equality, and likewise* :math:`BF` *to* :math:`FE` *of equality, I say that the question is impossible.*
*For since weight* :g:`C` *to* :g:`B`, *that is, straight line* :math:`AB` *to* :math:`AC`, *is compounded of the ratios of weights* :g:`C` *to* :g:`F` *and* :g:`F` *to* :g:`B`, *namely of straight lines* :math:`DF` *to* :math:`DC` *and* :math:`EB` *to* :math:`EF`, *that is, of the ratios* :math:`1` *to* :math:`2` *and* :math:`2` *to* :math:`1`,
*the part* :math:`AB` *to the whole* :math:`AC` *will be as* :math:`1` *to* :math:`1`, *the part equal to the whole, which is absurd. This, or a not dissimilar absurdity, always*

.. folio:: 0027
   :page: p. 10

*follows whenever the proposed question is impossible.*

*The first part of this proposition, or the first case, whose title I had transmitted to my friend without adding or indicating any solution, was demonstrated geometrically by a noble youth, conspicuous for his multitude of languages, arts, and sciences,*
*Pietro Paolo Caravaggio, son of my teacher Pietro Paolo. I set down his demonstration, which will place this static method in a geometric light.*

*Let* :math:`EF` *to* :math:`FB` *be as* :math:`2` *to* :math:`1`, *and* :math:`CF` *to* :math:`FD` *as* :math:`3` *to* :math:`2`; *I say that* :math:`ED` *to* :math:`DA` *is as* :math:`4` *to* :math:`5`, *and* :math:`CB` *to* :math:`BA` *as* :math:`2` *to* :math:`3`.*

(`Tab. 1, Fig. 9`, emend. `Fig. 10`) Let :g:`GH` be drawn parallel to :g:`EC`.
Since :g:`EC` to :math:`GF` is as :math:`CD` to :math:`DF`, namely as :math:`5` to :math:`2`, and :g:`EC` to :math:`FH` as :math:`15` to :math:`5`, :g:`EC` to :g:`GH` will be as :math:`15` to :math:`11`; and equally :g:`AE` to :math:`AG` and :g:`AC` to :math:`AH` as :math:`15` to :math:`11`;
therefore of those parts of which :g:`AE` is :math:`15`, :math:`GE` will be :math:`4`, and likewise of those parts of which :g:`AC` is :math:`15`, :math:`HC` will be :math:`4`; since therefore :math:`GE` to :math:`ED` is as :math:`CF` to :math:`CD`, namely as :math:`3` to :math:`5`, if as :math:`3` to :math:`5`,
so :math:`4` be made to another number, the number :math:`\tfrac{20}{3}` will come forth for :math:`ED`, whose remainder from :math:`EA` (:math:`15`) will be :math:`\tfrac{25}{3}`; therefore :math:`ED` to :math:`DA` will be as :math:`4` to :math:`5`.

Similarly since, of those parts of which :g:`AC` is :math:`15`, :math:`CH` is :math:`4`, and :math:`CH` to :math:`CB` is as :math:`2` to :math:`3`, :math:`CB` will be :math:`6`, and :math:`BA` :math:`9`: wherefore :math:`CB` to :math:`BA` will be as :math:`2` to :math:`3`.
