**Proposition XXIII · Theorem XII — The Seven-Line Spatial Theorem** (pp. 31–32, `Tab. 3, Fig. 28`)

*In any solid tetrahedron (triangular pyramid) :g:`AEGC`, suspend four arbitrary non-zero weights :g:`R` at :g:`A`, :g:`S` at :g:`E`, :g:`T` at :g:`C`, and :g:`Q` at :g:`G`. Let :g:`B, D, P, N, L, I` be the six static lever fulcrums on the six edges, and let :g:`H, K, M, F` be the four static centers of gravity of the four triangular faces.*

By *Axiom I–II*, the unique spatial center of gravity :g:`O` of all four masses can be reached by grouping the four vertices in **seven distinct associative ways**:

1. **Four Vertex-to-Opposite-Face Cevians**: :g:`AK`, :g:`EM`, :g:`CH`, and :g:`GF` (joining each vertex to the static center of the opposite triangular face);
2. **Three Skew-Edge Bimedians**: :g:`PL`, :g:`DN`, and :g:`BI` (joining the static fulcrums of each pair of opposite skew edges).

All **seven** spatial straight lines pass simultaneously through the exact same unique point :g:`O` (:term:`mira quadam concordia <concordia-septem-linearum>`), generalizing :person:`Federico Commandino's <commandino>` equal-weight $3:1$ centroid theorem (:cite:`1565 <commandino:1565:centro>`) to arbitrary weighted tetrahedra!
