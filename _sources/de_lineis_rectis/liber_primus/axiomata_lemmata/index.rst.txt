**Static Construction of Straight Lines Mutually Intersecting — Book I**

**Axiom I.** *Heavy bodies suspended from their common* :term:`center of gravity <centrum-gravitatis>` *weigh just as if their whole weight were in the aforesaid center of gravity.*

**Axiom II.** *Weights in the same position have a single center of gravity.*

**Postulate.** *Let it be granted that, any weight being proposed, another can be found to which the given weight has any commanded ratio.*

**Lemma I.** *Given several weights in any position, if from the center of gravity of one or more of them a balance-beam be drawn which passes through the center of gravity of all, that beam produced will pass through the center of gravity of the remaining weight.*

(`Tab. 1, Fig. 1`) Let the weights be :math:`A, B, C, D`, let the center of gravity of them all be :math:`E`, and that of weights :math:`A, D` be :math:`F`, and let :math:`FE` be drawn; I say that if it be produced toward the parts of the remaining weights :math:`B, C`, it will fall upon their center;
for if this is not so, let :math:`G` be the center of gravity of weights :math:`B, C`, so that the join :math:`FG` lies outside the line :math:`FE`. Since therefore :math:`FG` joins the centers of gravity of weights :math:`B, C` and :math:`A, D`, if it be made as :math:`BC` to :math:`AD`,
so the length :math:`FM` to :math:`MG`, the center of gravity of the same four weights :math:`B, C, D, A` remaining in their former position will lie on the same balance-beam :math:`FG`; and since this center is unique, it will not be :math:`E`, as was supposed.

**Corollary.** *Hence it is manifest that if two heavy bodies* :math:`B, C` *be joined by some balance-beam* :math:`BC`, *their center of gravity will lie in the common*

(`Tab. 1, Fig. 2`) *intersection* :math:`H` *of the balance-beam* :math:`BC` *joining the centers of the heavy bodies* :math:`B, C`,
*and the line* :math:`FH` *passing through the aforesaid centers* :math:`F` *and* :math:`E`. *For the center of the heavy bodies* :math:`B, C` *lies on* :math:`FH`, *as has been proved, as well as on* :math:`BC`; *therefore it will be* :math:`H`.

**Lemma II.**

*Let there be two weights* :g:`A` *and* :g:`B`, *whose center of gravity is* :g:`F` *on the balance-beam* :g:`AB`; *I say that weight* :g:`A` *is to* :g:`B` *as* :g:`BF <FB>` *to* :g:`FA <AF>`.

(`Tab. 1, Fig. 3`; :cite:`Archimedes, Eq. I.6 <archimedes:equipond>`) For if it is not so, let it be as :g:`A` to :g:`B`, so :math:`BK` to :math:`KA`; therefore :g:`K` will be the center of gravity of weights :g:`A` and :g:`B`; and since this center is unique, it will not be :g:`F`, as was supposed.
