Proposition XXVI: Concurrent Lines in a Double Pyramid
======================================================

.. folio:: 0051
   :page: p. 34

.. picture:: 0107.g31.svg
   :alt: Calibrated interactive SVG — Ceva 1678 Tabula III Fig. 31: Prop. XXVI

.. picture:: 0107.g31.png
   :alt: 1678 copperplate Tabula III Fig. 31: Double opposite pyramid (bipyramid) concurrency

**Proposition XXVI · Theorem XV** (p. 34, `Tab. 3, Fig. 31`)

*Let there be a pyramid whose vertex is* :g:`H`, *and whose base is triangle* :g:`AEC`; *and with the planes of triangles* :g:`AHE`, :g:`EHC`, :g:`CHA` *produced beyond point* :g:`H`, *let them be cut by another plane; and let the common sections of these planes be lines* :g:`AHI`, :g:`CHN`, :g:`EHL`, :g:`NL`, :g:`NI`, :g:`IL`;
*and with a figure of the Second Element* :g:`AFEDCBG` *constructed on the base of the proposed pyramid, let lines* :g:`FH`, :g:`DH`, :g:`BH` *be joined, which produced meet the sides of the opposite triangle; thus let them meet at points* :g:`K`, :g:`M`, :g:`O` *in order, and let lines* :g:`NK`, :g:`IM`, :g:`LO` *be joined;*
*I say that these intersect one another at the same point* :g:`P`, *and moreover, if* :g:`GH` *being joined be produced, it meets the opposite triangle at the aforesaid point* :g:`P`. *Now that this may be made manifest, let us suspend from the angles of the two triangles* :g:`NLI`, :g:`AEC` *in the reciprocal ratio of the lengths,*
*the weights* :g:`Q`, :g:`S`, :g:`R`, :g:`X`, :g:`V`, :g:`T`, *which will so most beautifully correspond to one another that one to one, or two to one, or two to two, or three to three, are among themselves reciprocally as the lengths from which they hang.*

(`Tab. 3, Fig. 31`) Let it be made as :g:`ED` to :g:`DC`, so weight :g:`R` to :g:`S`; as :g:`AF` to :g:`FE`, so :g:`S` to :g:`Q`; as :g:`NH` to :g:`HC`, so :g:`R` to :g:`X`; as :g:`LH` to :g:`HE`, so :g:`S` to :g:`V`; and as :g:`IH` to :g:`HA`, so :g:`Q` to :g:`T`;
and let :g:`R` be at :g:`C`, :g:`S` at :g:`E`, :g:`Q` at :g:`A`, :g:`T` at :g:`I`, :g:`V` at :g:`L`, and :g:`X` at :g:`N`;
since therefore :g:`ED` to :g:`DC` is as :g:`R` to :g:`S`, and :g:`AF` to :g:`FE` as :g:`S` to :g:`Q`, :g:`G` will be the center of heavy bodies :g:`Q`, :g:`S`, :g:`R`, and :g:`B` the center of :g:`Q`, :g:`R`.
And because :g:`IH` to :g:`HA` is as :g:`Q` to :g:`T`, :g:`AB` to :g:`BC` as :g:`R` to :g:`Q` (since :g:`B` is the center of heavy bodies :g:`Q`, :g:`R`), and :g:`NH` to :g:`HC` as :g:`R` to :g:`X`,
by the fourth problem, point :g:`O` will be the center of heavy bodies :g:`X`, :g:`T`. By the same reason, because :g:`F` is the center of gravity of heavy bodies :g:`Q`, :g:`S`, and :g:`H` of heavy bodies :g:`Q`, :g:`T`, and :g:`S`, :g:`V`,
:g:`K` will be the center of heavy bodies :g:`V`, :g:`T`. Likewise, since :g:`D` is the center of heavy bodies :g:`S`, :g:`R`, and :g:`H` of :g:`R`, :g:`X` and :g:`S`, :g:`V`, :g:`M` will be the center of heavy bodies :g:`X`, :g:`V`,
and therefore the joined lines :g:`NK`, :g:`IM`, :g:`LO` will intersect one another at the same point :g:`P`, and that will be the center of heavy bodies :g:`X`, :g:`V`, :g:`T`; now point :g:`H` is the center of heavy bodies :g:`S`, :g:`V`, :g:`R`, :g:`X`, :g:`Q`, :g:`T`, namely of all :g:`S`, :g:`V`, :g:`R`, :g:`X`, :g:`Q`, :g:`T`,
and :g:`G` the center of heavy bodies :g:`Q`, :g:`S`, :g:`R`; (`by Lem. 1`) therefore the balance-beam :g:`GH` produced will pass through :g:`P`, the center of the remaining heavy bodies :g:`X`, :g:`V`, :g:`T`;
and :g:`GH` to :g:`HP` will be as the three heavy bodies :g:`X`, :g:`V`, :g:`T` to the three :g:`Q`, :g:`S`, :g:`R`, and so concerning the other enunciated ratios.
