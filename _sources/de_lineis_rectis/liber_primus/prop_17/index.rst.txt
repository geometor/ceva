**Proposition XVII · Problem X** (pp. 23–25, `Tab. 2, Figs. 18–19` & `Tab. 1, Fig. 7`)

*With the figure of the Fourth Element set forth, out of the five ratios of which we treated in that very element, any three being given, it is proposed to investigate the remaining two.*

This problem has ten cases, like the preceding.

(`Tab. 2, Fig. 18`) I. Let there be given three ratios :g:`ED` to :g:`DC` as 5 to 4, :g:`GD` to :g:`DB` as 6 to 7, and :g:`FD` to :g:`DA` as 1 to 3 (in this first case alone the two straight lines :g:`AC`, :g:`EF` must not be parallel to each other) it is required therefore to trace out the remaining two ratios, that is, :g:`AB` to :g:`BC` and :g:`EG` to :g:`GF`.
Since therefore :g:`AC`, :g:`EF` are not parallel, produced they will meet, as at :g:`H`; thus because in the figure :g:`ACHFED` of the First Element, the two ratios :g:`ED` to :g:`DC` as 5 to 4 and :g:`AD` to :g:`DF` as 3 to 1 are known,
we shall make manifest also (from the first case of the sixth problem) the remaining two ratios, and :g:`EF` will be to :g:`FH` as 11 to 16; and :g:`AC` to :g:`AH` as 11 to 20.
Again in another figure :g:`GFHBAD` of the same Element are had the two ratios :g:`AD` to :g:`DF` as 3 to 1 and :g:`BD` to :g:`DG` as 7 to 6; therefore, as above, we shall give the remaining two, namely :g:`HF` to :g:`FG` as 28 to 11, and :g:`HA` to :g:`AB` as 24 to 11. But because :g:`CA` to :g:`AB`

.. folio:: 0041
   :page: p. 24

is compounded of the ratios :g:`AC` to :g:`AH` and :g:`AH` to :g:`AB`, namely of the numbers 11 to 20 and 24 to 11, from which is compounded the ratio 66 to 55;
:g:`CA` to :g:`AB` will be as 66 to 55, and by dividing, :g:`CB` to :g:`BA` as 11 to 55, indeed as 1 to 5. Similarly, because :g:`EF` to :g:`FG` is compounded of the ratios of straight lines :g:`EF` to :g:`FH` and :g:`FH` to :g:`FG`,
namely of the numbers 11 to 16 and 28 to 11, from which arises the ratio 11 to 6 2/7, or 77 to 44; by dividing, :g:`EG` to :g:`GF` will be as 33 to 44, or as 3 to 4.

II. Let the said figure of the Fourth Element be supposed in any manner, and having the three ratios :g:`AB` to :g:`BC` as 5 to 1, :g:`BD` to :g:`DG` as 7 to 6, and :g:`EG` to :g:`GF` as 3 to 4,
let it be required to trace out the remaining two ratios, namely :g:`ED` to :g:`DC` and :g:`FD` to :g:`DA`. Compounded is straight line :g:`FD` to :g:`DA`, namely weight :g:`A` to :g:`F`, of the ratios of weights :g:`A` to :g:`B`, :g:`B` to :g:`G`, and :g:`G` to :g:`F`,
namely of straight lines :g:`CB` to :g:`CA`, :g:`GD` to :g:`DB`, and :g:`EF` to :g:`EG`, indeed of the ratios 1 to 6, 6 to 7, and 7 to 3; wherefore :g:`FD` to :g:`DA` is as 1 to 3. By the same reason, because :g:`ED` to :g:`DC`, that is, heavy body :g:`C` to :g:`E`, is compounded of the ratios of heavy bodies :g:`C` to :g:`B`, :g:`B` to :g:`G`, and :g:`G` to :g:`E`,
namely of straight lines :g:`AB` to :g:`AC`, :g:`GD` to :g:`DB`, and :g:`EF` to :g:`FG`; indeed of the ratios 5 to 6, 6 to 7, and 7 to 4; :g:`ED` to :g:`DC` will be as 5 to 4.

III. The three ratios :g:`AD` to :g:`DF` as 3 to 1, :g:`FG` to :g:`GE` as 4 to 3, and :g:`ED` to :g:`DC` as 5 to 4 being known, if we suspend heavy bodies according to those three known ratios, we shall obtain the remaining two :g:`AB` to :g:`BC` as 5 to 1, and :g:`GD` to :g:`DB` as 6 to 7.

IV. The same will happen if the three ratios be :g:`ED` to :g:`DC` as 3 to 1; :g:`CB` to :g:`BA` as 4 to 3, and :g:`AD` to :g:`DF` as 5 to 4, for we shall find :g:`EG` to :g:`GF` as 5 to 1, and :g:`BD` to :g:`GD` as 6 to 7.

(`Tab. 2, Fig. 19`) V. Let there be had three ratios :g:`FG` to :g:`GE` as 4 to 3, :g:`ED` to :g:`DC` as 5 to 4, and :g:`CB` to :g:`BA` as 1 to 5; we must find the remaining two :g:`BD` to :g:`DG` and :g:`FD` to :g:`DA`, which (lest we forget the method we have used from the beginning) we shall perform thus. Let it be made as 3 to 4, that is, as straight line :g:`GE` to :g:`FG`, so weight :g:`F` 3 to weight :g:`E` 4;
and as :g:`CD` to :g:`DE`, that is, as 4 to 5, so weight :g:`E` 4 to weight :g:`C` 5; and finally as :g:`AB` to :g:`BC`, that is, as 5 to 1, so weight :g:`C` 5 to weight :g:`A` 1.
Since point :g:`B` is the center of gravity of heavy bodies :g:`A` 1, :g:`C` 5, and likewise :g:`G` the center of gravity of weights :g:`E` 4, :g:`F` 3,
on balance-beam :g:`GDB` will be the center of gravity of all heavy bodies :g:`A` 1, :g:`C` 5, :g:`E` 4, :g:`F` 3; now :g:`D` is the center of gravity of the two heavy bodies :g:`E` 4, :g:`C` 5;
therefore if it be possible that we posit some other point :g:`h` as the center

.. folio:: 0042
   :page: p. 25

of heavy bodies :g:`A` 1, :g:`F` 3, there will be on straight line :g:`DH`, say at :g:`I`, the center of gravity of all heavy bodies,
and therefore (as was shown before) it would not be on balance-beam :g:`BDG`, because it is unique; and since this cannot happen, the same point :g:`D` must be the center of gravity of all heavy bodies, and likewise of heavy bodies :g:`A` 1, :g:`F` 3,
and of heavy bodies :g:`E` 4, :g:`C` 5; on that account, as :g:`B` 6 to :g:`G` 7, so straight line :g:`GD` to :g:`DB`, and as :g:`A` 1 to :g:`F` 3, so :g:`FD` to :g:`DA`.

VI. But if the three known ratios be :g:`EG` to :g:`GF` as 4 to 3; :g:`FD` to :g:`DA` as 5 to 4; and :g:`AB` to :g:`BC` as 1 to 5,
by the same reason we shall know the remaining two :g:`GB` to :g:`DB` as 6 to 7; and :g:`ED` to :g:`DC` as 1 to 3.

(`Tab. 1, Fig. 7`) VII. Having the three ratios :g:`ED` to :g:`DC` as 1 to 2, :g:`AB` to :g:`BC` of equality, and :g:`GD` to :g:`DB` as 1 to 3, let it be required to investigate the remaining two, namely :g:`EG` to :g:`GF` and :g:`AD` to :g:`DF`.
Compounded is :g:`GF` to :g:`FE`, namely weight :g:`E` to :g:`G`, of the ratios of heavy bodies :g:`E` to :g:`C`, :g:`C` to :g:`B`, and :g:`B` to :g:`G`, namely of straight lines :g:`CD` to :g:`CE`, :g:`AB` to :g:`AC`, and :g:`GD` to :g:`DB`;
indeed of the ratios of the numbers 2 to 1, 1 to 2, and 2 to 6, wherefore :g:`GF` to :g:`FE` is as 2 to 6, and by converting, and thence dividing, :g:`EG` to :g:`GF` will be as 2 to 1.
Again, because :g:`DF` to :g:`DA`, namely weight :g:`A` to :g:`F`, is compounded of the ratios of heavy bodies :g:`A` to :g:`C`, :g:`C` to :g:`E`, and :g:`E` to :g:`F`,
namely of straight lines :g:`CB` to :g:`BA`, :g:`ED` to :g:`DC`, and :g:`FG` to :g:`GE`; or of the ratios 1 to 1, 1 to 2, and 2 to 4; :g:`DF` to :g:`FA` will be as 1 to 4.

And the other three cases we shall show in the same way; for they are similar to this seventh. The whole problem is therefore established.

**Corollary.**

*For if angle* :g:`ADC` *be conceived above angle* :g:`EDF`, *line* :g:`AD` *will fall upon line* :g:`ED`, :g:`BD` *upon* :g:`DG`, *and* :g:`CD` *upon* :g:`DF`; *and line* :g:`ABC` *will cut lines* :g:`ED`, :g:`GD`, :g:`FD`, *whence it will come about*
*that the figure of the Fourth Element passes over into that of the Third, and conversely the latter into the former, if that angle be restored to its former position;*
*wherefore if in the figure of the Fourth Element for the ratios* :g:`ED` *to* :g:`DC`, :g:`FD` *to* :g:`DA`, *there be taken the two* :g:`ED` *to* :g:`DA` *and* :g:`FD` *to* :g:`DC`,
*thence any three ratios, as we have said, being given, we shall also make known the remaining two;*
*and we shall perform the same in the figure of the Third Element by changing the ratios* :g:`AE` *to* :g:`EF`, :g:`CD` *to* :g:`DE` *into the ratios* :g:`AE` *to* :g:`ED` *and* :g:`CE` *to* :g:`EF`.
