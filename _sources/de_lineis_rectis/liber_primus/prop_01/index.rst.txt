Proposition I: First Element (Menelaus Transversal)
===================================================

.. folio:: 0019
   :page: p. 2

.. picture:: 0103.g4.svg
   :alt: Calibrated interactive SVG of Ceva Book I Prop. I Elementum I (Tab. I Fig. 4)

.. picture:: 0103.g4.png
   :alt: 1678 copperplate Tabula I Fig. 4: Elementum I Menelaus sector

**Proposition I · Problem I · Element I** (`Tab. 1, Fig. 4`)

*In a triangle :g:`AEC`, let straight lines :g:`EB` and :g:`CD` drawn from two vertices to the opposite sides :g:`AC` and :g:`AE` intersect at :g:`F`. To find the ratios of the segments by suspending weights at the three vertices.*

Suspend weight :g:`G` at :g:`A`, weight :g:`I` at :g:`E`, and weight :g:`H` at :g:`C` such that :g:`D` is the center of gravity of :g:`G` and :g:`I` ($G : I = \overline{ED} : \overline{DA}$) and :g:`B` is the center of gravity of :g:`G` and :g:`H` ($G : H = \overline{CB} : \overline{BA}$).

By *Axiom II*, grouping :g:`G` and :g:`I` at :g:`D` places the total center of gravity of all three weights on the straight line :g:`CD`; grouping :g:`G` and :g:`H` at :g:`B` places the exact same center of gravity on :g:`EB`. By *Axiom I*, the unique center of gravity of $G + H + I$ lies at their intersection :g:`F`.

Therefore, balancing along the two intersecting levers :g:`CD` and :g:`EB` yields directly:

$$\overline{CF} : \overline{FD} = (G + I) : H, \qquad \overline{EF} : \overline{FB} = (G + H) : I.$$

**Corollary & Scholium**: Treating $\triangle ABD$ or $\triangle AEB$ cut by transversal :g:`CD` immediately yields the classic sector theorem of :person:`Menelaus <menelaus>` (:cite:`Sphaerica III.1 <menelaus:sphaerica>`) and :person:`Ptolemy <ptolemy>` (:cite:`Almagest I.13 <ptolemy:almagest>`).
