**PREFACE.**

As I reflected quite often amid these vicissitudes of things and fortunes how welcome a solace for calamities it would be to philosophize, and how happy and fortunate those men must at last be accounted whom no cares call away or sever from the leisure of the sciences,
the thought entered my mind to uplift, so far as was lawful, by letters and by native wit my youth terrified by misfortunes. Entering therefore into geometry, which seemed to surpass the rest both in variety of matter and in its very kind,
when the distinguished and wonderful discoveries of :person:`Apollonius <apollonius>`, :person:`Archimedes <archimedes>`, :person:`Pappus <pappus>`, and others struck and carried away my mind (as youth is headlong without counsel), it pleased me to spread sails to the winds,
if by chance some fortune might open new shores and regions hitherto unknown to anyone. I began to probe the quadrature of the circle and the still untamed

.. folio:: 0016
   :page: Prooemium [f. ✠4r]

hyperbola; namely, a cylindrical segment and a hyperbolic solid almost entirely compressed by me into a pyramid had raised no small hope.
Thrice did sleepless nights persuade me that the quarrels of straight and curved were reconciled; thrice did the stubborn and self-tenacious figure flee the rule. Yet, as after once and again frustrated labors some light and new hope kept arising,
I clung like an obstinate Sisyphus to the stone rolling back, until most recently the :person:`indivisibles of Cavalieri <cavalieri>` (:cite:`1635 <cavalieri:1635:indivis>`), applied with fruitless success, subdued all the stubbornness of my mind.
Calling my mind away therefore from these pursuits (for I would not believe that this bridle has been set upon human minds without a higher design), God granted that by joining and mingling geometric and mechanical reasonings together I might bring something new to light,
some solace for a mind mocked in great things. For having left aside the customary apparatus of geometry, and having substituted weights in place of lines, while I examine certain ratios the thought succeeded, and many things hitherto buried and unknown came forth into the light.
The novelty and usefulness of the matter persuaded me to publish this discovery, such as it is, thinking that a rough and unpolished beginning would (as often happens) be perfected by the genius and keenness of others.

.. folio:: 0017
   :page: Prooemium [f. ✠4v]

My purpose is to untangle statically by weights alone very many problems whose solution according to the usual laws would be arduous and often even inextricable, without employing any prior construction of circles and lines as is the custom among geometers;
and this I shall accomplish whenever straight lines mutually intersecting are proposed whose sections are so determined that, any one being varied, all the rest must necessarily vary. Hence it pleased me to call it a :term:`static construction <statica-constructio>`; may it prove as advantageous and brief as I persuade myself,
and which alone I pray and desire. Nor indeed has any thirst for fame driven me to write these things, which amid so great a renown of matters and authors it would be the mark of a madman to seek and of a frivolous mind to desire.
You will find many things less polished, and lacking the final hand; grant indulgence to the spare hours which partly sterner cares, and partly also the complaints of friends and household judging the flower of my youth ill spent on these studies, scarcely left me to work them out.
Moreover, if anything herein should prove not altogether unworthy of regard, to :person:`Donato Rossetti <rossetti>`, eminent and most distinguished among the Mathematicians of our age, to whose first instructions I owe whatever of the liberal arts is in me, you also, most kind reader, owe it. Farewell.
