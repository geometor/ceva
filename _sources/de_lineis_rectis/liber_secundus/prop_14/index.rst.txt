**Proposition XIV · Theorem XII** (pp. 49–51, `Tab. 6, Figs. 52–54` & `Tab. 7, Figs. 55–56`)

*If two lines touching a circle or an ellipse produced meet one another, and from another point taken on the section another tangent be drawn, dividing the former two,*
*and their points of contact and points of division be joined by two mutually intersecting lines; and then from the point of intersection a line be drawn to the meeting point of the first tangents,*
*this line produced (when need be) will pass through the remaining point of contact, so that the resulting figure resolves into at least two figures of the First Element.*

Let :g:`ABC` be the circumference of an ellipse or circle touched by two lines :g:`AF` and :g:`CD`, which produced meet at :g:`E`;

.. folio:: 0067
   :page: p. 50

and having drawn another tangent :g:`FBD` cutting tangent :g:`AE` at :g:`F` and :g:`CE` at :g:`D`, let the two lines :g:`AD` and :g:`CF` be joined, dividing one another at :g:`G`. Now having drawn :g:`EG` and produced it (when need be), I say it passes through the remaining contact point :g:`B`.

For if the three tangents form a triangle circumscribed about the proposed ellipse or circle, I have already shown this in the first proposition of this Second Book. But if the tangent :g:`FBD` cuts the other two :g:`AFE` and :g:`CDE` between their contacts :g:`A`, :g:`C` and their meeting point :g:`E`,
I shall show this also by reduction to what cannot be. For let :g:`EG` joined not pass (if possible) through contact :g:`B`, but through :g:`N`; and having drawn another tangent :g:`KIL` produced both ways together with :g:`EAK` and :g:`ECL` to form triangle :g:`EKL` touching the circle or ellipse at :g:`A`, :g:`I`, :g:`C`,
let :g:`AL` and :g:`KC` be joined, intersecting one another at :g:`M`. Since :g:`ENG` is a single straight line, the two figures :g:`EDCGFN` and :g:`EFAGDN` belong to the First Element; and therefore line :g:`FN` to :g:`FD`—that is, weight :g:`D` to :g:`N`—is compounded from the ratios of weights :g:`D` to :g:`E` to :g:`N`,
namely of the lines :g:`CE` to :g:`CD` and :g:`GN` to :g:`GE`; and likewise in the other figure of the First Element, line :g:`FD` to :g:`ND`—that is, weight :g:`N` to :g:`F`—namely the two ratios of weights :g:`N` to :g:`E` to :g:`F`, arise from the ratios of the lines :g:`GE` to :g:`GN` and :g:`AF` to :g:`AE`:
therefore the two ratios :g:`FN` to :g:`FD` to :g:`ND`, that is :g:`FN` to :g:`ND`, will be compounded from the ratios :g:`CE` to :g:`CD`, :g:`GN` to :g:`GE` to :g:`GN`, and :g:`AF` to :g:`AE`, that is, from the ratios :g:`CE` to :g:`CD` and :g:`AF` to :g:`AE`.

Moreover (by *Book II, Prop. I*), :g:`KI` to :g:`IL` is compounded from the ratios :g:`EC` to :g:`CL` and :g:`KA` to :g:`AE`; and the same ratio is also compounded from the ratios :g:`DC` to :g:`CL`, :g:`FB` to :g:`BD`, and :g:`KA` to :g:`AF`; but :g:`EC` to :g:`CL` is compounded from the two :g:`EC` to :g:`DC` to :g:`CL`;
and likewise :g:`KA` to :g:`AE` is compounded from the ratios :g:`KA` to :g:`AF` and :g:`AF` to :g:`AE`; therefore the ratio compounded from :g:`EC` to :g:`DC` to :g:`CL` and :g:`KA` to :g:`AF` to :g:`AE` will equal that compounded from :g:`DC` to :g:`CL`, :g:`FB` to :g:`BD`, and :g:`KA` to :g:`AF`.
Taking away therefore on both sides the ratios :g:`DC` to :g:`CL` and :g:`KA` to :g:`AF`, the remaining ratio :g:`FB` to :g:`BD` will be compounded from the ratios :g:`EC` to :g:`DC` and :g:`AF` to :g:`AE`. But, as I have shown, :g:`FN` to :g:`ND` is also compounded from these same ratios;
therefore as :g:`FN` to :g:`ND`, so :g:`FB` to :g:`BD`; and by composition, :g:`FD` to :g:`ND` will be as the same :g:`FD` to :g:`BD`, and therefore :g:`ND` will equal :g:`BD`—the whole equal to the part—which is absurd; therefore :g:`ENG` will pass through contact :g:`B`, Q.E.D.

.. folio:: 0068
   :page: p. 51

**Corollary** (`Tab. 7, Figs. 55 & 56`)

*It is evident both in the ellipse and in the circle that* :g:`FB` *is to* :g:`BD` *as the ratio compounded from* :g:`CE` *to* :g:`CD` *and* :g:`AF` *to* :g:`AE`.
