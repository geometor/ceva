**Proposition VII · Theorem VII** (pp. 43–44, `Tab. 5, Figs. 43 & 45`)

*If three sides of a triangle be divided in the same manner as were the sides of the triangle of the second element, and the points of division be joined by three straight lines,* 
*which are bisected, and to their sections from the corresponding angles we draw lines, if these be produced they will cut one another at the same point, and this point will be within the triangle standing from the former joined lines.*

Let there be triangle :g:`ABC`, 
whose sides are so divided at :g:`D`, :g:`F`, :g:`E` that the ratio :g:`CF` to :g:`FA` is formed from the ratios :g:`BE` to :g:`EA`, and :g:`CD` to :g:`DB`; and let :g:`FD`, :g:`DE`, :g:`EF` be joined, which are bisected at :g:`K`, :g:`H`, :g:`G`. 
I say now that if we also join lines :g:`AG`, :g:`CH`, :g:`BK`, all meet one another at the same point, and indeed within triangle :g:`DEF`.

For :g:`CF` to :g:`FA` is compounded of the ratios :g:`BE` to :g:`EA`, and :g:`CD` to :g:`DB`; 
and likewise :g:`EK` to :g:`KD`, namely the ratio of equality, is compounded of the two ratios of equality :g:`FH` to :g:`HD`, and :g:`EG` to :g:`GF`: therefore if the three lines :g:`AG`, :g:`CH`, :g:`BK` be produced, they will meet together at the same point. 
But if anyone deny that this point exists within triangle :g:`EFD`, it will necessarily be on one of the lines :g:`BK`, :g:`AG`, :g:`CH`; otherwise those three lines would not meet one another at the same point; 
let us suppose therefore that it is first on line :g:`BK` at :g:`I`; so that lines :g:`AG`, :g:`CH` when produced fall upon :g:`I`. Since, with :g:`KG`, :g:`KH` joined, space :g:`GKHF` is a parallelogram, and line :g:`AC` cuts :g:`GF`, 
if :g:`AC` be produced toward :g:`C` and :g:`KH` toward :g:`H`, they will meet at :g:`M`; and by the same reasoning :g:`KG` and :g:`CA` produced toward points :g:`E`, :g:`A` will meet at :g:`L`. Lastly, with :g:`EL`, :g:`DM` joined, 
these will be (from the preceding) parallel to one another; wherefore :g:`AE`, :g:`CD` will not meet at :g:`B`, which is against the hypothesis; and therefore lines :g:`AG`, :g:`CH`
