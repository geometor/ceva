**Proposition XVI · Theorem XIV** (pp. 51–52, `Tab. 7, Fig. 57` [corrected from `tab. 1. fig. 5` in Errata p. 83!])

*In any triangle :g:`IAE`, the three straight lines :g:`IC`, :g:`AG`, and :g:`EN` bisecting the three interior angles all concur at a single point :g:`F` (the **Incenter** $X(1)$)!*

By Euclid VI.3 (Angle Bisector Theorem), each angle bisector divides the opposite side in the exact ratio of the two adjacent sides: $\overline{AC}:\overline{CE} = \overline{IA}:\overline{IE}$, $\overline{EG}:\overline{GI} = \overline{AE}:\overline{AI}$, and $\overline{IN}:\overline{NA} = \overline{IE}:\overline{AE}$. Multiplying all three ratios cancels every side length identically to $1$, so by the Converse of Ceva (*Book II Prop. I Coroll.*), all three bisectors concur at :g:`F`—equivalently, suspending weights proportional to the opposite side lengths $(a : b : c)$ balances the triangle right at its incenter!

**Lemma VI** (p. 52, `Tab. 7, Figs. 58, 60, 61`): Constructs the common external and internal tangent lines to two given circles (preparing Propositions XVII–XVIII).
