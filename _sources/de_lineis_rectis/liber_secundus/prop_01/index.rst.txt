Proposition I: Inscribed Conic Concurrency & Converse of Ceva's Theorem
=======================================================================

.. folio:: 0055
   :page: p. 38

.. picture:: 0109.g38.svg
   :alt: Calibrated interactive SVG of Ceva Liber II Prop. I Inscribed Ellipse & Converse of Ceva (Tab. IV Fig. 38)

.. picture:: 0109.g38.png
   :alt: 1678 copperplate Tabula IV Fig. 38: Triangle circumscribed about ellipse and Converse of Ceva

**Proposition I · Theorem I** (p. 38, `Tab. 4, Fig. 38`)

*If a triangle :g:`GAC` is circumscribed about a circle or an ellipse touching its three sides at contact points :g:`F`, :g:`B`, and :g:`D`, then the three straight lines :g:`GB`, :g:`AD`, and :g:`CF` joining each vertex to the opposite :term:`point of tangency <punctum-contactus>` all concur at a single interior point :g:`E`!*

By *Lemma I*, the alternating tangent segments satisfy $\overline{AF} \cdot \overline{CB} \cdot \overline{GD} = \overline{FG} \cdot \overline{DC} \cdot \overline{BA}$. For a circle, :g:`E` is the classical **Gergonne Point** :g:`X(7)` (:person:`Joseph-Diez Gergonne <gergonne>`, 1818), discovered here by Ceva 140 years earlier for *both* circles and arbitrary inscribed ellipses!

**Corollary (p. 38 — The Converse of Ceva's Theorem!)**: From the uniqueness of the third contact point on :g:`AC` (if a line through :g:`G` and :g:`E` met :g:`AC` at any distinct point $H \neq B$, the static product would fail), Ceva explicitly establishes the **full Converse of Ceva's Theorem**: whenever three points on the sides of a triangle divide them in cyclic product $1$, the three cevians necessarily concur at a single point!
