Proposition III: Proportional Side Divisions and Bisected Base
==============================================================

.. folio:: 0056
   :page: p. 39

.. picture:: 0111.g39.svg
   :alt: Calibrated interactive SVG — Ceva 1678 Tabula V Fig. 39: Prop. III

.. picture:: 0111.g39.png
   :alt: 1678 copperplate Tabula V Fig. 39: Triangle with proportionally divided sides and bisected base

**Proposition III · Theorem III** (p. 39, `Tab. 5, Fig. 39`)

*If in a triangle two sides containing an angle be cut similarly, and the base be bisected, and lines be drawn from the angles to the opposite points of section, they will cut one another at the same point;*
 *so that the figure composed of those same lines is that of the second element.*

Let there be triangle :g:`ACE`, and as :g:`AB` to :g:`BC`, so let :g:`ED` be placed to :g:`DC`; let base :g:`AE` be bisected at :g:`G`, and with :g:`AD`, :g:`EB` joined cutting one another at :g:`F`,
let us join line :g:`CF`: I say that this produced passes through point :g:`G`; if this be not true, let it fall if possible upon :g:`H`; and since, by the second element, :g:`EH` to :g:`HA`,
namely weight :g:`A` to :g:`E`, is compounded of the ratios of weights :g:`A` to :g:`C` to :g:`E`, that is of straight lines :g:`CB` to :g:`BA` and :g:`ED` to :g:`DC`, or rather of straight lines :g:`CB` to :g:`BA` and :g:`BA` to :g:`BC`;
therefore :g:`EH` will be to :g:`HA` as :g:`BC` to :g:`BC`, and hence :g:`EH` will be equal to :g:`HA`. But :g:`EG` is also equal to :g:`GA`; therefore as :g:`EH` to :g:`HA`, so :g:`EG` to :g:`GA`, and *componendo*, as :g:`EA` to :g:`AH`, so the same :g:`EA` to :g:`AG`; wherefore :g:`AG` will be equal to :g:`AH`, namely the whole to its part, which is absurd. Therefore line :g:`CF` will not fall on :g:`H`, but on :g:`G`, which was to be proved.
