Proposition III: Twelfth-Semicircle Arcs from Squared Side Ratios
=================================================================

.. folio:: 0081
   :page: p. 64

**Proposition III · Theorem III** (`Tab. 8, Figs. 69–70`)

*With the same things supposed, if in the first case DA to AE be in square in the subsesquitertian ratio, and in the second in the subquadruple ratio, arc CH will be the twelfth part of the semicircumference of that circle.*

Because first, in the first case, the square on :g:`DA` is placed to the square :g:`EA` as 3 to 4, arc :g:`HKA` will be to the semicircumference of the circle of the same arc as 3 to 4, that is, as 9 to 12.
Again, because :g:`DA` in square is subsesquitertian of :g:`EA` itself, and triangle :g:`DEA` stands in the semicircle (since the angle at :g:`B` is right), :g:`DA` will be the side of an inscribed equilateral triangle, and therefore angle :g:`DEA` will be the sixth part of four right angles;
its double will be the third part of four right angles, but :math:`\frac{2}{3}`

.. folio:: 0082
   :page: p. 65

parts of two right angles; wherefore also circumference :g:`CKA` to the semicircumference of the circle of the same arc will be as 2 to 3, yea as 8 to 12;
and by inversion the same semicircumference to the same arc will be as 12 to 8; yet arc :g:`HKA` to the same semicircumference was as 9 to 12; therefore *ex aequali* arc :g:`HKA` to arc :g:`CKA` will be as 9 to 8;
thence by conversion of ratio and by converting, their difference, namely arc :g:`CH` to arc :g:`CKA`, will be as 1 to 8; and the same arc :g:`CKA` to the semicircumference of the same circle whose arc :g:`HC` is, is as 8 to 12;
therefore again *ex aequali* arc :g:`HC` to the semicircumference of the circle will be as 1 to 12.

And in the second case, because the square on :g:`DA` is placed to that on :g:`AE` as 1 to 4, arc :g:`HKA` will also be to its semicircumference as 1 to 4;
but angle :g:`AEC` is the third part of two right angles, that is, circumference :g:`AHC` to the semicircumference of the same circle is as 1 to 3, or as :math:`1\frac{1}{3}` to 4; therefore by inversion the said semicircumference to its arc :g:`AHC` will be as 4 to :math:`1\frac{1}{3}`;
and therefore *ex aequali* arc :g:`HKA` to arc :g:`AHC` will be as 1 to :math:`1\frac{1}{3}`; wherefore :g:`AKH` to :g:`HC` will be as 1 to :math:`\frac{1}{3}`; but the semicircumference of the circle whose arc is :g:`AKH` to this same arc is as 4 to 1;
therefore again *ex aequali* the semicircumference to this same arc will be as 4 to :math:`\frac{1}{3}`, or as 12 to 1, Q.E.D.
