Proposition XVI: Center of Gravity of an Infinite Hyperbolic Plane
==================================================================

.. folio:: 0097
   :page: p. 80

.. picture:: 0121.g83.png
   :alt: 1678 copperplate Tabula X Fig. 83: Center of gravity D of infinitely long hyperbolic plane BECMA

**Proposition XVI · Theorem XVI**

*Let* :g:`AMCE` *be a half-section through axis* :g:`AE` *of the aforesaid solid, and let rectangle* :g:`BE` *be applied to* :g:`AE` *so that* :g:`BA` *is equal to semi-axis* :g:`DM` *of the opposite sections; it is to be shown that point* :g:`D` *is the center of gravity of plane* :g:`BECMA`, *even though of infinite length toward* :g:`C`, *with rectangle* :g:`AF` *removed.*

Since I showed in the preceding proposition that the infinitely wide round solid generated by the revolution of plane :g:`FKC` about axis :g:`FC` is equal to the cylinder generated by the revolution of rectangle :g:`ZR` about axis :g:`✠Z`, together with cylinder :g:`GI`, whose axis is :g:`FC` (margin: *Tab. 10, Fig. 83*),
and :g:`CD` is to :g:`CF` in length as :g:`DM` is to :g:`MN`, or to :g:`XZ`, in power; if a cylinder be conceived whose altitude is :g:`CF` and base radius :g:`DM`, this will be equal to the aforesaid cylinder whose axis is :g:`✠Z`;
and therefore it is evident that this conceived cylinder is equal to the solid :g:`FKCE` of infinite width, once there is removed from it

.. folio:: 0098
   :page: p. 81

cylinder :g:`GI`, namely that which is made from the revolution of rectangle :g:`CL` about axis :g:`FC`; that is, in the present figure also, it is clear that the cylinder generated by the revolution of rectangle :g:`BE` about axis :g:`AE` is equal to the round solid of infinite width
produced from the revolution of plane :g:`EFCMA` about the same axis :g:`EA`, provided however that from this solid be removed the cylinder whose half-rectangle at the axis is :g:`AF`. Therefore the moment of rectangle :g:`BE` will be equal to the moment of plane :g:`FCM` of infinite length toward :g:`C`
(margin: *from the beautiful Lemma 31 of Torricelli in De dimensione parabolae*), if the entire plane :g:`EBAMCFE` be balanced upon straight line :g:`EA`, and rectangle :g:`AF` be conceived as of no weight.
Therefore, the center of gravity of the aforesaid two planes thus constituted as of one magnitude will be in the finite line :g:`EA`, and therefore (although it may seem incredible),
since this magnitude has a center of gravity, that center will also be in the diameter :g:`GC` of the same plane magnitude; wherefore it is found at :g:`D`, the common intersection of lines :g:`AE` and :g:`GC`, which was to be demonstrated.
