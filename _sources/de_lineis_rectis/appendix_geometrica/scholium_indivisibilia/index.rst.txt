   :page: p. 81

.. picture:: 0121.g84.png

**SCHOLIUM.**

*These theorems, several of which I had deduced from geometric principles, I have demonstrated more expeditiously by Cavalieri's method, although Luca Valerio, the subtlest Archimedes of our age, long ago explored geometrically the centers of gravity of a hemisphere and a parabolic conoid. I confess that the method of indivisibles is a great shortcut in geometry, especially in measuring solids, however irregular—a work full of windings if we wish to use geometric reasonings. Yet one must proceed cautiously, for it happens not rarely that Cavalieri's reasoning fails altogether, especially regarding surfaces of round solids: behold an example.*

Let there be a sphere whose axis is :g:`AB`, cut by any planes perpendicular to the axis :g:`CD`, :g:`EF`, :g:`GH`, etc. (margin: *Tab. 10, Fig. 84*);
I shall say that the whole surface of the sphere has to its portion :g:`EBF` the same ratio which the circle has to its segment :g:`EBF`. For it is evident that all sections :g:`CD`, :g:`EF`, :g:`GH`, etc. are circles about diameters :g:`CD`, :g:`EF`, :g:`GH`, etc. parallel to one another;
wherefore the circumferences of the same circles will also be equidistant from one another, and likewise the diameters will be parallel to one another. And because the circumference about diameter :g:`CD` is to that

.. folio:: 0099
   :page: p. 82

about diameter :g:`EF` as diameter :g:`CD` is to :g:`EF`, and likewise the circumference about diameter :g:`EF` is to the circumference about diameter :g:`GH` as diameter :g:`EF` is to diameter :g:`GH`, jointly all circumferences about :g:`CD`, :g:`EF`, :g:`HG` will be to all circumferences about :g:`EF`, :g:`GH`, etc.
as all diameters :g:`CD`, :g:`FE`, :g:`HG`, etc. are to all diameters :g:`EF`, :g:`GH`, etc.: that is, the whole surface of the sphere :g:`AEBF` will have to its part :g:`EBF` the same ratio which the circle has to its segment :g:`EBF`, which however is false;
for circle :g:`ACBD` has to its segment :g:`EBF`, less than a semicircle, a greater ratio than square :g:`AB` has to the square of straight line :g:`BE`, that is, than the surface of the sphere has to its portion :g:`EBF`;
for the circle whose radius is :g:`AB` is equal to the whole surface of the sphere, whereas the circle whose radius is :g:`BE` is equal to surface :g:`EBF` of the same sphere;
wherefore the surface of the sphere is to its portion :g:`EBF` as the square on :g:`AB` is to the square on :g:`BE`, or as triangle :g:`ABE` is to triangle :g:`EIB`:
now circle :g:`AB` is posited to be in the same ratio to its segment :g:`EBF`, or semicircle :g:`AEB` to trilineal figure :g:`EBI`; therefore the remaining spaces will also be in the same ratio:
that is, segments :g:`AEC`, :g:`EBG` together, to segment :g:`EBG`, will be as triangle :g:`ABE` to triangle :g:`EIB`, or again as square :g:`AB` to square :g:`BE`, indeed as the aggregate of squares :g:`AE`, :g:`EB` to :g:`EB` itself;
but the segment of a circle whose base is :g:`AE`, similar to :g:`EBG`, falls inside segment :g:`ACE`; therefore segment :g:`ACE` will have to segment :g:`EGB` a greater ratio than square :g:`AE` to square :g:`EB`,
and jointly the two segments :g:`AEC`, :g:`EBG` will also have to segment :g:`EBG` a greater proportion than the two squares :g:`AE`, :g:`EB` together, that is, :g:`AB` itself to square :g:`EB`, or than triangle :g:`AEB` to :g:`EBI` itself;
wherefore the two antecedents together to the two consequents together, that is, the semicircle to trilineal figure :g:`AGBI`, or double to double, namely the circle to its segment :g:`EBF`,
will have a greater ratio than triangle :g:`AEB` to :g:`EBI` itself, or greater than square :g:`AB` to :g:`BE`, which was to be shown.

*On the contrary, if you substitute a cone for the sphere in the aforesaid reasoning, you will deduce the truth: so that the whole conical surface is to its part cut off between the vertex of the cone and a plane parallel to its base as the triangle through the axis is to that triangle intercepted between the said vertex and the aforesaid plane, which*

.. folio:: 0100
   :page: p. 83

*is a part of the same triangle through the axis.*

*Hence you see, in measuring the surfaces of round solids, how uncertain is the otherwise most ingenious method of indivisibles.*
