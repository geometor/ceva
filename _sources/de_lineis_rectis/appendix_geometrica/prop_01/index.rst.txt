Proposition I: Squared Sides and Opposite Angles of a Right Triangle
====================================================================

.. folio:: 0078
   :page: p. 61

.. picture:: 0117.g68.png
   :alt: 1678 copperplate Tabula VIII Fig. 68: Right scalene triangle inscribed in semicircle

**Appendix Geometrica · Proposition I · Theorem I** (`Tab. 8, Fig. 68`)

*In any right-angled scalene triangle, the square of the hypotenuse has to the square of the greater side a lesser ratio, whereas the squares of these lines taken separately have to the square of the lesser side a greater ratio, than the opposite angle to the opposite angle.*

Let triangle :g:`ABC` be right-angled at :g:`B`, whose hypotenuse is :g:`AC` and greater side :g:`BA`. I say first that :g:`AC` to :g:`AB` in square has a lesser ratio than the right angle :g:`ABC` has to angle :g:`BCA`.
Let :g:`AC` be bisected at :g:`E`, and with center :g:`E` and radius :g:`EA` or :g:`EC` let semicircle :g:`ABC` be described, whose circumference will also pass through point :g:`B` (for the angle at :g:`B` is right); next, because side :g:`AB` is greater than side :g:`BC`, arc :g:`AB` will also be greater than arc :g:`BC`;
and thus the circumference of semicircle :g:`ABC` will not be bisected at point :g:`B`; let it be bisected therefore, and let the point of division be :g:`D`, which will indeed fall between :g:`A` and :g:`B`;
and having joined lines :g:`DA`, :g:`DIE`, :g:`DC`, :g:`DB`, :g:`BC`, :g:`BE`, let perpendiculars :g:`BG`, :g:`DE` be raised from points :g:`B`, :g:`D` to diameter :g:`AC`, and let line :g:`DH` bisect angle :g:`ADB`;
finally, because angle :g:`ADC` is double angle :g:`ADE`, that is :g:`ADI` (for this can easily be deduced), and likewise angle :g:`ADB` is double angle :g:`ADH`, by alternation angle :g:`ADC` will be to angle :g:`ADB` as angle :g:`ADI` to :g:`ADH`;
and angle :g:`ADC` is less than angle :g:`ADB` (for it stands in a minor segment of the circle); therefore angle :g:`ADI` will be less than :g:`ADH`, and hence point :g:`H` will fall between points :g:`I` and :g:`B`.
These things thus laid down, because in triangle :g:`ADB` the angle at the vertex is bisected by line :g:`DH`, as straight line :g:`AD` is to :g:`DB`, so is segment :g:`AH` of the base to :g:`HB`, but :g:`AI` to :g:`HB`, and much more to :g:`IB`,
has a lesser ratio than straight line :g:`AH` to :g:`HB`, namely :g:`AD` to :g:`DB`; but straight line :g:`AD` to :g:`DB` has also a lesser

(margin: *Almagest*) ratio than arc :g:`AD` to arc :g:`DB`;
therefore straight line :g:`AI` to :g:`IB` will have a lesser ratio than the said arc :g:`AD` to arc :g:`DB`; and by composition and conversion of ratio, straight line :g:`AB` to :g:`AI`, or :g:`AG` to :g:`AE`, will have a greater ratio than arc :g:`ADB` has to arc :g:`AD`, and the doubles of the consequents;
but :g:`AC` is double :g:`AE`, and circumference :g:`ADC` is double :g:`AD`; therefore :g:`AG` to :g:`AC`, namely square :g:`AB` to square :g:`AC`, will have a greater ratio than circumference :g:`ADB` to circumference :g:`ADC`, yea than angle :g:`AEB` to two right angles, and their halves,
namely angle :g:`ACB` to the right angle :g:`ABC`; and by inversion, square :g:`AC` to square :g:`AB` will have a lesser ratio than angle :g:`ABC` to angle :g:`ACB`.

**II.** I say that square :g:`AC` to square :g:`BC`, that is, line :g:`AC` to line :g:`BC` in square, has a greater ratio than the right angle :g:`ABC` to angle :g:`BAC`. For since straight line :g:`AG` to :g:`AC` had a greater ratio than circumference :g:`ADB` to circumference :g:`ADC`, by inversion straight line :g:`AC` to :g:`AG` will have a lesser; but by conversion of ratio, straight line :g:`AC` to :g:`CG` will have a greater ratio than circumference :g:`ADC` to :g:`BC`; yet as straight line :g:`AC` to :g:`CG`, so is square :g:`CA` to square :g:`CB`, and as circumference :g:`ADC` to :g:`CB`, so are two right angles to angle :g:`BEC`, or their halves, that is, one right angle :g:`ABC` to angle :g:`BAC`; therefore square :g:`AC` to square :g:`CB` will have a greater ratio than the right angle :g:`ABC` to angle :g:`BAC`.

**III.** Lastly, I say that square :g:`AB` to :g:`BC` itself has also a greater ratio than angle :g:`BCA` to :g:`BAC`: for (as was just demonstrated) straight line :g:`AC` to :g:`CG` has a greater ratio than circumference :g:`ADC` to :g:`CB` itself; therefore by division :g:`AG` to :g:`GC`, that is, square :g:`AG` to :g:`CB` itself, or square :g:`AB` to :g:`BC`, will have a greater ratio than circumference :g:`ADB` to :g:`BC` itself, namely than angle :g:`ACB` to angle :g:`BEC`, and their halves; that is, angle :g:`ACB` to angle :g:`BAC`, Q.E.D.
