Proposition XII: Hyperbolic Conoid Inscribed in a Conical Frustum
=================================================================

.. folio:: 0093
   :page: p. 76

.. picture:: 0121.g79.svg
   :alt: Calibrated interactive SVG — Ceva 1678 Tabula X Fig. 79: Appendix Prop. XII

.. picture:: 0121.g79.png
   :alt: 1678 copperplate Tabula X Fig. 79: Hyperbolic conoid ACE inscribed in conical frustum ABDE

**Proposition XII · Theorem XII** (pp. 76–77, `Tab. 10, Fig. 79`)

*If a frustum of a cone intercepted between two parallel planes encloses a portion of a hyperbolic conoid so that both solids stand on the same base and touch each other along the circumference of this base,*
 *the portion of the conoid will be equal to the frustum of the cone minus that cone whose altitude is common with the portion, and whose base is the common circle.*

Around the same axis :g:`CF` let there be conceived a portion of a hyperbolic conoid and a frustum of a cone, and let their generating figures be hyperbola :g:`ACE` and trapezium :g:`ABDE`, so that the two sides :g:`AB`, :g:`DE` touch the hyperbola at points :g:`A`, :g:`E`, and side :g:`BD` parallel to :g:`AE` at point :g:`C`;
then, having joined lines :g:`AC`, :g:`AD`, let us conceive a cone whose triangle through axis :g:`AC` is :g:`BAD`. I say that portion :g:`ACE` of the conoid is equal to frustum :g:`ABDE` of the cone minus cone :g:`BAD`.

Let any point :g:`O` be taken on axis :g:`CF`, and through it let a plane be drawn parallel to base :g:`AE`, by which plane let those three conceived solids be cut, and let the sections be circles :g:`IN`, :g:`LH`, :g:`IP`.

(margin: *Conics* III.16) Since as the square of tangent :g:`CB` is to that of tangent :g:`BA`, so is rectangle :g:`HIL` to square :g:`IA`, by alternation square :g:`BC` will be to rectangle :g:`HIL` as square :g:`BA` to :g:`AI`, or as square :g:`BC` to :g:`IK`;
therefore square :g:`BC` has the same ratio to rectangle :g:`HIL` as to square :g:`IK`, and hence square :g:`IK` will equal rectangle :g:`HIL`. But (margin: *Elements* II.6) rectangle :g:`HIL` together with square :g:`LO` equals square :g:`IO`;
therefore square :g:`IO` will be equal to the two squares :g:`LO`, :g:`IK` taken together; wherefore circle :g:`IN`, whose radius is :g:`IO`, will also be equal to the two circles :g:`IP`, :g:`LH`, whose radii are :g:`IL`, :g:`LO`.

.. folio:: 0094
   :page: p. 77

Now, taking the same rule :g:`AE`, all the circles :g:`AE`, :g:`IN`, :g:`BD`, etc. of frustum :g:`ABDE` of the cone are equal to all the circles :g:`AE`, :g:`LH`, etc. of the portion of the conoid, together with all the circles :g:`IP`, :g:`BD`, etc. of cone :g:`BAD`;
from which it follows that portion :g:`ACE` of the hyperbolic conoid, together with cone :g:`BAD`, is equal to frustum :g:`ABDE`; and subtracting cone :g:`BAD` in common, portion :g:`ACE` of the conoid will be equal to frustum :g:`ABDE` minus cone :g:`BAD`, which was to be demonstrated.
