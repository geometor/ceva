Proposition XV: Finite Volume of an Infinite Hyperbolic Solid
=============================================================

.. folio:: 0096
   :page: p. 79

.. picture:: 0121.g82.svg
   :alt: Calibrated interactive SVG of Ceva's Infinite Hyperbolic Solid of Finite Volume (Tab. X Fig. 82)

.. picture:: 0121.g82.png
   :alt: 1678 copperplate Tabula X Fig. 82: Infinitely extended hyperbolic solid equal to finite cylinder

**Proposition XV · Theorem XV — Infinitely Extended Solid of Finite Volume** (pp. 79–80, `Tab. 10, Fig. 82`)

*An **infinitely extended hyperbolic solid of revolution**—generated by revolving four symmetric branches of an equilateral hyperbola and its asymptotes :g:`EK` around the transverse axis :g:`FC`—has a strictly **finite volume**, equal to a finite right cylinder!*

Following :person:`Evangelista Torricelli's <torricelli>` famous *Solidum Hyperbolicum Acutum* (:cite:`De sphaera I.5 <torricelli:1644:opera>`, Gabriel's Horn) and :person:`Apollonius <apollonius>` (*Conics* II.22), Ceva decomposes the infinitely wide solid into nested coaxial **cylindrical shells** (*superficies cylindricæ* `GLIB, HM, QO, ...`). Because the hyperbola equation $xy = k$ keeps the lateral area $2\pi x \cdot (2y) = 4\pi k$ constant for every cylindrical shell, each cylindrical shell equals a fixed circle of radius :g:`HN` (the mean proportional between whole axis :g:`HM` and semi-axis :g:`HD`)! Stacking those equal circles across the finite semi-axis :g:`DC` yields a finite right cylinder!
