Proposition II: Second Element (Interior Ceva Concurrency)¶

Proposition II · Problem II · Element II

Let there be a triangle EAC, and from its angles let lines EF, AF, CF be drawn to the same point F within the triangle, which being produced from F meet the sides in order at the points B, K, D; it is proposed to suspend heavy bodies I, G, H from the aforesaid angles E, A, C so that weight G to I is as \(ED\) to \(DA\); the same G to H as \(CB\) to \(BA\); H to I as \(EK\) to \(KC\); weight I to the two G, H as \(BF\) to \(FE\); weight H to the two I, G as \(DF\) to \(FC\); and finally, as the single G to the two I, H, so \(KF\) to \(FA\).

(Tab. 1, Fig. 5) Let it be made as \(CB\) to \(BA\), so weight G to H, and as \(ED\) to \(DA\), so the same weight G to I.

Since therefore the figure \(EDBACF\) is that of the First Element, and \(CB\) is to \(BA\) as G to H, and the straight line \(ED\) to \(DA\) as G to I, also \(DF\) to \(FC\) will be as weight H to the two weights I, G, and likewise \(BF\) to \(FE\) as weight I to the two G, H.

(Corol. Prop. 1, Lem. 1) Moreover, because F is the center of gravity in which is the weight of the heavy bodies I, G, H, the produced line AF will pass through K, the center of the remaining weight I, H,

f. 0021 · p. 4

(Lem. 2) therefore as weight I to H, so \(CK\) to \(KE\).

Again, because AK is a certain balance-beam at whose extremities A, K are the weights G and I, H, (Ax. 2, Lem. 2) the center of gravity of the aforesaid weights I, G, H will lie on the same balance-beam AK; and since this center is unique and lies on both balance-beams DC and EB, as we showed above, it will necessarily be at F; therefore it will be as \(KF\) to \(FA\), so weight G to the two I, H together, which was to be done.

Scholium. When this same figure is set forth again, we shall signify the aggregate of weights I, H by the letter K, for in K is the center of gravity, and therefore the weight of the heavy bodies I, H.
