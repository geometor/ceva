Proposition XXVI: Concurrent Lines in a Double Pyramid¶

Proposition XXVI · Theorem XV (p. 34, Tab. 3, Fig. 31)

Let there be a pyramid whose vertex is H, and whose base is triangle AEC; and with the planes of triangles AHE, EHC, CHA produced beyond point H, let them be cut by another plane; and let the common sections of these planes be lines AHI, CHN, EHL, NL, NI, IL; and with a figure of the Second Element AFEDCBG constructed on the base of the proposed pyramid, let lines FH, DH, BH be joined, which produced meet the sides of the opposite triangle; thus let them meet at points K, M, O in order, and let lines NK, IM, LO be joined; I say that these intersect one another at the same point P, and moreover, if GH being joined be produced, it meets the opposite triangle at the aforesaid point P. Now that this may be made manifest, let us suspend from the angles of the two triangles NLI, AEC in the reciprocal ratio of the lengths, the weights Q, S, R, X, V, T, which will so most beautifully correspond to one another that one to one, or two to one, or two to two, or three to three, are among themselves reciprocally as the lengths from which they hang.

(Tab. 3, Fig. 31) Let it be made as ED to DC, so weight R to S; as AF to FE, so S to Q; as NH to HC, so R to X; as LH to HE, so S to V; and as IH to HA, so Q to T; and let R be at C, S at E, Q at A, T at I, V at L, and X at N; since therefore ED to DC is as R to S, and AF to FE as S to Q, G will be the center of heavy bodies Q, S, R, and B the center of Q, R. And because IH to HA is as Q to T, AB to BC as R to Q (since B is the center of heavy bodies Q, R), and NH to HC as R to X, by the fourth problem, point O will be the center of heavy bodies X, T. By the same reason, because F is the center of gravity of heavy bodies Q, S, and H of heavy bodies Q, T, and S, V, K will be the center of heavy bodies V, T. Likewise, since D is the center of heavy bodies S, R, and H of R, X and S, V, M will be the center of heavy bodies X, V, and therefore the joined lines NK, IM, LO will intersect one another at the same point P, and that will be the center of heavy bodies X, V, T; now point H is the center of heavy bodies S, V, R, X, Q, T, namely of all S, V, R, X, Q, T, and G the center of heavy bodies Q, S, R; (by Lem. 1) therefore the balance-beam GH produced will pass through P, the center of the remaining heavy bodies X, V, T; and GH to HP will be as the three heavy bodies X, V, T to the three Q, S, R, and so concerning the other enunciated ratios.
