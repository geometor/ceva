Lemma III & Proposition VI: Six Cases of Element I (Caravaggio Proof)¶

Lemma III (p. 7, Tab. 1, Fig. 9): Given a straight line divided at a point, one can construct a segment in any compound ratio of given ratios.

Proposition VI · Problem VI (pp. 7–10, Tab. 1, Fig. 4): In the First Element (a triangle :g:`AEC` cut by two intersecting lines :g:`EB`, :g:`CD` at :g:`F`), given any two of the four ratios among sides and transversals, to determine the remaining two ratios.

Ceva enumerates all $\binom{4}{2} = 6$ combinatorial cases systematically via weights $G, H, I$:

  1. Case I: Given $\overline{AD} : \overline{DE}$ and $\overline{AB} : \overline{BC}$, find $\overline{CF} : \overline{FD}$ and $\overline{EF} : \overline{FB}$.

  2. Cases II–VI: All remaining five pairs of given ratios solved uniformly by clearing weight ratios.

Scholium — Pure Euclidean Proof by Pietro Paolo Caravaggio Jr. (p. 10, Tab. 1, Fig. 10): To satisfy geometers who prefer purely Euclidean proofs without mechanics, Ceva records an elegant demonstration of Case I communicated by Pietro Paolo Caravaggio Jr. (filius præceptoris sui). Drawing GH through F parallel to base EC, similar triangles ([VI.4]) immediately express $\overline{CF} : \overline{FD}$ and $\overline{EF} : \overline{FB}$ directly from $\overline{AD} : \overline{DE}$ and $\overline{AB} : \overline{BC}$ without invoking weights.
