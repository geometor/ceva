Proposition IX: Fifteen Combinatorial Cases of Element II¶

Proposition IX · Problem VII (Tab. 1, Fig. 5)

The figure of the Second Element being set out, and the weights understood as suspended in the same way, given any two of the six ratios ED to DA, AB to BC, CK to KE, KF to FA, DF to FC, and EF to FB, it is required to investigate the remaining four.

There are fifteen cases of this problem; for multiplying 6 by 5 makes the product 30, whose half is 15, namely the number of pairs derived from six.

I. First, let two ratios be given: ED to DA as 6 to 7, and AB to BC as 8 to 9; and let those we must exhibit be EF to FB, DF to FC, KF to FA, and EK to KC.

Now in figure EACF, since two ratios ED to DA and AB to BC are given, the remaining two will be known, namely DF to FC as 16 to 39, and EF to FB as 34 to 21; next, because in figure ACEF of the First Element two ratios EF to FB as 34 to 21 and AB to BC as 8 to 9 are given, the remaining two ratios AF to FK as 37 to 18 and EK to KC as 16 to 21 will also be given.

II. But if the given ratios had been AB to BC as 6 to 7 and CK to KE as 8 to 9, the four ratios would likewise be given: BF to FE as 16 to 39, AF to FK as 34 to 21, CF to FD as 37 to 18, and AD to DE as 16 to 21.

III. And if the two given ratios were CK to KE as 6 to 7 and ED to DA as 8 to 9, in the same manner we shall make known the four ratios KF to FA as 16 to 39, CF to FD as 34 to 21, EF to FB as 37 to 18, and CB to BA as 16 to 21.

IV. Let the two given ratios be AF to FK as 21 to 13 and EK to KC as 10 to 11; we must find the remaining four, namely ED to DA, AB to BC, EF to FB, and CF to FD. Since in the First Element whose vertex is C and center F the two ratios AF to FK as 21 to 13 and EK to KC as 10 to 11 are given, the remaining two

EF to FB as 23 to 11 and AB to BC as 10 to 13 will be given; and because again in the First Element whose vertex is E and the same center F the two ratios AF to FK as 21 to 13 and EK to KC as 10 to 11 are given, we shall likewise make known the remaining two: ED to DA will be as 13 to 11, and CF to FD as 24 to 10.

V. But if the given ratios are CF to FD as 21 to 13 and AD to DE as 10 to 11, the four ratios will likewise be known: AF to FK as 23 to 11, CK to KE as 10 to 13, AB to BC as 13 to 11, and EF to FB as 24 to 10.

VI. And if the two given ratios had been EF to FB as 21 to 13 and CB to BA as 10 to 11, the remaining four ratios would be had in the exact same way, namely CK to KE as 13 to 11, AF to FK as 24 to 10, CF to FD as 23 to 11, and AB to BC as 10 to 13.

VII. Having the two ratios EF to FB of equality and CF to FD as 52 to 18, we shall also find the remaining four. For in the First Element whose vertex is A and center F, both ratios EF to FB and CF to FD are given, wherefore we shall not be ignorant of the two remaining, namely CB to BA as 17 to 18, and AD to DE as 35 to 17. Therefore the two ratios EF to FB of equality and AB to BC as 18 to 17 are given; hence in the First Element whose vertex is C and center F, the two remaining will also be given: EK to KC as 18 to 35, and KF to FA as 17 to 53.

VIII. And if the given ratios had been EF to FB of equality and AF to FK as 52 to 18, we know by the same reasoning EK to KC as 17 to 18, CB to BA as 35 to 17, AD to DE as 18 to 35, and DF to FC as 17 to 53.

IX. But if the two known ratios are CF to FD as 1 to 1, and AF to FK as 52 to 18, the remaining four will not lie hidden: AD to DE will be as 17 to 18, EK to KC as 35 to 17, CB to BA as 18 to 35, and BF to FE as 17 to 53.

X. Let the two given ratios be ED to DA as 39 to 105 and BF to FE as 105 to 69; we shall know the remaining four as well: for in the First Element whose vertex is A and center F, the two exposed ratios are given, therefore the two remaining ratios will also become plain, namely CF to FD as 144 to 30, and CB to BA as 39 to 30. Yet since again in another First Element, whose vertex is C and whose center is the same F, two ratios are known—BF to FE as 105 to 69, and CB to BA as 39 to 30—

the remaining ratios will likewise be given, AF to FK as 135 to 39, and EK to KC as 30 to 105.

The remaining five cases are wholly similar to this tenth case above, although their position appears different, and therefore they are solved in the same manner, which was to be shown.
