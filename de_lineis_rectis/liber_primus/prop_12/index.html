Proposition XII: Ten Combinatorial Cases of Element III¶

Proposition XII · Problem IX (Tab. 2, Fig. 15)

Having set forth the figure of the Third Element, out of the five ratios of which we treated in that very element, any three being given, let it be required to make manifest the remaining two.

Of this problem there are ten cases; for the number five is resolved into ten binary pairs; since multiplying 5 by 4 makes 20, half of which is 10.

Let first be given the three ratios AF to FE as 3 to 4, BG to GE of equality, and CD to DE as 5 to 4; we must investigate the remaining two, that is, FG to GD and AB to BC; and because in this case only AC must not be parallel to FD, let us draw from point A the parallel AHI.

Since BG to GH is compounded of the ratio BG to GE and GE to GH, and GE to GH is as EF to FA, BG to GH will be compounded of the ratios BG to GE and EF to FA, namely of the ratios 4 to 4 and 4 to 3; and therefore BG to GH will be as 4 to 3; and by dividing, BH to HG as 1 to 3; but HG to HE is as 3 to 7; therefore by equality BH to HE will be as 1 to 7. Again, CD to DI is compounded of the proportions CD to DE and DE to DI; but ED to DI is as EF to FA; therefore CD to DI is compounded of the ratios CD to DE and EF to FA, that is, of the ratios 5 to 4 and 4 to 3; CD to DI is as 5 to 3; and by dividing, CI to ID as 2 to 3; and ID to IE, that is, AF to FE, is as 3 to 7; therefore by equality, as CI to IE, so 2 to 7; since therefore in the figure of the First Element, whose vertex is C and center H, the two ratios CI to IE as 2 to 7 and BH to HE as 1 to 7 are given, the remaining two also become clear, that is, IH to HA as 7 to 9 and CB to BA of equality; and as IH to HA, so DG to GF; therefore we have given the remaining two ratios FG to GD as 9 to 7, and CB to BA as 1 to 1, namely of equality.

II. In the lower figure of the same Third Element let there be given three ratios CD to DE as 7 to 4; CB to BA as 2 to 4; and DG to GF as 5 to 11; it is necessary to lay open the remaining two ratios AF to FE and EG to GB.

The proportion EA to EF, namely of weight F to A, is compounded of the proportions of weights F to D, D to C, and C to A, namely of straight lines DG to GF, EC to ED, and AB to BC; that is, of the ratios 5 to 11 to 4 to 2; wherefore EA to EF is as 5 to 2, and by dividing, AF to FE will be as 3 to 2.

Again EB to EG, that is, weight G to B, is compounded of the proportions of weights G to D, D to C, and C to B, namely of straight lines DF to FG, EC to ED, and AB to AC; that is, of the ratios 16 to 11 to 4 to 6; thus EB to EG is as 16 to 6, and by dividing BG to GE will be as 10 to 6.

  1. But if the three given ratios be AF to FE as 7 to 4, AB to BC as 2 to 4, and FG to GD as 5 to 11, in the very same way we shall show CD to DE as 3 to 2, and BG to GE as 5 to 3.

  2. Let there be given three ratios BG to GE as 8 to 15; CD to DE as 5 to 7; and FG to GD as 12 to 11; we must lay open the remaining two, that is, AF to FE and AB to BC (Tab. 1, Fig. 6).

Straight line AC to AB, namely weight B to C, is compounded of the ratios of weights B to G, G to F, and F to C, namely of straight lines EG to EB, FD to FG, and CE to ED; that is, of the ratios 15 to 23 to 12 to 7; therefore AC to AB is as 15 to 7, and by dividing, CB to BA is as 8 to 7. Now having given the three proportions ED to DC as 7 to 5; FG to GD as 12 to 11; and CB to BA as 8 to 7; in the manner which we used in the first part of the second case of this problem, we shall find AF to FE to be as 3 to 8.

V. But if the known ratios shall be BG to GE as 8 to 15; AF to FE as 5 to 7; and DG to GF as 12 to 11; we shall similarly show AB to BC to be as 8 to 7; and CD to DE as 3 to 8.

VI. Having the three ratios EG to GB of equality, FG to GD as 7 to 3; and AB to BC as 3 to 2, we must find the remaining two AF to FE and CD to DE. Compounded is EC to ED, namely weight D to C, of the ratios of weights D to G, G to B, and B to C, that is, of the ratios 7 to 10 to 5 to 3; therefore EC to ED is as 7 to 3, and by dividing CD to DE as 4 to 3; thus having given the three ratios BG to GE of equality, CD to DE as 4 to 3; and FG to GD as

7 to 3; from the first part of the second case will also be given the ratio AF to FE, and it will be as 1 to 2.

VII. Let there be given three ratios AF to FE as 1 to 6; ED to DC as 5 to 4; and AB to BC as 5 to 6; we must make known the remaining two, namely FG to GD and EG to GB. Compounded is FG to GD, namely weight D to F, of the ratios of heavy bodies D to C, C to A, and A to F, namely of straight lines EC to ED, AB to BC, and EF to EA; that is, of the ratios 9 to 5 to 6 to 7, wherefore FG to GD is as 9 to 7.

Then EB to EG, that is, weight G to B, is compounded of the ratios of heavy bodies G to D, D to C, and C to B, namely of straight lines FD to FG, EC to ED, and AB to AC; that is, of the ratios 16 to 9 to 5 to 11; therefore EB to EG is as 16 to 11; and by dividing, BG to GE will be as 5 to 11.

VIII. Having the three ratios AF to FE, again as 1 to 6, FG to GD as 9 to 7; ED to DC as 5 to 4; we must investigate the remaining two, namely AB to BC and EG to GB. Compounded is AB to BC, that is, heavy body C to A, of the ratios of heavy bodies C to D, D to F, and F to A, namely of straight lines ED to EC, FG to GD, and EA to FE; that is, of the ratios 5 to 9 to 7 to 6; therefore AB to BC is as 5 to 6. Next, since weight F - A to weight D - C is compounded of the ratios of weights F - A to F, F to D, and D to D - C, namely of straight lines AF to AE, DG to GF, and CE to DC; that is, of the ratios 1 to 7 to 9 to 4; F - A to D - C will be as 1 to 4; and by compounding, E to D - C will be as 5 to 4; and because D - C to G is compounded of the ratios of heavy bodies D - C to D and D to G, that is, of straight lines DC to CE and FG to FD; that is, of the ratios 4 to 9 to 16; D - C to G will be as 4 to 16; but previously E to D - C was as 5 to 4; therefore by equality E to G, that is, BG to BE, will be as 5 to 16; and by converting, and thence dividing, EG to GB will be as 11 to 5, which &c.

IX. Let there be proposed three ratios CD to DE as 4 to 5; EG to GB as 11 to 5; and AB to BC as 5 to 6; and let it be required to search out the remaining two. Compounded is AF to FE, namely weight F - A to A, of the ratios of heavy bodies F - A to E, E to B, and B to A, and because E to D - C is constituted of the ratios of heavy bodies E to B, B to C, and C to D - C, namely of straight lines BG to GE, AC to AB, and ED to DC; that is, of the ratios of the numbers 5 to 11 to 5 to 4; weight E to D - C will be as 5 to 4; but by conversion of ratio, and thence

by converting, F - A to E will be as 1 to 5; but the ratios of weights E to B and B to A, namely of straight lines BG to GE and AC to AB, are in sequence as 5 to 11 to 6; therefore by equality AF to FE will be as 1 to 6.

Similarly, because FD to GF, that is, heavy body G to D, is compounded of the ratios of heavy bodies G to B, B to C, and C to D, namely of straight lines EB to EG, AC to AB, ED to EC; or of the ratios 16 to 11 to 5 to 9; FD to GF will be as 16 to 9; and by dividing, DG to GF will be as 7 to 9.

X. Finally, if there be given three ratios AF to FE as 4 to 5; EG to GB as 11 to 5; and CB to BA as 5 to 6; by the same reasoning we shall make manifest CD to DE as 1 to 6; and FG to GD as 7 to 9, which &c.
