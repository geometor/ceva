Proposition XIII: First Pyramid Theorem of Element III¶

Proposition XIII · Theorem IV (Tab. 2, Fig. 16)

Having set forth the figure of the same Third Element, let two lines AD, FC be joined moreover. I say that GD to GF is as the pyramid whose base is triangle EAD and altitude BC to the pyramid whose base is triangle CEF and altitude AB.

Since DG to GF, namely weight F to D, is compounded of the ratios F to A, A to C, and C to D, namely of straight lines AE to EF, CB to BA, and ED to EC, or of the same permuted, that is, of the ratios AE to EF, ED to EC, and CB to BA; and from the former two is compounded triangle AED to triangle CFE (since the angle at E is common), DG to GF will be compounded of the ratios of triangle AED to EFC and of straight line CB to BA; that is, DG to GF is as the pyramid whose base is triangle AED and altitude BC, to the pyramid whose base is triangle EFC and altitude AB, which was to be proved.

Corollary.

It is clear that if CB be equal to AB, GD is to GF as triangle EAD is to triangle CFE.
