Proposition XXIII: Seven Concurrent Lines in a Tetrahedron¶

Proposition XXIII · Theorem XII

Let there be a pyramid whose vertex is G, and whose base is triangle AEC; and with the figure of the Second Element described in triangle EGC, such as is EIGLCDK, let us join the two lines AI, AL; and any point N being taken on AG, let us draw NE cutting AI at H, and NC cutting AL at M; and with lines GHF, GMB drawn moreover, let us understand CF, AD, EB to be joined. I say first that these lines cut one another at the same point P; moreover, that the lines conceived within the pyramid, AK, EM, CH, GP, FL, IB, ND, also meet one another simultaneously at the same point O.

Now that these things may clearly appear, let us as usual suspend from the angles of the same pyramid, in the reciprocal ratio of the lengths, the heavy bodies R, S, Q, T; this being posited, any three to the remaining one, or any two to the remaining two, or any two to one, or finally one to one, will be reciprocally among themselves with a certain wonderful harmony as the lengths from which they hang.

(Tab. 3, Fig. 28) Let it be made as GI to IE, so weight S to Q; as CL to LG, so weight Q to T; and as AN to NG, so weight Q to R; and let R be at A, S at E, T at C, and Q at G; because in the Second Element whose heavy bodies are S, Q, T and center K, GI is to IE as weight S to Q, and CL to LG as Q to T, it will be by Problem II also CD to DE as S to T; and by the same reason in the Second Element whose heavy bodies are R, Q, S, EF will be to FA as R to S. Likewise in the Second Element whose heavy bodies are R, Q, T, and center M, the straight line AB will be to BC as weight T to R; therefore since EF to FA is as R to S, CD to DE as S to T, and AB to BC as T to R, the lines EB, AD, CF will intersect one another at the same point, such as P: and out of them together with triangle AEC will be formed a Second Element, whose heavy bodies are R, S, T, and center P; wherefore on line GP will be the center of gravity of all the heavy bodies R, S, T, Q: and likewise because H is the center of gravity of heavy bodies R, S, Q, K of heavy bodies S, Q, T, and M of heavy bodies R, Q, T, the same center of heavy bodies R, S, T, Q will be on

each of the balance-beams AK, EM, CH; but because this center is unique, the lines GP, AK, EM, CH will meet one another at the same point O, namely at the center of gravity of all the heavy bodies R, S, T, Q. Next, because L is the center of gravity of heavy bodies Q, T, and F of heavy bodies R, S, point O, inasmuch as it is the center of gravity of heavy bodies R, S, T, Q, will lie on balance-beam FL; say the same of balance-beams IB, DN; therefore all the balance-beams, or lines GP, AK, EM, CH, FL, IB, DN, will meet one another at the same point O, their common center; and hence as CO to OH, so the three heavy bodies R, Q, S, that is, weight H to C; as EO to OM, so weight M, namely the three heavy bodies R, Q, T, to S; as AO to OK, so weight K, namely the heavy bodies S, Q, T, to R; and as OG to OP, so weight P, or the heavy bodies S, T, R, to Q; likewise as weight L to F, that is, the heavy bodies Q, T to R, S, so FO to OL; as weight N to D, namely the heavy bodies R, Q to S, T, so DO to ON; and as weight I to B, namely the heavy bodies Q, S to R, T, so BO to OI. And the remaining ratios become known from the Second Elements whose centers are H, M, K, P, and therefore the whole proposition is manifest.
