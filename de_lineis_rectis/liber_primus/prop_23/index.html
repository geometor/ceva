Proposition XXIII: Seven Concurrent Lines in a Tetrahedron¶

Proposition XXIII · Theorem XII — The Seven-Line Spatial Theorem (pp. 31–32, Tab. 3, Fig. 28)

In any solid tetrahedron (triangular pyramid) :g:`AEGC`, suspend four arbitrary non-zero weights :g:`R` at :g:`A`, :g:`S` at :g:`E`, :g:`T` at :g:`C`, and :g:`Q` at :g:`G`. Let :g:`B, D, P, N, L, I` be the six static lever fulcrums on the six edges, and let :g:`H, K, M, F` be the four static centers of gravity of the four triangular faces.

By Axiom I–II, the unique spatial center of gravity O of all four masses can be reached by grouping the four vertices in seven distinct associative ways:

  1. Four Vertex-to-Opposite-Face Cevians: AK, EM, CH, and GF (joining each vertex to the static center of the opposite triangular face);

  2. Three Skew-Edge Bimedians: PL, DN, and BI (joining the static fulcrums of each pair of opposite skew edges).

All seven spatial straight lines pass simultaneously through the exact same unique point O (mira quadam concordia), generalizing Federico Commandino's equal-weight $3:1$ centroid theorem (1565) to arbitrary weighted tetrahedra!
