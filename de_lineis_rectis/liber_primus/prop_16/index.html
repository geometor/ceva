Proposition XVI: Base Ratio in Element III¶

Proposition XVI · Theorem VII (p. 23, Tab. 2, Fig. 17)

With the same things posited, I say that BC to CA is as the pyramid whose base is triangle FEB and altitude GD, to the pyramid whose base is triangle EAG and altitude FD.

Compounded is the ratio CB to CA, namely of weight A to B, of the ratios of weights A to F, F to G, and G to B, namely of straight lines EF to EA, DG to DF, and EB to EG, and also from the same taken perturbedly, that is, from EF to EA, EB to EG, and DG to DF; and triangle FEB to triangle AEG is compounded of the ratios FE to EA and EB to EG (since angle AEB is common); therefore BC to CA will be as the pyramid whose base is triangle FEB, altitude GD, to the pyramid whose base is triangle AEG, and altitude DF, which was &c.
