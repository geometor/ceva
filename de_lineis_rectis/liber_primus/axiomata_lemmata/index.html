Axioms, Postulate, and Lemmas I–II¶

Static Construction of Straight Lines Mutually Intersecting — Book I

Axiom I. Heavy bodies suspended from their common center of gravity weigh just as if their whole weight were in the aforesaid center of gravity.

Axiom II. Weights in the same position have a single center of gravity.

Postulate. Let it be granted that, any weight being proposed, another can be found to which the given weight has any commanded ratio.

Lemma I. Given several weights in any position, if from the center of gravity of one or more of them a balance-beam be drawn which passes through the center of gravity of all, that beam produced will pass through the center of gravity of the remaining weight.

(Tab. 1, Fig. 1) Let the weights be \(A, B, C, D\), let the center of gravity of them all be \(E\), and that of weights \(A, D\) be \(F\), and let \(FE\) be drawn; I say that if it be produced toward the parts of the remaining weights \(B, C\), it will fall upon their center; for if this is not so, let \(G\) be the center of gravity of weights \(B, C\), so that the join \(FG\) lies outside the line \(FE\). Since therefore \(FG\) joins the centers of gravity of weights \(B, C\) and \(A, D\), if it be made as \(BC\) to \(AD\), so the length \(FM\) to \(MG\), the center of gravity of the same four weights \(B, C, D, A\) remaining in their former position will lie on the same balance-beam \(FG\); and since this center is unique, it will not be \(E\), as was supposed.

Corollary. Hence it is manifest that if two heavy bodies \(B, C\) be joined by some balance-beam \(BC\), their center of gravity will lie in the common

(Tab. 1, Fig. 2) intersection \(H\) of the balance-beam \(BC\) joining the centers of the heavy bodies \(B, C\), and the line \(FH\) passing through the aforesaid centers \(F\) and \(E\). For the center of the heavy bodies \(B, C\) lies on \(FH\), as has been proved, as well as on \(BC\); therefore it will be \(H\).

Lemma II.

Let there be two weights A and B, whose center of gravity is F on the balance-beam AB; I say that weight A is to B as BF to FA.

(Tab. 1, Fig. 3; Archimedes, Eq. I.6) For if it is not so, let it be as A to B, so \(BK\) to \(KA\); therefore K will be the center of gravity of weights A and B; and since this center is unique, it will not be F, as was supposed.
