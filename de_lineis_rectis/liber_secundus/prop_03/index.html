Proposition III: Proportional Side Divisions and Bisected Base¶

Proposition III · Theorem III (p. 39, Tab. 5, Fig. 39)

If in a triangle two sides containing an angle be cut similarly, and the base be bisected, and lines be drawn from the angles to the opposite points of section, they will cut one another at the same point;

so that the figure composed of those same lines is that of the second element.

Let there be triangle ACE, and as AB to BC, so let ED be placed to DC; let base AE be bisected at G, and with AD, EB joined cutting one another at F, let us join line CF: I say that this produced passes through point G; if this be not true, let it fall if possible upon H; and since, by the second element, EH to HA, namely weight A to E, is compounded of the ratios of weights A to C to E, that is of straight lines CB to BA and ED to DC, or rather of straight lines CB to BA and BA to BC; therefore EH will be to HA as BC to BC, and hence EH will be equal to HA. But EG is also equal to GA; therefore as EH to HA, so EG to GA, and componendo, as EA to AH, so the same EA to AG; wherefore AG will be equal to AH, namely the whole to its part, which is absurd. Therefore line CF will not fall on H, but on G, which was to be proved.
