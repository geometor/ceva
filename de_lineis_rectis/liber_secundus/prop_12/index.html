Proposition XII: Cubic Area Ratio on Three Parabola Tangents¶

Proposition XII · Theorem XI (pp. 48–49, Tab. 6, Fig. 50)

If a line touching a parabola cuts two other tangents, and these points of intersection and the points of contact of those same tangents are joined by two mutually intersecting lines; the cubes on the portions of the first tangent intercepted between the parabola and the two remaining tangents will stand to one another as the triangles taken homologously, whose bases are the remaining tangents, and whose vertex is the same point in which the said joined lines intersected one another.

Let the straight line FD touch the parabola AEC at E, and let it cut the two tangents FA and DC at F and D; then let AD and FC be joined, intersecting one another at H. I say that the cube on FE to the cube on ED is in the same ratio as triangle FAH, whose base is tangent AF, is to triangle DHC, whose base is the other tangent DC. Let AC and EH be joined, and let the tangents AF and CD produced meet at G; let EH produced both ways cut AC at B, which on the other side will pass through G. Since in the Fourth Element, in which H is the center of the weights EB, namely of the weights F, A, C, D, the line AB to BC—namely weight C to A—is compounded from the ratios of the weights C to F, F to D, and D to A, namely of the lines FH to HC, ED to EF, and AH to HD, or from the same taken in perturbed order, that is, from the ratios FH to HC, AH to HD, and DE to EF; yet the same AB to BC is compounded from the duplicate ratio of FE to ED, or from the triplicate of the same ratio FE to ED together with the inverse ratio of DE to EF. Therefore, if the ratio DE to EF be taken away on both sides, there will remain the triplicate of FE to ED, that is, the cube on FE to the cube on ED, compounded from the ratios FH to HC and AH to HD; since triangle FHA to DHC is likewise compounded from these same ratios, it is evident that the cube on FE to the cube on ED is as triangle FHA to triangle DHC, Q.E.D.

$$\triangle FHA : \triangle DHC = \overline{FE}^3 : \overline{ED}^3.$$
