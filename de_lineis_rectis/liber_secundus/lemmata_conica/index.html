Lemmas I–III: Polygons Circumscribed About Circles and Ellipses¶

Book II · Lemma I (Tab. 4, Figs. 32–34)

If any rectilinear figure be circumscribed about a circle or an ellipse so that each of its sides touches the aforesaid circle or ellipse, then the sides themselves are so divided by the points of contact

that the ratio of the parts of one side is compounded of the ratios of the similarly taken parts of the remaining sides in succession.

Let triangle ECA, or quadrilateral ACEG, or any other polygon be circumscribed about the circle or ellipse BDF, and let the points of contact be

B, D, F, ✠. We must show that CD to DE is compounded in the triangle of the ratios AF to FE and CB to BA; and in the quadrilateral of the three ratios GF to FE, A to G, and CB to BA.

In a circle, tangent DC equals CB, BA equals AF, and FE equals ED (when the circumscribed figure is a triangle); hence, since CD to DE is compounded of the ratios DC to CB, CB to BA, BA to AF, AF to FE, and FE to ED, if the ratios of equality DC to CB, BA to AF, and FE to ED be removed, there will remain the ratio compounded only of the two ratios CB to BA and AF to FE, or of the same taken perturbedly, namely AF to FE and CB to BA. By the same reasoning we shall prove in a quadrilateral circumscribed about a circle that the ratio CD to DE is compounded of the ratios GF to FE, A to G, and CB to BA: therefore what we proposed is manifest whenever a triangle, or a quadrilateral, or any other polygon is circumscribed about a circle.

But if it be about an ellipse, we can find some cylinder of which this proposed ellipse is a section (and this we shall prove below); let the base of this cylinder be circle

f. 0053 · p. 36

GLI (Tab. 4, Fig. 35), and let the said ellipse be the section of the cylinder denoted by the letters FDB, about which let triangle EAC be circumscribed; from the points of contact FDB of the circumscribed figure, let lines FG, DL, BI fall to points G, I, L of the circumference; next let us draw planes through EA and FG, AC and BI, and EC and DL: these will touch the surface of the cylinder along the lines FG, DL, BI. Finally, extending the plane of the base, let all the common intersections of these planes be ME, AH, CK, as well as MGH, HIK, KLM. And since the former planes were drawn through the lines FG, BI, DL, which are parallel to one another, the common intersections of those planes, such as AH, CK, EM, will also be parallel to one another and to the aforesaid sides FG, BI, DL; therefore AF will be to FE as HG to GM, CB to BA as KI to IH, and CD to DE as KL to LM; in circle GLI the ratio KL to LM is compounded of the ratios HG to GM and KI to IH; therefore CD to DE will also be compounded of AF to FE and CB to BA. Similarly we shall show in quadrilateral CBAGFED that the ratio CD to DE is compounded of the ratios EF to FG, A to G, and CB to BA. We shall use the same process if any other figure be circumscribed, Q.E.D.

Lemma II (Tab. 4, Fig. 36)

It remains for us to show what we assumed in the preceding lemma: namely, given any ellipse, say FDB, to find a certain cylinder and so cut it by a plane that the resulting section is similar and equal to the given ellipse.

Let the axis of the given ellipse be line D and the second diameter FB, to which let AC be taken equal, and about it let circle AC be described; now any right cylinder (not scalene) erected upon this circle as base will be the one sought. Let us take it to be one whose axial parallelogram is AMGC, with diagonal GA greater than axis D and side GM less than the axis: therefore if with center G and radius GE equal to D a circle be described, its circumference will divide line AM; let E therefore be the point of intersection, and let GE joined be produced toward the base so as to cut AC produced at O, from which point let us draw line ON in the same plane of the circle perpendicular

f. 0054 · p. 37

to OC; and passing a plane through lines ON and GEO, let section GIEH be made in the cylinder; since this is an ellipse, we shall show that it is also similar and equal to the given FBD. For let us bisect straight line EG at P, through which as center of the ellipse let straight line IPH pass parallel to NO, produced both ways to the surface of the cylinder; this will therefore be an ordinate at right angles to axis EG, and thus line IPH will be called the second diameter, which indeed will be equal to AC or FB. Now if we conceive ellipse EIGH superposed upon ellipse DFB so that line D coincides with line GE, line FB will also coincide with IH, and thus (as we shall also show) ellipse will fit upon ellipse, Q.E.D.

Lemma III (Tab. 4, Fig. 37)

If, when one ellipse is conceived as placed upon another, two conjugate diameters of the one coincide with two of the other, the aforesaid ellipses will be similar and equal to one another.

Let ellipse FABCE be placed upon ellipse BACED, whose conjugate diameters AE, BC are common. I say that these sections coincide with one another; for if it be possible, let some point such as F be assigned which lies on one ellipse only. Let line FDG be drawn ordinatewise to diameter EA, cutting the other ellipse at D and the said diameter at G: thence from point E let us erect at right angles to AE straight line EH, a third proportional to the two EA and BC; therefore EH will be the latus rectum and EA the transverse diameter; yet since both ordinate FG and DG are equal in square to the same rectangle GEH deficient by a figure similar and similarly placed to that contained by lines AE, EH, the said two ordinates will also be equal in length, namely the whole to a part, which is absurd; wherefore no point can be assigned which does not exist in both ellipses, and thus the aforesaid ellipses will be similar and equal to one another, Q.E.D.
