Lemmas I–III: Polygons Circumscribed About Circles and Ellipses¶

Book II — Conic Sections, Reciprocal Triangles & Optical Mensuration

Lemma I (pp. 35–36, Tab. 4, Figs. 32–35): If any polygon (triangle, quadrilateral, etc.) is circumscribed about a circle or an ellipse, the product of alternating tangent segments equals the product of the complementary tangent segments.

For a circle, equal tangents from each external vertex cancel pairwise identically; for an ellipse, Ceva invokes parallel cylindrical projection (sectio cylindrica, Fig. 35, citing Giovanni Alfonso Borelli's Euclides Restitutus / Apollonius Conics): projecting a circle HIK along the parallel generators of a cylinder onto an oblique elliptical section ABC preserves every affine collinearity ratio along tangent lines!

Lemma II (pp. 36–37, Tab. 4, Fig. 36): Given any ellipse, to construct a right circular cylinder whose oblique planar section is congruent to that ellipse, citing Serenus of Antinoöpolis (De Sectione Cylindri).

Lemma III (p. 37, Tab. 4, Fig. 37): Two ellipses sharing equal conjugate diameters enclosing equal angles coincide identically, citing Apollonius (Conics I.15).
