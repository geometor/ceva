Proposition IX & Lemmas IV–V: Inscribing an Ellipse Given Two Contact Points¶

Proposition IX · Problem I (pp. 44–46, Tab. 5, Fig. 44)

To a proposed triangle, to inscribe an ellipse, or when it is possible, a circle, so that out of the three points of contact any two be given.

f. 0062 · p. 45

Let there be triangle ABC, and in it the given points F, E, through which the ellipse to be drawn must touch lines AB, CB. Let the problem be supposed as done, and let the inscribed ellipse be FED, touching the remaining side AC at D. Since therefore CD to DA is compounded of the geometrically given ratios FB to FA, and CE to EB, point D will be given; wherefore if lines DF, FE, ED be joined, they too will be given; and so also the joined AH, BI, CK, which will be diameters of the same ellipse. And since the center of the said ellipse exists on each of them, and they meet one another at one and the same point G, this intersection will be given, and therefore the aforesaid center G will be given. Lines AG, BG, CG will therefore be those which are said to be from the center of the section; wherefore if one of them be produced, namely FG from point G, and on the production GL be marked equal to FG, point L will be given, together with diameter FL. And since the given point E is placed on the section, and tangent BF is given in position, that which is drawn from the same point parallel to BF, meeting diameter FL at M, will be determined in position and length, and will be applied ordinate-wise to the same diameter FL. Now let it be made as the given FM to ME, so ME to another line Y, to which let MN be cut equal, erected perpendicularly from the given point M to FL given in position; and with line FO drawn indefinitely from the given point F parallel to MN, let LN be joined, and produced until it meets FO at O. This being placed, point O will be given; and rectangle FMN will be equal to the square of the ordinate ME, which rectangle is applied to line FO, and has for its breadth FM intercepted between ordinate EM and contact F, and falls short by a figure similar and similarly placed to that which is contained by diameter FL and line FO; wherefore diameter FL will be the transverse side of the figure, and FO the upright side. If therefore, given two terminated straight lines FO, FL at right angles to one another, we find an ellipse about diameter FL, so that the vertex is point F at a right angle, and the ordinates in angle BFL are equal in square, as ME, to the rectangles applied to FO having as breadth the line intercepted between the vertex F of the section and the ordinates themselves, and falling short by a figure similar and similarly placed to that which is contained by lines FO and FL, this ellipse will touch line BA at F and will pass through point E. I say moreover that such an ellipse will so pass through E that it does not

f. 0063 · p. 46

cut, but touches BC and AC, and therefore that the problem has been satisfied.

These things, however, will become clear by two lemmas, retaining the same letters and hypothesis.

Lemma IV. (Tab. 6, Fig. 47, p. 46)

If the ellipse FED passing through point E does not touch line BC, let it touch if possible another line BK, and let FK, KE be joined. Since therefore FB touches the section at F, the diameter BG will bisect at L the line FK which joins the contacts F, K, and FL will be equal to LK; but by hypothesis FI is also equal to IE; therefore line EK will be parallel to diameter BG, and hence they will not meet. But because DC to DA is compounded of the ratios BF to FA and CE to EB, and FD, FE, ED are bisected at N, I, K, the joined lines AN, BI, CK will cut one another at the same point within triangle FED; and on that account point G will lie below line FE. Yet it also lies below FK, because the two tangents BF, BK meet; therefore line KE will not be parallel to diameter BG, which cannot happen. Therefore the ellipse DFE will touch line BEC at point E.

Lemma V. (Tab. 6, Figs. 48, 49, p. 46)

If an ellipse whose center is G, touching two straight lines BFA and BEC at E and F, does not also touch ADC at D, let ARO be the line touching the section. And because OR to RA is compounded of the ratios BF to FA and OE to EB, it is established that the point of contact is at R. Therefore, since AF and AR touch the ellipse, the diameter AG will bisect FR, which joins the contacts; yet FD was also bisected by hypothesis at N. Therefore RD will be parallel to diameter AG, wherefore FL too will be bisected by that same diameter at center G (for by Euclid VI.2, as FN to ND, so FG to GL); and hence line GF, that is GP, will be equal to GL, the whole to its part, which is absurd, since point L lies within the ellipse and therefore within line GP.
