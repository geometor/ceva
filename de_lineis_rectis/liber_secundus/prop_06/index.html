Proposition VI: Parallelism via Midpoints and Exterior Vertex Line¶

Proposition VI · Theorem VI (pp. 41–43, Tab. 5, Fig. 42)

If the sides and base of a triangle be bisected, and from the section of the base two indefinite straight lines be drawn through the divisions of the sides; then, through the vertex of the triangle, outside it, another straight line be drawn cutting those lines which we drew from the said point of the base; finally from the two points of those divisions to the angles

f. 0059 · p. 42

adjacent to the base, and toward the same parts, two other lines be drawn, these will be parallel to one another.

Let there be triangle DAG, whose sides DA, AG and base DG are bisected at points E, K, F; and with FE, FK joined let them be produced indefinitely; and let straight line BAH be drawn both ways through vertex A, cutting the aforesaid indefinite lines however it may at points B, H, yet so that it lies wholly outside triangle AGD, and let BD, HG be joined; I say that these are parallel to one another. For either BH and DG are parallel to one another, or not; if they are parallel, let EK be joined, which will be parallel to DG, and therefore also to straight line BHA. Wherefore since DG is to EK as DA is to AE, or as AD to DE, or BF to FE by the supposed parallels; and as BF to FE, so BH to EK; DG will have the same ratio to EK as BH has to the same EK; and therefore DG, BH will be equal, and they are also parallel; hence BHGD will be a parallelogrammic space, and so HG, BD will themselves also be parallel: But if BH, DG be not parallel, let them be produced until they meet at point I. And since BI to IH is compounded of the ratios BI to IA to IH, and straight line BI to IA, that is weight A to B in the first element, whose weights are B, I, D, and center E, is compounded of the ratios of weights A to E to B, namely of straight lines ED to DA, and FB to FE; and as FB to FE, that is BH to HA, so FH to HK on account of parallelogram AKFE, BI to IA is compounded of the ratios ED to DA, and FH to HK. Likewise, since in the first element, whose weights are AIF, and center K, IA to IH, namely weight H to A, is compounded of the ratios of weights H to K to A, namely of straight lines KF to FH, and AG to GK; and as AG to GK, so DA to DE, therefore IA to IH is compounded of the ratios KF to FH, and DA to DE; for this reason the former ratio BI to IH will be formed from the ratios ED to DA, FH to HK, KF to FH, and DA to DE; or from the same taken in perturbed order, that is from the ratios ED to DA to DE, KF to FH to HK; and so as BI to IH, so KF to HK.

Again DI to IG is compounded of the ratios DI to IF to IG: But in the first element, whose weights are DIB, and center E, straight line DI to IF, that is weight F to D, is compounded of the ratios of weights F to E to D, namely of straight lines EB to BF, or AB to BH; or rather KF to FH, and DA to AE, and likewise in the first element,

f. 0060 · p. 43

whose weights are FIA, and center K, straight line IF to IG is compounded of the ratios of weights G to K to F, namely of straight lines KA to AG, and FH to HK; therefore the former ratio DI to IG is compounded of the ratios of straight lines KF to FH, DA to AE, KA to AG, and FH to HK; or from the same in perturbed order, namely from the ratios KF to FH to HK, KA to AG, and DA to AE, or AG to KA. Wherefore DI to IG will be as KF to HK, namely in the same ratio in which BI was to IH; therefore BD will be parallel to GH, which &c.
