Proposition VIII: Segment Ratio on a Cevian Intersecting a Chord¶

Proposition VIII · Theorem VIII (p. 44, Tab. 5, Fig. 46)

If, as above, three sides of a triangle be divided; and two points of division be joined by one straight line, which cuts another line drawn from an angle of the same triangle, yet not from that to which the joined line is subtended, so will that line be divided by this one that its segments are compounded of two ratios, one of which is from the parts of the untouched side, while the other consists of the portions of that line which is the segment of the other side intercepted between the divided line and the angle to which the remaining side of the triangle is subtended.

Let there be triangle ACF, whose three sides AF, FC, CA are divided in order at G, D, B, so that the ratio AG to GF is compounded of the two ratios CD to DF, and AB to BC, and let GD joined cut AE drawn at H; I say that EH to HA is compounded of the ratios CB to BA, and ED to DC. For in the figure of the first element, whose weights are A, F, D, and center H, the ratio of straight line EH to HA, namely of weight A to E, is compounded of the ratios of weights A to F to E, namely of straight lines FG to GA, and DE to DF; but the ratio of straight line FG to GA is compounded (by supposition) of the ratios CB to BA, and FD to DC, therefore the aforesaid ratio EH to HA will be compounded of the ratios CB to BA, FD to DC, and DE to DF, or of the same taken in perturbed order CB to BA, DE to DF to DC; that is, of the proposed ratios CB to BA, and ED to DC, which &c.
