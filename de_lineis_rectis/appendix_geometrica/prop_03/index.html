Proposition III: Twelfth-Semicircle Arcs from Squared Side Ratios¶

Proposition III · Theorem III (Tab. 8, Figs. 69–70)

With the same things supposed, if in the first case DA to AE be in square in the subsesquitertian ratio, and in the second in the subquadruple ratio, arc CH will be the twelfth part of the semicircumference of that circle.

Because first, in the first case, the square on DA is placed to the square EA as 3 to 4, arc HKA will be to the semicircumference of the circle of the same arc as 3 to 4, that is, as 9 to 12. Again, because DA in square is subsesquitertian of EA itself, and triangle DEA stands in the semicircle (since the angle at B is right), DA will be the side of an inscribed equilateral triangle, and therefore angle DEA will be the sixth part of four right angles; its double will be the third part of four right angles, but \(\frac{2}{3}\)

parts of two right angles; wherefore also circumference CKA to the semicircumference of the circle of the same arc will be as 2 to 3, yea as 8 to 12; and by inversion the same semicircumference to the same arc will be as 12 to 8; yet arc HKA to the same semicircumference was as 9 to 12; therefore ex aequali arc HKA to arc CKA will be as 9 to 8; thence by conversion of ratio and by converting, their difference, namely arc CH to arc CKA, will be as 1 to 8; and the same arc CKA to the semicircumference of the same circle whose arc HC is, is as 8 to 12; therefore again ex aequali arc HC to the semicircumference of the circle will be as 1 to 12.

And in the second case, because the square on DA is placed to that on AE as 1 to 4, arc HKA will also be to its semicircumference as 1 to 4; but angle AEC is the third part of two right angles, that is, circumference AHC to the semicircumference of the same circle is as 1 to 3, or as \(1\frac{1}{3}\) to 4; therefore by inversion the said semicircumference to its arc AHC will be as 4 to \(1\frac{1}{3}\); and therefore ex aequali arc HKA to arc AHC will be as 1 to \(1\frac{1}{3}\); wherefore AKH to HC will be as 1 to \(\frac{1}{3}\); but the semicircumference of the circle whose arc is AKH to this same arc is as 4 to 1; therefore again ex aequali the semicircumference to this same arc will be as 4 to \(\frac{1}{3}\), or as 12 to 1, Q.E.D.
