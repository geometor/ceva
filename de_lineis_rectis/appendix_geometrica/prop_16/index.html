Proposition XVI: Center of Gravity of an Infinite Hyperbolic Plane¶

Proposition XVI · Theorem XVI

Let AMCE be a half-section through axis AE of the aforesaid solid, and let rectangle BE be applied to AE so that BA is equal to semi-axis DM of the opposite sections; it is to be shown that point D is the center of gravity of plane BECMA, even though of infinite length toward C, with rectangle AF removed.

Since I showed in the preceding proposition that the infinitely wide round solid generated by the revolution of plane FKC about axis FC is equal to the cylinder generated by the revolution of rectangle ZR about axis Z, together with cylinder GI, whose axis is FC (margin: Tab. 10, Fig. 83), and CD is to CF in length as DM is to MN, or to XZ, in power; if a cylinder be conceived whose altitude is CF and base radius DM, this will be equal to the aforesaid cylinder whose axis is Z; and therefore it is evident that this conceived cylinder is equal to the solid FKCE of infinite width, once there is removed from it

cylinder GI, namely that which is made from the revolution of rectangle CL about axis FC; that is, in the present figure also, it is clear that the cylinder generated by the revolution of rectangle BE about axis AE is equal to the round solid of infinite width produced from the revolution of plane EFCMA about the same axis EA, provided however that from this solid be removed the cylinder whose half-rectangle at the axis is AF. Therefore the moment of rectangle BE will be equal to the moment of plane FCM of infinite length toward C (margin: from the beautiful Lemma 31 of Torricelli in De dimensione parabolae), if the entire plane EBAMCFE be balanced upon straight line EA, and rectangle AF be conceived as of no weight. Therefore, the center of gravity of the aforesaid two planes thus constituted as of one magnitude will be in the finite line EA, and therefore (although it may seem incredible), since this magnitude has a center of gravity, that center will also be in the diameter GC of the same plane magnitude; wherefore it is found at D, the common intersection of lines AE and GC, which was to be demonstrated.
