Proposition XV: Finite Volume of an Infinite Hyperbolic Solid¶

Proposition XV · Theorem XV

An infinitely wide hyperbolic round solid is equal to the cylinder whose opposite bases are common, together with another right cylinder whose altitude is the semi-axis of the solid, while the radius of its base is a line equal to the mean proportional between the whole axis and its half of the opposite sections which are called conjugate, and those are the ones which are seen in the same plane passed through the axis of the solid.

Let there be hyperbola EB, and let its asymptotes ED, DC contain a right angle EDC (margin: Tab. 10, Fig. 82), and having taken any point B on hyperbola EB, from it let BC be drawn parallel to DE; and opposite to figure EBCDE of infinite length toward E let there be conceived EDFGE similarly equal to the aforesaid, so that line FDC is a single straight line and the figure composed of both is EBCFGE endlessly long; then let this composite figure be revolved about axis FC so that an infinitely wide round solid is formed, and let its section through the axis be figure EBIKLGE. Now because to hyperbolas GE, EB, IK, LK the asymptotes FDC, KDE are common, the said four hyperbolas so constituted will be opposite sections, which are called conjugate, and their two conjugate axes will be equal to each other. Therefore let one of them be MDH, and from point H having drawn line HSN parallel to asymptote EK, let NM be joined, which will be parallel to straight line DS, namely to the other asymptote FC (for MH is to HD as NH is to HS), and since angle EDC of rectangle HSD is bisected by its diagonal DH, rectangle HSD will be a square, which since it is about diagonal MH of the other rectangle HNM, this too will be a square; and its side HN will be a mean proportional between the whole axis MH and its half HD. I say that the entire solid EFKC infinitely extended on the sides EK is equal to a right cylinder whose base is equal to the circle about radius HN, and whose axis is

straight line DC, together with cylinder GI, whose axis is FC. Let there be conceived any number of cylindrical surfaces BGLI, HM, QO, about the same axis FC and within the infinitely extended solid EFKC; and because rectangles DCI, DRN, DP are equal to one another (margin: Conics II.22), their quadruples too, namely rectangles LIB, MNH, OPQ, will be equal to one another. Now because cylindrical surface GBIL is to the circle whose radius is XZ as rectangle LIB is to square XZ (margin: Evangelista Torricelli, De sphaera I.5, etc.), namely to rectangular square MNH, which spaces are equal, the said cylindrical surface GLIB will be equal to the circle whose radius is XZ; by the same reasoning cylindrical surface MH will be equal to the circle whose radius is NH; and likewise surface QO will be equal to the circle whose radius is TV; and this always holds wherever points INP be taken. Therefore, since all cylindrical surfaces GLIB, HM, QO, etc. are equal to all circles whose radii are XZ, NH, TV, etc., it is evident that the entire infinitely wide round solid EFKC is equal to a right cylinder whose altitude is DC, namely the semi-axis of the solid, and whose base is the circle whose radius SR, or NM, is a mean proportional between the whole axis HM and its half MD, etc., together with cylinder GLIB, about axis FC, which was to be demonstrated.
