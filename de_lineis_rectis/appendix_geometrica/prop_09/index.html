Proposition IX: General Oblique Cylindrical Portions via Prisms and Pyramid¶

Proposition IX · Theorem IX (pp. 71–73, Tab. 9, Fig. 76)

It is to be shown in this following figure that the prism whose opposite bases are triangles AI3, DF6, together with that prism whose opposite bases are triangles LYI, EFZ, minus four times the pyramid whose base is KXM and vertex V, with that prism likewise removed whose base is triangle AL9 and one side is straight line AD, is equal to the two portions of the cylinder L98ESG and L7ESG.

Let a cylinder be divided by two planes so that sections A98D, AGD are segments of ellipses or circles (margin: Tab. 9, Fig. 76), which cut each other along straight line AD above center V of circle or ellipse AGD; and having bisected AD at T, let TV be joined, which we extend on both sides so that it meets circle or ellipse AGD at points C, G; thence having taken GS equal to CT, through points V, S, and G let parallels to straight line AD be drawn, such as VM, LSE, and HG, which produced on both sides meet the two joined lines AL, DE extended at points I, and F; then draw G8 a side of the cylinder, and from points I, F lines I3, F6 parallel to the same side G8, meeting the line passing through 8 parallel to IGF at points 3, 6: moreover having joined lines 3A, 6D, 8T, let us extend through lines 3I, IA a plane cutting the portion of the cylinder intercepted between the proposed segments, and let it make on its convex surface, lying before our eyes, line 9L. And since 3I is parallel to side G8 of the cylinder, indeed to the axis of the same cylinder, and line ILA is parallel to GC, since

plane C8G extended through the axis is parallel to the plane through 3I, IA; straight line 9L will be a side of the cylinder, and therefore parallel to line G8; thence having drawn from center V line VK, namely the diagonal of the parallelogram contained by lines V, VG in angle VG, let line KX be drawn in the plane of triangle AI3 parallel to 3I, and MX, LY parallel to the same 3A; and let MX, KX meet at point X; and let line Y7Z be parallel to IF. These being supposed, let us conceive a prism whose opposite bases are triangles AI3, DF6; and likewise another having opposite bases triangles LYI, EFZ; thence a pyramid whose base is triangle MKX and vertex point V. I say that the aforesaid two prisms, minus the composite of four times the said pyramid and the prism whose base is triangle AL9 and altitude the same as the aforesaid two prisms have, is equal to the two portions of the cylinder L98ESG, L7ESG.

Since square IA is equal to squares AL, LI together with twice rectangle ALI, square IA will be equal to square AL together with twice rectangle AIL, minus square IL; but twice square MK is equal to twice rectangle AIL; therefore square IA will be equal to square AL together with two squares MK - LI; on that account triangle A3I of the larger prism will be equal to triangle A9L of the cylinder portion, together with twice triangle MKX of the pyramid, minus triangle LYI of the smaller prism (for these triangles are mutually similar and similarly described upon the sides of the said squares). Moreover, because twice rectangle BHP, that is twice square ON, surpasses twice rectangle RPH by twice rectangle QHP and square PH likewise taken twice, and this excess is equal to square QH - QP ✠ PH; twice square NO will be greater than twice rectangle RPH by square QH - QP ✠ PH, or, which is the same, twice square NO - QH ✠ QP - PH will be equal to twice rectangle RPH; and having added in common squares RP, PH, square RH will be equal to twice square NO together with squares QP, RP - QH: for adding square PH to - PH leaves nothing. Wherefore also triangle R4H of the larger prism will be equal to the triangles similar to themselves and similarly described upon the sides of the said squares as bases, namely to twice the triangle whose base is NO of the aforesaid pyramid, together with that whose base is RP of the cylinder portion inscribed in the larger prism, at the same time

with that triangle constructed on base QP of the cylinder portion inscribed in the smaller prism, minus triangle QH2 of the same smaller prism.

Finally, because square TG is equal to itself together with square SG minus the same, triangle TG8 of the larger prism will be equal to the same triangle TG8 of the larger cylinder portion together with triangle SG7 of the smaller cylinder portion, minus the same triangle SG7, inasmuch as it belongs to the smaller prism; since therefore all triangles AI3, RH4, TG8, etc. of the larger half-prism are equal to twice all the triangles of pyramid MXKV, namely MKX and those whose bases are NO, etc., together with all the triangles, namely AL9, and those whose bases are RP, TG, etc. of the larger semiportion of the cylinder inscribed in the aforesaid larger half-prism, together with all the triangles, namely SG7, and those whose bases are QP, etc. of the smaller semiportion of the cylinder inscribed in the smaller half-prism LYG, yet minus all the triangles LIY, QH2, SG7, etc. of the said smaller half-prism; the half-prism whose opposite bases are triangles AI3, TG8 will be equal to twice the pyramid whose base is triangle MKX and vertex V, together with the two semiportions of the cylinder (of which the larger is that which lies between the two triangles AL9, TG8; while the other is contained by the smaller half-prism TLG), minus that same half-prism. And if this smaller half-prism be added in common, while that whose base is triangle AL9 in the same altitude in which the two said half-prisms stand be taken away, there will remain the prism whose base is trapezium L93I at the same aforesaid altitude, which together with the smaller half-prism will be equal to twice the said pyramid together with the two semiportions of the cylinder, of which the larger lies inscribed beneath the said quadrangular half-prism, and the other beneath the smaller; and their doubles, that is prism 93IL6 together with prism YLIZ, will be equal to four times the pyramid whose base is triangle MKX and vertex V, together with the two portions of the cylinder L98ESG, L7ESG; if therefore four times the said pyramid be taken away in common, the said two portions of the cylinder will be equal to the rectilinear solid which remains when the aforesaid quadruple is removed, Q.E.D.
