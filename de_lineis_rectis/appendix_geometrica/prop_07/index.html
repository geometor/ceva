Proposition VII: Cylindrical Hoof as Two-Thirds of Circumscribed Prism¶

Proposition VII · Theorem VII (pp. 67–69, Tab. 9, Fig. 74)

A portion of a cylinder contained by two semi-ellipses or semicircles, whose common section is the second diameter, together with the convex surface of the cylinder intercepted between the same conic semi-sections, is two-thirds of a certain triangular prism circumscribed about it.

Let a parallelepiped be conceived circumscribed about a certain semicylinder whose opposite bases are semicircles (margin: Tab. 9, Fig. 74); and let this together with the semicylinder inscribed in it be cut transversely by some plane, so that section BKQA is made in the parallelepiped, and BCA in the semicylinder, and let line BA be the common section of the cutting plane and the axial parallelogram on which the semicylinder stands; then let another plane pass through BA, cutting off both solids, and let it make section BLPA in the parallelepiped, and BDA in the semicylinder. I say that the portion of the cylinder contained by the two semicylinder sections or semi-ellipses BCA, BDA, and by the curved surface of the cylinder intercepted between those same conic semi-sections, is two-thirds of the solid circumscribed about it (namely a triangular prism, as I shall show) touching the inscribed portion at points B, A, and along line CD; and its opposite bases are triangles AQP, KBL. Let BA be bisected at E, and let CE, ED be joined. Since plane KLPQ touches the cylindrical portion along line CD, and QK is the common section of planes KLPQ, BKQA, KQ will touch section BCA at C; but because we cut two parallel planes of the parallelepiped, namely KLPQ and the opposite one in which line BA lies, by plane AK, the common section QK touching section BCA at C will be parallel to the common section or second diameter BA; and therefore joined EC will be the conjugate semidiameter of BA. Similarly I shall show LP, touching section BDA at D, to be parallel to the same BA, and hence ED

to be the conjugate semidiameter of the same BA. Next, because KB, QA are the common sections of the cutting plane KA and the two parallel triangles KBL, QAP (for they are parts of two parallel planes of the parallelepiped) and the triangles themselves touch the cylinder portion at points B, A; the two lines BK, AQ will not only be parallel to each other, but also tangent to section BCA at points B, A; whence it follows that the said BK, AQ are also parallel to the same semidiameter CE. And equally using the same reasoning I shall show that lines BL, AP touch section BDA at those same two points B, A, and are parallel to each other and to the same semidiameter ED, wherefore spaces KA, BP will be parallelograms, and therefore QK, PL will be parallel and equal to each other, inasmuch as they are equal to the one straight line BA, from which it follows that space KP is a parallelogram; thus the circumscribed solid QBP will be a prism whose opposite bases are triangles KBL, QAP. Moreover let us extend through line KL and point E a plane cutting off the prism and the cylinder portion inscribed in it, and let the section of the prism be triangle KEL; then finally through any taken point F on line BA let us draw another plane parallel to CDE cutting pyramid KBLE and again both said solids, and let the sections of the drawn planes be GNF, IMF, HFO; and since the same plane KA cuts the mutually parallel planes of triangles QPA, CDE, IMF, KLB, all the common sections QA, CE, IF, KB will be mutually parallel, and likewise all AP, ED, FM, BL, and equally QP, CD, IM, KL; wherefore, as BE is to EF, so KE is to EG, and LE to EN. Since these last two ratios are similar, it is necessary that line GN be parallel to KL. Moreover, because (margin: Conics I.21) the square on CE is to the square on HF as rectangle AEB is to rectangle AFB; and in the same ratio is also the square on ED to the square on FO; line CE will be to HF as ED is to FO; and angle HFO is equal to angle CED (because CE is parallel to HF and ED to FO), therefore triangles CED, HFO will be similar, and hence side HO will also be parallel to CD, which is a side of the cylinder, from which it follows that the same HO is a straight line, inasmuch as it is a side of the cylinder; therefore since HO is parallel to line CD, it will likewise be parallel to lines IM, KL, and GN; and in this way triangles CED, IFM, KBL, GNF, and HOF are similar. These things having been shown, since square CE,

that is IF itself, is to square FH as rectangle AEB is to rectangle AFB (margin: ibid.), by conversion of ratio rectangle AEB, that is square EB, will be to square EF as square IF is to its own excess over square HF; but as square BE is to square EF, so square KB, or IF, is to square GF; wherefore as square IF is to the said excess, so the same square IF is to GF itself; and therefore square GF will be equal to the excess of square IF over square HF; that is, square HF together with square GF will be equal to square IF; indeed triangle IFM of prism CLE will be equal to triangle GFN of pyramid KBLE together with triangle HFO of the semicylindrical portion; and equally triangle CED of the same prism will be equal to the triangle of the said semiportion CEDB, namely to itself; and since triangle KBL of the prism is also equal to the triangle of the pyramid, that is to itself, all the triangles of half-prism CLE will be equal to all the triangles of the said semiportion of the cylinder together with all the triangles of pyramid KBLE: that is, half-prism CLE will be equal to semiportion CEDB of the cylinder together with pyramid KBLE, and so the whole prism will be equal to the whole portion CADB together with twice the said pyramid; but twice the same pyramid is a third part of the whole circumscribed prism, therefore the two remaining third parts of the same prism will be equal to the set-out portion of the cylinder, and therefore this same portion will be two-thirds of the aforesaid circumscribed prism QBP, Q.E.D.

Corollary I.

By force of this demonstration it is evident that, if that described portion of the cylinder be cut by any plane HFO parallel to CED, each segment of it, such as HFOB, is equal to its circumscribed prism IFL, minus the frustum of the pyramid lying hidden in the same prism: and equally the other remaining segment is equal to its circumscribed prism QAM, with pyramids KBLE, GNFE removed.

Corollary II.

Likewise it is evident that the triangle composed of the conjugate semidiameters of the said two semi-ellipses or semicircles, together with that part of the side of the cylinder intercepted between the same semidiameters, that is triangle CED, is equal to the base of the circumscribed prism.
