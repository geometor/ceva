About This Edition¶

geometor-ceva establishes the authoritative Digital Scriptorium critical edition and exact symbolic engine for Giovanni Ceva’s (1647–1734) landmark quarto treatise, De lineis rectis se invicem secantibus statica constructio (Milan, 1678). Dedicated to Ferdinando Carlo Gonzaga, Duke of Mantua, this revolutionary work introduced the static barycentric method to mathematics—resolving the concurrency and collinearity properties of intersecting straight lines by substituting fictitious gravitational weights in place of lines and invoking Archimedes’ Law of the Lever ($u \cdot \overline{AF} = v \cdot \overline{FB}$), while introducing algebraic negative weights ($F - A$) nearly a century and a half before August Ferdinand Möbius.

The edition reconstructs the complete 1678 treatise across four synchronized units: Liber Primus (establishing Menelaus’s transversal theorem, Ceva’s concurrency theorem $\frac{AF}{FB}\cdot\frac{BD}{DC}\cdot\frac{CE}{EA} = 1$, Caravaggio’s Euclidean parallel-line proof, 3D skew quadrilaterals, and 7-line tetrahedral concurrency), Liber Secundus (conic contact-ratio invariance, the contact cevian concurrency anticipating Gergonne $X(7)$ and its isotomic conjugate anticipating Nagel $X(8)$, and generalization to arbitrary odd $(2m + 1)$-gons), and the Appendix Geometrica (pure geometric area-ratio foundations, cubature of the cylindrical ungula, hemisphere and paraboloid centroids, and Ceva’s famous foundational critique of Cavalieri’s method of indivisibles).

Within the GEOMETOR canon, geometor-ceva forms the primary bridge uniting classical Archimedean statics (geometor/archimedes) with classical Greek transversal lemmas (geometor/menelaus, geometor/pappus), homogeneous barycentric coordinates in geometor/etc, and modern radical-free Rational Trigonometry (geometor/wildberger), establishing the computational foundation for cevian concurrency and harmonic division across all geometries.

Typographic Conventions of the 1678 Milan Imprint¶

In his prefatory Typographus Lectori note, Milanese printer Ludovico Monza informs the reader that his shop lacked sufficient cast sorts of the Greek cross + and therefore employed the typographic dagger/obelus † to denote algebraic addition of weights (e.g., A † F for $A + F$ concentrated at an interior fulcrum), paired with the analytic minus sign - (F - A for $F - A$, Liber Primus p. 5 Scholium) when balancing an exterior fulcrum. Our Latin track preserves Ceva’s authentic notation alongside clear editorial explanation.
